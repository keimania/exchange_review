### 2.2 Novation Process

Upon acceptance of a trade for clearing, each ICE clearing house interposes itself as the central counterparty through novation. The original bilateral contract between buyer and seller is extinguished and replaced by two new contracts: one between the CCP and the buyer, and one between the CCP and the seller. Each clearing member's counterparty exposure is thereby solely to the relevant ICE clearing house rather than to any bilateral counterparty.

For CDS clearing at ICE Clear Credit, the novation process involves the submission of eligible CDS contracts by clearing participants; once accepted, ICE Clear Credit becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer. [Source: [ICE Clear Credit -- CDS Client Clearing](https://www.ice.com/clear-credit/cds-client-clearing)]

### 2.3 Netting

Each ICE clearing house provides multilateral netting across all positions within a clearing member's account. Variation margin is calculated and netted across all positions, resulting in a single net pay or collect amount per settlement cycle. Initial margin benefits from portfolio-based margining under the ICE Risk Model (IRM) and IRM 2, which recognise offsets and diversification within a portfolio of correlated positions.

ICE Clear Europe provides separate netting pools by product segment (Energy, Financials & Softs). [Source: [ICE Clear Europe Risk Management](https://www.ice.com/clear-europe/risk-management)]

## 3. Risk Management

### 3.1 Margin Framework

ICE uses two proprietary margin models across its clearing houses:

**ICE Risk Model (IRM 1) -- Legacy Model:**

The original ICE Risk Model calculates initial margin on at least a daily basis for futures and options. It uses a scenario-based approach similar to SPAN, evaluating potential portfolio losses across a range of price and volatility movements. Margin parameters (scanning ranges) are calibrated to cover potential losses under normal and stressed market conditions.

**ICE Risk Model 2 (IRM 2) -- Next-Generation VaR Model:**

IRM 2 is a Filtered Historical Simulation (FHS) Value-at-Risk (VaR) methodology that represents a significant evolution from IRM 1.

| Feature | IRM 1 (Legacy) | IRM 2 |
|---------|----------------|-------|
| Methodology | Scenario-based (parametric, similar to SPAN) | Filtered Historical Simulation VaR |
| Risk Perspective | Instrument-by-instrument | Portfolio-level -- captures all relationships and diversifying effects |
| Responsiveness | Static parameter updates | Dynamic -- responsive to changing market conditions through volatility filtering |
| Anti-Procyclicality | Limited | Built-in anti-procyclical features to avoid "big step" margin changes |
| Stress Resilience | Scenario add-ons | Resilient against stress events and correlation breakdown |
| Seasonality | Not explicitly modelled | Adjusts for seasonality where appropriate |

**IRM 2 Calculation Steps:**

1. **Risk Factor Transformation** -- Market data objects are transformed from original instruments to a coherent set of risk factors (price, rate, and volatility factors required to price all traded futures and options)
2. **Scenario Generation** -- Risk factors are scaled to generate risk scenarios reflecting current market conditions via filtered historical simulation
3. **Portfolio Repricing** -- Relevant pricing functions convert risk factor scenarios back to instrument-level P&L
4. **Base Margin** -- Portfolio VaR is calculated from the distribution of simulated P&L outcomes

Each ICE clearing entity maintains a separate default waterfall. The general structure across all three entities follows a similar pattern but with entity-specific variations.

**ICE Clear Europe Default Waterfall:**

ICE Clear Europe operates two separate Guaranty Fund segments for Futures & Options: an **Energy Segment** and a **Financials & Softs Segment**. Each segment is calibrated on a Cover-2 basis (sufficient to cover the simultaneous default of the two clearing member groups with the largest aggregate exposure under extreme but plausible scenarios).

| Layer | Resource | Approximate Size |
|-------|----------|-----------------|
| 1 | Defaulting member's initial margin | Varies |
| 2 | Defaulting member's Guaranty Fund contribution | Full amount consumed before other resources |
| 3 | ICE's F&O Initial Contribution ("Skin in the Game") | Consumed before non-defaulting members' contributions |
| 4 | Default Insurance | $100 million (renewed September 2025, three-year term) |
| 5 | Non-defaulting members' Guaranty Fund contributions (relevant segment first) | Total minimum Guaranty Fund requirements ~$2.9 billion (as of January 31, 2025) |
| 6 | Cross-segment Guaranty Fund (if loss exceeds relevant segment) | Contributions from the other F&O segment |
| 7 | Assessment powers on non-defaulting members | Limited to a set multiple of prior Guaranty Fund contributions |

**Segment Segregation Rule:** Where a loss relates to Financials & Softs contracts, the clearing member's Financials & Softs Segment contributions are exhausted before the Energy Segment is accessed, and vice versa. [Source: [ICE Clear Europe Risk Management](https://www.ice.com/clear-europe/risk-management); [ICE Clear Europe Risk FAQ](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/clear_europe/ICE_Clear_Europe_Risk_FAQ.pdf)]

### 3.5 Stress Testing & Back-testing

**Stress Testing:**

All ICE clearing houses conduct daily stress testing to assess the adequacy of their financial resources:

- **ICE Clear Europe:** Stress tests are designed to determine whether the Guaranty Fund is sufficient to cover the simultaneous default of the two clearing member groups with the largest aggregate credit exposure under extreme but plausible market conditions. Stress scenarios include historical scenarios (e.g., 2008 financial crisis, 2020 COVID shock) and hypothetical scenarios.
- **ICE Clear U.S.:** Conducts daily stress testing of margin and Guaranty Fund adequacy. The ICUS Guaranty Fund is designed to provide adequate funds based on Cover-2 under extreme but plausible market conditions.
- **ICE Clear Credit:** Conducts stress testing under extreme but plausible scenarios for the CDS portfolio and the Treasury portfolio (separately).

**Back-testing:**

ICE conducts daily back-testing to validate that its margin models adequately cover actual portfolio losses at the required confidence level. Back-testing compares predicted margin coverage against actual observed P&L. Results are reviewed by risk committees and model calibration is adjusted as needed.

[Source: [ICE Clear Europe PFMI Disclosure Framework](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/clear_europe/ICE_Clear_Europe_Disclosure_Framework.pdf); [ICE Clear U.S. Disclosure Framework](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/clear_us/ICUS_DisclosureFramework.pdf)]

### 3.6 Recovery & Resolution

**ICE Clear Europe Recovery Plan:**

ICE Clear Europe maintains a comprehensive recovery plan as required by the Bank of England and filed with the SEC (for CDS clearing). The recovery plan describes tools (Recovery Options) that ICE Clear Europe could use to recover from losses:

| Recovery Tool | Description |
|--------------|-------------|
| **Powers of Assessment** | ICE Clear Europe can immediately collect additional resources from non-defaulting clearing members intraday and in cash. Assessments are limited to a set multiple of the non-defaulting members' Guaranty Fund contributions |
| **Reduced Gains Distribution (RGD)** | Variation margin gains haircut -- ICE Clear Europe can reduce the payout of variation margin gains to non-defaulting members to cover residual losses |
| **Investment Loss Allocation** | ICE Clear Europe contributes a fixed amount of its own capital to cover losses from issuer/counterparty default on investments of clearing members' Guaranty Fund contributions or initial margin |
| **Non-Default Loss Allocation** | Similar framework for losses unrelated to member defaults |

The Recovery Plan considers factors including speed of implementation, impact on the clearing house and clearing members, and effects on other market infrastructure.

**Resolution Authority:**

- **ICE Clear Europe:** The Bank of England acts as the resolution authority under the UK Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 and the Banking Act 2009.
- **ICE Clear Credit:** The FDIC acts as the resolution authority under Title II of the Dodd-Frank Act, with the CFTC and SEC as primary supervisors.
- **ICE Clear U.S.:** The CFTC has primary regulatory oversight.

A **Crisis Management Group** (CMG) arrangement exists for ICE Clear Europe, involving the Bank of England, CFTC, SEC, and other relevant authorities to coordinate supervisory and resolution activities. [Source: [ICE Clear Europe CMG Framework](https://www.cftc.gov/media/2456/cftc-lch-arrangementmou112918/download); [Federal Register -- ICE Clear Europe Recovery Plan Amendments, September 2023](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/09/14/2023-19844/self-regulatory-organizations-ice-clear-europe-limited-order-approving-proposed-rule-change-relating)]

### 4.2 DVP Model

For physically settled transactions, ICE clearing houses use delivery-versus-payment (DVP) mechanisms to ensure simultaneous exchange of securities/commodities and cash, eliminating principal risk. Cash settlement flows are processed through approved settlement banks.

ICE Clear Europe processes cash-settled futures and options through daily pay/collect of variation margin. For physically delivered contracts (e.g., Brent Crude Oil, London Cocoa), delivery procedures are specified per contract and coordinated through approved delivery facilities and settlement banks. [Source: [ICE Clear Europe Operations](https://www.ice.com/clear-europe/operations)]

### 4.3 Settlement Finality

**ICE Clear Europe** has received settlement finality designation under the **Financial Markets and Insolvency (Settlement Finality) Regulations 1999 (SFR)** of the United Kingdom. Under SFR, payment instructions within ICE Clear Europe's system are protected from administrators or liquidators of insolvent firms, enhancing systemic risk protection for clearing members. [Source: [ICE Clear Europe Regulation](https://www.ice.com/clear-europe/regulation)]

**ICE Clear U.S. and ICE Clear Credit** achieve settlement finality under U.S. law, including protections under the Commodity Exchange Act and relevant provisions of the Dodd-Frank Act. [Source: [ICE Clear U.S. Disclosure Framework](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/clear_us/ICUS_DisclosureFramework.pdf)]

ICE Clear Credit has access to the Federal Reserve System, providing access to central bank liquidity. ## 7. Interoperability

ICE's clearing houses do not currently maintain formal CCP interoperability arrangements (i.e., links where two CCPs clear the same instruments allowing counterparties to clear at different CCPs).

However, ICE has explored and implemented cross-margining concepts:

- **NYPC / DTCC Cross-Margining (Historical):** ICE previously operated New York Portfolio Clearing (NYPC), which linked with DTCC to provide cross-margining between interest rate futures and U.S. Treasury cash positions. This was subsequently consolidated into ICE Clear Europe operations. [Source: [Finadium -- NYPC consolidated into ICE Clear Europe](https://finadium.com/new-york-portfolio-clearing-consolidated-ice-europe-clear-europe-cost-savings/)]

- **Intra-ICE Offsets:** While separate legal entities, ICE's clearing houses benefit from the common ICE parent for operational efficiencies, though each maintains its own separate default waterfall and financial resources. Cross-margining across ICE entities is not formally provided. [unverified]

## 9. Recent Developments

### ICE Clear Credit -- U.S. Treasury Clearing (2025-2026)

The most significant recent development across ICE's clearing houses is ICE Clear Credit's entry into U.S. Treasury clearing:

- **August 2025:** ICE Clear Credit filed application with the SEC to register as a clearing agency for U.S. Treasury securities
- **November 2025:** SEC instituted proceedings to evaluate the application
- **February 2026:** SEC granted approval; ICE Clear Credit's Treasury Clearing Service became operationally live -- the first alternative venue to DTCC/FICC for clearing U.S. Treasury securities

The Treasury clearing service is established as a distinct offering from the CDS clearing service with a separate rulebook, membership, risk management framework, financial and liquidity resources, and risk committee. The SEC extended the original U.S. Treasury clearing mandate deadlines by 12 months (cash transactions to December 31, 2026; repo transactions to June 30, 2027). [Source: [ICE Press Release, February 2026](https://ir.theice.com/press/news-details/2026/ICE-Clear-Credits-Treasury-Clearing-Service-Receives-SEC-Approval-and-is-Now-Operationally-Live/default.aspx); [Federal Register -- ICE Clear Credit Order, February 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/06/2026-02333/ice-clear-credit-llc-order-granting-an-application-for-registration-as-a-clearing-agency-under)]

### IRM 2 Rollout Expansion (2025)

ICE continued the phased rollout of its next-generation margin model:
- Freight products at ICE Clear Europe transitioned to IRM 2 in September 2025
- Additional energy products at ICE Clear Europe transitioned in November 2025
- Remaining product groups (interest rates, equity derivatives, agricultural) remain on IRM 1 pending future phase dates

[Source: [BusinessWire -- ICE Launches Latest Phase of IRM 2, November 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251117596802/en/ICE-Launches-Latest-Phase-of-its-VaR-Based-Portfolio-Margining-Methodology-ICE-Risk-Model-2)]

### Default Insurance Renewal (2025)

ICE renewed its default insurance programme for a three-year term commencing September 17, 2025:
- ICE Clear Europe: $100 million
- ICE Clear Credit: $75 million
- ICE Clear U.S.: $25 million

[Source: [ICE Quarterly Clearing Disclosures Q3 2025](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/Understanding_ICE_Clearing_Disclosures_Q3_2025.pdf)]

### ICE Clear Credit -- Client Volume Incentive Program (2025)

In April 2025, ICE Clear Credit introduced a Client Volume Incentive Program providing tiered discounts on client clearing fees based on annual billing volumes. [Source: [Federal Register, April 2025](https://regulations.justia.com/regulations/fedreg/2025/04/16/2025-06412.html)]
