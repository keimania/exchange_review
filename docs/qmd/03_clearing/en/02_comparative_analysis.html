1.3 Key Observations

  1. Eurex Clearing has the largest aggregate CCP/parent financial commitment at EUR 1.7 billion (including the Deutsche Borse Letter of Comfort), providing the most comprehensive loss-absorbing capacity beyond participant resources.

  2. ASX has the most unique SITG model: ASX Clear’s cash equities default fund is 100% funded by the CCP itself (AUD 250M), and ASX Clear (Futures) contributes 69% of the total default fund (AUD 450M of AUD 650M). This reflects the vertically integrated ownership model.

  3. ICE introduced innovation with its default insurance layer (renewed for three-year terms), which adds $200M across its clearing houses between SITG and non-defaulting members’ contributions. Aggregate ICE SITG + insurance exceeds $660M.

  4. CME places distinct SITG amounts in its Base ($100M) and IRS ($150M) waterfalls, totaling $250M in pre-funded own resources.

  5. U.S. user-owned CCPs (NSCC, OCC) have relatively smaller SITG amounts (estimated $25-76M), reflecting their cooperative ownership models where participants bear the primary mutualized risk.

  6. HKEX uses a fixed-percentage model (10% of fund size) for its SITG across all three clearing houses, providing simplicity and proportionality.

  7. Cover 2 is the predominant standard for systemically important CCPs (LCH, OCC, CME, Eurex, JSCC, HKEX), while Cover 1 is used by some smaller or less systemic CCPs.

2.2 Methodology Deep-Dive

SPAN (CME Legacy / HKCC PRiME / ASX Clear (Futures)) - Evaluates 16 hypothetical risk scenarios combining price and volatility shifts - Calculates worst-case loss within each combined commodity group - Adds intra-commodity spread charges and subtracts inter-commodity spread credits - Transparent and widely adopted (50+ exchanges globally), but limited by its parametric nature and inability to capture tail risk dynamically - Being replaced by SPAN 2 at CME; JSCC migrated away from SPAN to VaR in November 2023

PRISMA / FHS (Eurex) - 750 filtered historical scenarios + 250 stress period scenarios = 1,000 total - Volatility filtering ensures model reactivity to changing conditions - Forward-looking stress period VaR floor prevents margin erosion during calm periods - Cross-margining between listed fixed income (2-day HP) and OTC IRS (5-day HP) within PFI01 liquidation group – unique feature delivering up to 70% margin savings - Annual margin floor recalibration (e.g., Prisma Release 15.0 effective June 2025)

PAIRS (LCH) - Expected Shortfall (ES) at 99.7% confidence – highest confidence level among major CCPs - 10-year lookback with volatility scaling (EWMA-based) to reflect current conditions - Floor calibrated using unscaled 10-year lookback plus 2.5-year GFC window - Member MPOR of 5 days for SwapClear, 7 days for client clearing (additional 2 days for portfolio transfer) - Proven in managing the Lehman Brothers default (2008): used only 35% of Lehman’s IM for a $9 trillion portfolio

STANS (OCC) - World’s first large-scale Monte Carlo-based margin methodology for derivatives clearing (2006) - 99% Expected Shortfall with enhanced 99.5% ES for the two largest sub-portfolio exposures - 2-day holding period (longer than CME’s 1-day for comparable products) - Full portfolio margining capturing non-linearities, correlations, and tail risk - Being enhanced to address 0DTE option risks (Intraday Risk Charge approved April 2025)

IRM 2 (ICE) - Next-generation Filtered Historical Simulation VaR replacing scenario-based IRM 1 - Portfolio-level risk assessment capturing all relationships and diversifying effects - Built-in anti-procyclical features and seasonality adjustment - Phased rollout: live for ICE Clear U.S. equity index futures (Jan 2022), ICE Clear Europe freight and energy (Sep-Nov 2025), remaining products TBD

VaR-based approaches (JSCC, HKSCC, CDP, Cboe Clear Europe) - JSCC migrated from SPAN to VaR (November 2023) using dual HS-VaR and AS-VaR methods, with 1,250+ data points – a major modernization aligning with global trends - Cboe Clear Europe uses CVaR with 700 historical scenarios and real-time recalculation every 5 minutes - HKSCC uses VaR for large/mid-cap securities (Tier P) but flat-rate margins for smaller securities (Tier N)

2.3 Key Observations

  1. Global convergence toward VaR/ES models: SPAN is being phased out in favor of historical simulation VaR and Expected Shortfall approaches. CME (SPAN 2), JSCC (VaR), and ICE (IRM 2) have all migrated or are migrating away from parametric scenario-based methods.

  2. LCH operates at the highest confidence level (99.7% ES with PAIRS), reflecting the systemic importance and complexity of OTC IRS clearing.

  3. Eurex Prisma offers the most advanced cross-margining, enabling margin offsets between listed and OTC products within the same liquidation group – a capability most other CCPs lack.

  4. Holding periods vary significantly: 1 day (CME ETD, ICE ETD), 2 days (OCC, Eurex listed, Cboe Clear Europe), 3 days (NSCC), 5 days (OTC products at LCH, CME, Eurex, JSCC, ASX).

  5. Anti-procyclicality measures are increasingly mandated: Eurex uses stress period VaR floors; Cboe Clear Europe uses Stressed Historical Simulation; CME SPAN 2 incorporates built-in anti-procyclical features. EMIR explicitly requires CCPs to implement anti-procyclicality tools.

3.3 T+0 / Instant Settlement

  • India: Pioneer in shortened settlement. NSE’s NSCCL achieved T+1 for equities in January 2023, making India the first major market. SEBI is now piloting T+0 and instant settlement for select securities.
  • China: CSDC effectively operates T+1 for A-shares (settlement next day), with some bond products settling T+0. China’s pre-funding model ensures settlement certainty.
  • DLT Initiatives: DTCC’s Project Ion processes 100,000+ equity transactions daily on R3 Corda in parallel production; CME is piloting tokenization with Google Cloud Universal Ledger (GCUL); Eurex Clearing launched DLT-enabled collateral mobilization with HQLAx (July 2025).

10.3 KRX/KSD Positioning

Korea’s post-trade infrastructure, centered on KRX (as CCP) and KSD (as CSD), can be benchmarked against global peers across key dimensions:

Structural Model: - KRX operates as an integrated exchange-CCP entity (similar to pre-reform structures), while most Tier 1 peers separate exchange and CCP functions (e.g., JPX/JSCC, HKEX/HKSCC, CME Group/CME Clearing) - KSD operates as a separate CSD, comparable to JASDEC (Japan), Clearstream (Germany), or HKSCC’s depository function - The KRX-KSD split mirrors the CDP-SGX-DC model in Singapore and the JSCC-JASDEC model in Japan

Margin Methodology: - KRX uses KRX-SPAN (modified SPAN) for listed derivatives – placing it alongside HKCC (PRiME/SPAN) and ASX Clear (Futures) (SPAN) in the SPAN-based group - The global trend is moving away from SPAN toward VaR/ES-based methods (CME SPAN 2, JSCC VaR, Eurex Prisma, ICE IRM 2). KRX may consider a similar migration to align with global best practices - For OTC clearing, KRX uses a VaR-based method, consistent with the global standard

Settlement Cycle: - KRX operates at T+2 for equities, in line with Japan (T+2), Hong Kong (T+2), Singapore (T+2), Australia (T+2), and the EU (T+2) - The U.S. and Canada moved to T+1 in May 2024; India achieved T+1 in January 2023; China operates at T+1 - Korea has successfully instituted KRW 1B split settlement (April 2026) to manage settlement liquidity and is actively developing its T+1 migration roadmap to keep pace with global peers - KRX settles government bonds at T+1, comparable to Japan (T+1 for JGBs since May 2018)

Default Waterfall: - KRX’s Joint Compensation Fund for derivatives is estimated at ~KRW 100 billion (~$75M), comparable in scale to CDP’s S$80M but significantly smaller than Tier 1 CCPs - KRX places its own SITG contribution in the waterfall before non-defaulting members’ resources, consistent with global best practice - The structure follows the “defaulter pays first” principle, standard across all major CCPs

OTC Clearing Scope: - KRX’s OTC clearing scope (KRW IRS, KRW CRS) is narrower than JSCC (JPY IRS + CDS + JGB), LCH (multi-currency IRS + FX + CDS), or CME (multi-currency IRS + FX) - Expanding OTC clearing mandates and product coverage would align with global trends and G20 commitments

Technology: - KRX uses EXTURE+ (KOSCOM-developed, 2014), an integrated exchange-clearing platform with ~30-50 microsecond latency and 100,000+ TPS. KSD operates dedicated settlement systems with ISO 20022 adoption and SWIFT connectivity. KRX has begun exporting its clearing/settlement technology (Thai Stock Exchange contract, April 2025). KSD’s security token mandatory registration framework (effective January 2027) positions Korea as an early mover in DLT-based securities infrastructure. - Peers are investing heavily in cloud migration (DTCC-AWS, OCC-AWS, CME-Google Cloud, LCH-AWS/Azure, Eurex-Google Cloud) and DLT-based initiatives - The CHESS replacement failure at ASX (AUD 250M write-off) provides a cautionary example for technology modernization projects

Regulatory Framework: - Korea’s FSC/FSS oversight is comparable to Japan’s JFSA/BOJ and Hong Kong’s SFC/HKMA dual-regulator models - Korea does not have a dedicated CCP recovery and resolution regime, similar to Japan, Hong Kong, Singapore, and Australia – unlike the EU (CCP RRR fully applicable since February 2023) and the U.S. (Dodd-Frank Title VIII/Title II) - Developing a CCP R&R framework would align with FSB Key Attributes and global best practices

Key Recommendations for KRX/KSD: 1. Margin methodology upgrade: Consider migrating from KRX-SPAN to a VaR/ES-based methodology (following JSCC’s 2023 precedent) 2. T+1 readiness: Begin industry consultation and system readiness assessment for potential T+1 migration, monitoring UK/EU timelines (2027 target) 3. OTC clearing expansion: Evaluate expanding OTC clearing mandates to additional currency pairs and product types 4. CCP R&R framework: Advocate for dedicated CCP recovery and resolution legislation 5. Technology modernization: Assess cloud migration opportunities and DLT/tokenization pilots for collateral management 6. Cross-border connectivity: Leverage existing KSD links with Euroclear/Clearstream/DTC; explore deeper integration with regional CCPs (JSCC, HKSCC) for cross-border clearing arrangements
