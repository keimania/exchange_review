1. Overview & Profile

Item Details

Abbreviation CDP

SWIFT Code CDPLSGSGXXX
Country / City Singapore
Address SGX Centre 1, 2 Shenton Way, #02-02, Singapore 068804
Website https://www.sgx.com/securities/depository
Year Established 1987
Parent Company Singapore Exchange Limited (SGX Group) – wholly-owned subsidiary
Regulatory Authority Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)
Regulatory Status Approved Clearing House (ACH) under Securities and Futures Act 2001 (SFA); designated Central Depository under the Companies Act
CPMI-IOSCO PFMI Compliance Yes – publishes PFMI disclosure (SGX RegCo PFMI Page)
Role Provides integrated clearing, settlement, and depository services for the Singapore securities market (equities and fixed income)
Systemic Importance Designated as systemically important FMI by MAS; value of transactions cleared as percentage of Singapore GDP was ~94% (as of 2012 IMF assessment) [Source: IMF Country Report No. 13/345]

Relationship to SGX Group:

CDP is one of two clearing houses within SGX Group: - CDP – clears and settles equities, corporate bonds, ETFs, REITs, unit trusts, warrants, depository receipts, and other listed securities traded on SGX-ST (Singapore Exchange Securities Trading) - SGX-DC (SGX Derivatives Clearing Limited) – clears derivatives products (futures, options, OTC derivatives); separate entity with its own clearing fund (~S$557 million as of Dec 2024)

Both CDP and SGX-DC are wholly-owned subsidiaries of SGX and are regulated as Designated Clearing Houses (DCHs) under the SFA. [Source: MAS – Clearing Houses]

KRX/KSD Comparison: In Korea, KRX serves as the CCP while KSD serves as the CSD, and they are separate entities. In Singapore, CDP uniquely combines both CCP and CSD functions within a single legal entity.

Key Historical Milestones

Year Event
1987 CDP established as a wholly-owned subsidiary of the Stock Exchange of Singapore (SES) to serve as the central securities depository for the Singapore market
1999 SES and SIMEX merged to form Singapore Exchange (SGX); CDP became a subsidiary of SGX
2006 CDP implemented real-time surveillance capabilities for settlement risk
2012 SGX embarked on post-trade modernisation programme to upgrade clearing, settlement, and depository systems

2. Clearing Model

2.1 CCP Services

CDP acts as the central counterparty for all securities traded on SGX-ST, providing clearing services for the following:

Product Class Description
Equities Ordinary shares, preference shares listed on SGX-ST
Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) All ETFs listed on SGX-ST
REITs & Business Trusts Real Estate Investment Trusts and business trusts
Fixed Income Listed corporate bonds, government bonds
Warrants & Structured Products Structured warrants, daily leverage certificates
Depository Receipts Singapore Depository Receipts
Unit Trusts Exchange-traded unit trusts
Extended Settlement (ES) Contracts Marginable futures-like contracts on single stocks
Rights Rights issues during corporate actions

[Source: SGX Clearing Information]

2.2 Novation Process

CDP becomes the counterparty to each side of a transaction through novation as soon as the trade is executed or reported on SGX-ST. The novation process differs based on membership type:

  1. Both parties are Clearing Members (CMs): The trade is directly novated to CDP, with CDP becoming the buyer to the selling CM and the seller to the buying CM.

  2. One party is a Trading Member (TM), the other a CM: The qualifying CM assumes the rights, duties, and obligations of the counterparty TM, and the transaction is then novated to CDP.

  3. Both parties are Trading-only Members: The respective qualifying CMs assume the rights and obligations of each TM, and the transaction is then novated to CDP.

[Source: SGX Rulebook – Rule 6.2 Clearing and Novation]

KRX Comparison: KRX follows a similar novation model – upon trade execution on the exchange, KRX interposes itself as the CCP to both sides. The key difference is that KRX operates as a single integrated exchange-CCP entity, while CDP is a separate legal subsidiary within the SGX Group.

2.3 Netting

CDP operates a multilateral netting system:

  • Securities Netting: Net securities obligations are calculated per Clearing Member per security. On T+2, each CM has a single net delivery or receive obligation for each security.
  • Cash Netting: Net cash obligations are calculated per Clearing Member. A single net pay or receive amount is determined for each settlement day.
  • Netting Cut-off: Netting occurs at end of trade day for trades executed that day, with settlement obligations due on T+2.

2.4 Clearing Membership

CDP has two categories of clearing membership:

Type Minimum Capital Key Requirements
General Clearing Member (GCM) S$5 million base capital (or S$8 million if also a clearing member of SGX-DC) Must be Singapore-registered corporation; hold Capital Markets Services Licence (unless exempt); register a CEO; maintain reserve fund (30% of audited net profits transferred annually until base capital less unappropriated profits reaches S$15 million)
Bank Clearing Member (BCM) No specific minimum (subject to banking regulations) Must be authorised to conduct banking business in Singapore; must have adequate credit rating indicating intrinsic safety and soundness

[Source: SGX Rulebook – Rule 3 Membership; SGX Rulebook – Rule 5 Financial/Capital Requirements]

Additional requirements: - CDP may prescribe additional capital, financial, and other requirements based on volume, risk exposure, risk concentration, margin policies, and nature of business conducted - GCMs can clear for their own account and for Trading Members they have qualified (sponsored) - BCMs can clear for custodial clients

KRX Comparison: KRX clearing membership requires minimum net capital of KRW 10 billion (~S$10 million) for securities clearing members, broadly comparable to CDP’s S$5 million threshold.

3. Risk Management

3.1 Margin Framework

CDP employs a risk-based margining framework for its clearing members:

Parameter Details
Margin Methodology Risk-based margin calculated at a confidence level of at least 99% [Source: SGX CDP PFMI Disclosure]
Close-out Period 1 to 5 days depending on contract liquidity
Margin Calculation Frequency At least twice daily (intraday and end-of-day)
Margin Types Initial Margin, Variation Margin, Additional Margin (concentration, wrong-way risk)
Collateral Accepted Cash (SGD), government securities, and other approved collateral with appropriate haircuts [unverified – specific collateral schedule not confirmed from public sources]

Key features of the margin framework (post-May 2023 refinements): - Initial margin is designed to cover potential losses during the close-out period at a 99% confidence level - Margin calculations incorporate both current market risk and historical stress scenarios - CDP may call for additional margin during periods of heightened volatility

[Source: Allen & Gledhill – CDP Margin and Clearing Fund Refinements]

3.2 Margin Call Schedule

Cycle Timing Description
Intraday Margin During trading hours CDP monitors positions intraday and may issue margin calls to cover shortfalls in margin requirements and potential mark-to-market losses
End-of-Day Margin After market close Full margin recalculation; margin calls issued for any shortfall
Margin Call Deadline Within one Trading Day Clearing Members must meet margin calls within one Trading Day after the event giving rise to the margin call
Customer Margin Restoration Within two Market Days CMs must call from customers to restore to Initial Margin level within 2 Market Days of Customer Asset Value falling below Customer Required Margins

[Source: SGX Rulebook – Rule 6A Margin Requirements; SGX Rulebook – Regulatory Notice 3.3.12]

3.3 Default Waterfall (with approximate sizes)

CDP’s default waterfall specifies the order in which financial resources are applied following a clearing member default:

Layer Resource Approximate Size / Notes
1 Defaulter’s Margin Margins and collateral posted by the defaulting CM; first line of defence
2 Defaulter’s Clearing Fund Contribution The defaulting CM’s funded contribution to the Clearing Fund
3 CDP’s Skin-in-the-Game (SITG) CDP’s own capital contribution to the Clearing Fund – at least 25% of the total Clearing Fund [unverified – exact current CDP SITG amount not confirmed; the 25% ratio is derived from SGX Group-level statements]
4 Non-Defaulting CMs’ Funded Clearing Fund Contributions Funded contributions from other (non-defaulting) Clearing Members
5 Non-Defaulting CMs’ Contingent Contributions Unfunded (callable) contributions from non-defaulting CMs – utilised only after all funded resources are exhausted (per 2023 refinements)
6 CDP’s Remaining Capital Any remaining financial resources of CDP

Clearing Fund Size: - Total CDP Clearing Fund: S$80 million (as of 30 June 2024 and 31 December 2024) [Source: SGX Group Financial Results FY2024/H1 FY2025] - For comparison, SGX-DC Clearing Fund: S$557 million (as of same dates)

Key 2023 Refinements: - Contingent contributions from non-defaulting CMs were deprioritised – funded contributions (of both CMs and CDP) are now utilised ahead of unfunded resources - A cap was placed on clearing members’ total clearing fund liability to give certainty on maximum exposure - CDP’s own contribution was structured to be at least 25% of the total clearing fund, which SGX states is proportionately higher than many other CCPs in Europe and the US

[Source: Allen & Gledhill; SGX Group Financial Results H1 FY2025]

KRX Comparison: KRX’s Joint Compensation Fund for the securities market was approximately KRW 100 billion (~S$100 million) as of 2023, broadly comparable in scale to CDP’s S$80 million. KRX also places its own contribution (skin-in-the-game) in the waterfall ahead of non-defaulting members’ contributions, consistent with CPMI-IOSCO guidance. [unverified – KRX fund size approximate]

3.4 Default Fund / Guarantee Fund

The CDP Clearing Fund is sized and managed as follows:

Parameter Details
Total Fund Size S$80 million (as of Dec 2024)
CDP SITG Contribution At least 25% of the Clearing Fund
CM Funded Contribution Based on a percentage of average daily risk margin (post-2023 methodology)
CM Contingent Contribution Callable, capped, and used only after funded resources exhausted
Fund Sizing Methodology Revised in 2023 – changed approach for sizing the Clearing Fund to be more risk-sensitive
Fund Review Frequency Reviewed periodically; fund calls can be made if contributions are insufficient

[Source: SGX RegCo Consultation Paper on CDP Margining and Clearing Fund (Nov 2022)]

3.5 Stress Testing & Back-testing

CDP conducts regular stress testing and back-testing as part of its PFMI compliance:

  • Stress Testing: CDP performs stress tests of its financial resources to ensure adequacy under extreme but plausible market conditions. These include both historical stress scenarios and hypothetical scenarios. [unverified – specific stress scenario details not publicly disclosed]
  • Back-testing: Margin models are back-tested to validate that they achieve the targeted coverage level (99% confidence). Results are reviewed regularly and models are recalibrated as needed. [unverified – specific back-testing results not publicly disclosed]
  • MAS Oversight: MAS conducts supervisory assessments of CDP’s risk management practices, including its stress testing framework. [Source: MAS Parliamentary Reply on CDP Oversight (2024)]

3.6 Recovery & Resolution

  • Recovery Tools: CDP has recovery plans that include tools such as variation margin haircutting, partial tear-up, and voluntary auction mechanisms to manage residual losses beyond the default waterfall. [unverified – specific recovery plan details not publicly available]
  • Resolution Authority: MAS is the resolution authority for CDP as a systemically important FMI in Singapore.
  • Default Management Enhancements (2019): SGX implemented enhancements to the default management capabilities of both SGX-DC and CDP, including amendments to the default management process for clearing members. [Source: Allen & Gledhill (2019)]
  • 2024 Refinements: SGX revised the cap on clearing members’ liability for multiple defaults, providing greater certainty to CMs on their maximum financial exposure. [Source: Mondovisione (2024)]

4. Settlement

4.1 Settlement Cycle

Product Settlement Cycle Notes
Equities (shares, ETFs, REITs) T+2 Shortened from T+3 on 10 December 2018
Listed Corporate Bonds T+2 Aligned with equities
Singapore Government Securities (SGS) T+1 Settled via MAS’s MEPS+ system (not CDP)
Unlisted Corporate Bonds Negotiable Fully negotiable settlement dates
Unit Trusts Various Depends on fund manager’s terms

[Source: Clearstream – Settlement Process Singapore; DBS Vickers – New Securities Settlement Framework]

KRX Comparison: KRX also settles equities on T+2 (moved from T+3 in 2024). Both markets are now aligned with the global standard. The US moved to T+1 in May 2024; Singapore has not yet announced plans for T+1 migration. [unverified – Singapore T+1 status]

4.2 DVP Model

CDP operates a true DVP (Delivery versus Payment) model since the December 2018 system upgrade:

Feature Details
DVP Type BIS Model 3 – gross securities settlement with net cash settlement [unverified – exact BIS model classification]
Securities Settlement Book-entry transfer within CDP; settlement only finalised once intraday batch or RTGS is completed
Cash Settlement Via settlement banks through MAS’s MEPS+ RTGS system for SGD; multi-currency settlement supported
Settlement Windows Settlement from 09:00 on settlement date; multiple intraday batch settlement cycles
Simultaneous Settlement Securities and cash settle simultaneously – securities delivery occurs only upon confirmed cash payment

[Source: DBS Vickers – New Securities Settlement Framework]

4.3 Settlement Finality

Settlement finality is clearly defined under CDP rules:

  • Securities Finality: Delivery of securities is effected and final upon CDP debiting the securities from the Delivering Securities Account. Receipt is final upon CDP crediting the securities into the Receiving Securities Account.
  • Cash Finality: Settlement of a Settlement Participant’s payment obligation is effected and final upon CDP debiting an equivalent sum of money from the Settlement Participant’s Cash Ledger Account.
  • Legal Basis: Settlement finality is protected under the Securities and Futures Act 2001 (SFA), which provides statutory protection for the irrevocability and finality of settlements effected through approved clearing houses.

[Source: SGX Rulebook – CDP Settlement Rules]

4.4 CSD / Settlement Infrastructure

CDP operates as Singapore’s sole CSD for exchange-traded securities:

Function Details
Securities Held All SGX-listed securities including equities, corporate bonds, ETFs, REITs, warrants, structured products, unit trusts, depository receipts
Account Types Direct Securities Account (for individual retail investors linked directly to CDP); Sub-Account (held under a Depository Agent such as a broker or custodian bank)
Immobilisation SGX-listed securities are immobilised – all securities are held in book-entry form at CDP
Corporate Actions CDP processes dividends, rights issues, bonus issues, share splits, and other corporate actions on behalf of account holders
Number of Accounts Over 1.5 million CDP Securities Accounts held by retail investors [unverified – approximate figure based on industry reports]

Key Statistics (FY2025, July 2024 – June 2025): - Securities Daily Average Traded Value (SDAV): S$1.34 billion (up 26.5% YoY) - Total Securities Traded Value: S$336.4 billion (up 27.5% YoY) - Average Net Clearing Fees: 2.59 basis points (up from 2.46 bps)

[Source: SGX Group Annual Report FY2025]

4.5 CSD Linkages

CDP maintains cross-border linkages with international CSDs and settlement systems:

Counterpart Type Description
Clearstream Banking Luxembourg (CBL) CSD Link CBL clients can settle transactions with counterparties who are direct account holders with CDP; allegements accepted via depository from domestic market counterparties [Source: Clearstream – Settlement Services Singapore]
Euroclear Indirect Link Access to Singapore market securities through correspondent relationships [unverified – specific link details not confirmed]
MAS MEPS+ Cash Settlement CDP uses MAS’s MEPS+ RTGS system for SGD cash settlement; MEPS+ also handles SGS bond settlement directly
Settlement Banks Banking Interface CDP interfaces with designated settlement banks (e.g., DBS, OCBC, UOB, international banks) for cash settlement on behalf of clearing members

KRX/KSD Comparison: KSD maintains bilateral CSD linkages with over 50 foreign CSDs and ICSDs for cross-border settlement. CDP’s linkage network is smaller, reflecting Singapore’s different market positioning as a financial hub with strong custodial bank intermediation.

4.6 Securities Lending & Borrowing

CDP operates the SGX Securities Borrowing and Lending (SBL) programme:

Feature Details
Role CDP acts as principal counterparty to both lender and borrower; lenders take on counterparty risk of CDP, not the borrower
Eligibility (Lender) Retail and institutional investors with CDP securities accounts; minimum holdings: 10,000 units for shares priced <=S$1, or S$10,000 in value for shares priced >S$1
Borrowing Rate 0.5% for index stocks, REITs, business trusts; 4.0% for other securities (as of Dec 2019 variable rate introduction)
Lending Fee Lenders receive 70% of the borrowing fee
Title Transfer Legal ownership temporarily transferred to borrower; voting rights suspended during lending period; economic benefits (dividends) retained by lender
Registration Fee Free for lenders

[Source: SGX Group Media Centre – SBL Enhancement (2019); SGX Securities Lending Pool]

6. Regulatory Framework (MAS Oversight)

CDP is regulated by the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) under multiple frameworks:

Framework Details
Securities and Futures Act 2001 (SFA) CDP is licensed as an Approved Clearing House (ACH) and operates a central depository system. MAS has powers to regulate, inspect, investigate, and enforce compliance.
Payment Systems (Oversight) Act (PS(O)A) MAS may designate CDP’s payment and settlement systems under PS(O)A, subjecting them to additional oversight requirements including access regime provisions.
Companies Act CDP is additionally regulated as a central depository under the Companies Act.
PFMI Standards CDP is expected to observe the CPMI-IOSCO Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures and publishes periodic self-assessments.
MAS Supervisory Powers Include power to request information, make regulations, inspect, investigate, establish access regimes, and issue directions.

Key Regulatory Requirements for CDP: - Fair and objective access criteria for participation - Safe and efficient functioning of clearing facility - Protection of the investing public - Adequate financial resources and risk management - Regular reporting and disclosure to MAS

[Source: MAS – Clearing Houses; MAS – ACH/RCH Licence]

KRX Comparison: KRX is supervised by the Financial Services Commission (FSC) and Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) of Korea. Unlike CDP which is a private company subsidiary of a listed exchange, KRX is a special-purpose corporation established under the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act.

7. Interoperability

CDP does not currently maintain formal CCP interoperability arrangements (CCP links) with other CCPs for cross-margining or mutual clearing. Its interoperability is primarily through:

  • CSD Linkages: As described in Section 4.5, CDP maintains links with Clearstream and indirect access via Euroclear for cross-border settlement
  • SGX Group Integration: Within SGX Group, CDP and SGX-DC operate as complementary but separate clearing entities – CDP for securities and SGX-DC for derivatives. There is no formal cross-margining between the two entities. [unverified – cross-margining status]
  • ASEAN Linkage: SGX has been involved in ASEAN exchange connectivity initiatives (ASEAN Trading Link, launched 2012 but discontinued), which aimed to enable cross-border trading and settlement across ASEAN exchanges. [Source: Industry reports]

9. Recent Developments

2022 – Marketnode DLT Direct-to-Depository Service

SGX CDP made available Marketnode’s DLT-enabled direct-to-depository service for debt issuers. The platform eliminates paper trails and reduces bond settlement time by 60% (from T+5 to T+2). OCBC Bank’s US$100 million Euro Commercial Paper issuance was the first transaction on the platform. Over S$1 billion in digital bonds have been issued through the platform. [Source: SGX Group (Jun 2022); Marketnode]

2023 – Margin and Clearing Fund Framework Refinements

Effective 26 May 2023 (rules) and 12 June 2023 (margin methodology), CDP implemented significant changes: - Revised approach for sizing the Clearing Fund to be more risk-sensitive - Deprioritised contingent contributions – funded resources now utilised first - Cap on clearing members’ total clearing fund liability - CDP maintains skin-in-the-game at minimum 25% of the Clearing Fund

[Source: Allen & Gledhill (2023)]

2024 – Revised Clearing Member Liability Cap for Multiple Defaults

SGX Group refined the cap on clearing members’ liability for multiple defaults, providing CMs with greater certainty on their maximum financial exposure in tail-risk events involving sequential defaults. [Source: Mondovisione (Mar 2024)]

2025 – Strong Market Activity

FY2025 (July 2024 – June 2025) saw significantly increased activity: - Securities daily average traded value reached S$1.34 billion (up 26.5%) - Total securities traded value reached S$336.4 billion (up 27.5%)

[Source: SGX Group Annual Report FY2025]

Digital Asset and Blockchain Initiatives

  • SGX and Temasek established Marketnode as a joint venture for digital market infrastructure
  • MAS and SGX successfully piloted blockchain technology for settlement of tokenised assets (Project Ubin) [Source: MAS Media Release (2018)]
  • SGX Derivatives (separate from CDP) launched Bitcoin and Ethereum perpetual futures in November 2025
