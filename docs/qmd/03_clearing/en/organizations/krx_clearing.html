1. Overview & Profile

Item Details
Official Name Korea Exchange (한국거래소) — Clearing Division
Abbreviation KRX
Website https://global.krx.co.kr
Year Established 2005 (as KRX); clearing function inherited from predecessor exchanges (KOFEX 1999, KSE 1956)
Country / City South Korea / Busan (headquarters); Seoul (market operations)
Ownership Structure Public institution under the Financial Services Commission (FSC); not a stock corporation but a member-based self-regulatory organization established by statute
Parent Company / Group None (independent statutory entity)
Regulatory Authority FSC (Financial Services Commission) as primary regulator; FSS (Financial Supervisory Service) conducts examination; BOK (Bank of Korea) provides systemic oversight
Clearing Model Integrated exchange-CCP — KRX serves as both trading venue and central counterparty for all exchange-traded products
Products Cleared Equities (KOSPI, KOSDAQ), listed derivatives (futures & options), bonds, OTC IRS/CRS
Clearing / Settlement Agent KSD (Korea Securities Depository, 한국예탁결제원) handles settlement and custody as CSD
CPMI-IOSCO PFMI Compliance IMF FSAP assessment conducted (2014); publishes regulatory disclosures per FSC requirements
QCCP Status Recognized as a Qualifying CCP under Basel III framework

[Source: KRX Global Website, https://global.krx.co.kr]; [Source: IMF, “Republic of Korea FSAP”, 2014]; [Source: Capital Market Act (자본시장과 금융투자업에 관한 법률)]

Key Historical Milestones

  • 1956: Korea Stock Exchange (KSE) established with basic clearing function for equities.
  • 1996: Derivatives clearing function added with the launch of KOSPI 200 futures/options — one of the world’s first index derivatives products to achieve massive retail participation.
  • 1999: Korea Futures Exchange (KOFEX) established in Busan with dedicated derivatives clearing.
  • 2005 (January 27): Korea Exchange (KRX) formed through merger of KSE, KOFEX, and KOSDAQ; unified clearing across all market segments under one CCP.
  • 2009: EXTURE (Exchange Trading Using Revolutionary Engine) trading/clearing system launched.
  • 2014: IMF FSAP assessment completed, evaluating KRX’s clearing and risk management against PFMI standards.
  • 2016: EXTURE+ next-generation platform deployed with enhanced clearing capabilities.
  • 2021: Split settlement system introduced for equity settlement (10 billion KRW units).
  • 2024: Split settlement units reduced from KRW 10 billion to KRW 5 billion (15-20% deposit reduction for members); derivatives daily settlement volume up 47.9% YoY.
  • 2025 (April): KRX clearing/settlement technology exported to Thai Stock Exchange (SET).
  • 2025 (June): Night session clearing operational (18:00-06:00 KST) for derivatives.
  • 2026 (February): Settlement operating hours expanded from 9:00-16:10 to 7:00-16:10.
  • 2026 (Late April): Further split settlement reduction from KRW 5 billion to KRW 1 billion units.

2. Clearing Model

2.1 CCP Services

KRX operates as an integrated exchange-CCP, a model common among Asian exchanges (similar to pre-reform structures at HKEX, SGX, and historical structures at JPX before the JSCC spin-off):

  • Novation: KRX interposes itself as the counterparty to all matched trades — becoming the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer upon trade matching.
  • Scope: All exchange-traded products across KOSPI Market, KOSDAQ Market, derivatives market, bond market, and mandated OTC products (IRS/CRS).
  • Multilateral Netting: Net obligations calculated by settlement category (equity trading, bond trading, repo, derivatives).
  • Guarantee Function: KRX guarantees settlement of all matched trades through its default waterfall and clearing fund structure.

[Source: KRX, “증권시장 청산결제 업무규정_전문_20260304”]

2.2 Clearing Membership

From KRX regulations (업무규정):

Category Description
Clearing Members (결제회원) Securities companies and other financial institutions meeting capital requirements set by FSC regulations; directly participate in KRX clearing
Membership Tiers Based on capital adequacy and business scope — different requirements for equity, derivatives, and bond clearing
Capital Requirements Per FSC regulations; minimum net capital requirement varies by business scope (e.g., KRW 20 billion+ for derivatives clearing)
Non-Clearing Participants Must clear through a clearing member; the clearing member assumes guarantee obligation for the non-clearing participant’s trades
Clearing Member Obligations Maintain clearing deposits, contribute to Joint Compensation Fund, meet margin requirements, comply with position limits

[Source: KRX, “파생상품시장 업무규정_전문_20250609”]

2.3 Netting

From 증권시장 청산결제 업무규정, Article 9:

Netting Category Description
Equity Trading (주식매매) Multilateral netting of all buy/sell obligations per settlement date
Government Bond Market (국채시장) Special netting provisions with separate calculation
Repo Transactions (환매채거래) Netted independently from outright bond trades
Bond Market (채권시장) Separate netting cycle from equity market
Derivatives Daily mark-to-market with cash settlement of variation margin
  • Settlement amounts calculated to KRW 1 precision.
  • Netting is legally enforceable under the Capital Market Act, providing protection against insolvency claw-back.

[Source: KRX, “증권시장 청산결제 업무규정_전문_20260304”]

3. Risk Management

3.1 Margin Framework

Listed Derivatives — KRX-SPAN (Modified SPAN)

KRX employs a modified SPAN (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk) methodology for listed derivatives margin calculation:

  • Methodology: Scenario-based approach drawing on CME SPAN portfolio risk concepts, adapted for Korean market characteristics.
  • Calculation Frequency: Daily at market close; positions marked to settlement price.
  • Margin Formula: Position x Price x Multiplier x Margin Rate.
  • Typical Initial Margin Rate: ~8-10% for KOSPI 200 futures.
  • Maintenance Margin Rate: ~5-6% (lower than initial margin threshold).

[Source: KRX, “파생상품시장 업무규정_전문_20250609”]

Margin Types

Type Korean Term Description
Initial Margin 개시증거금 Required when opening new positions; must be deposited before or at time of order
Maintenance Margin 유지증거금 Minimum balance required to maintain positions; breach triggers margin call
Trading Margin 거래증거금 Cleared by exchange; due by 11:00 next trading day for member-level settlement
Client Margin 위탁증거금 Pre-funded before order entry for retail; post-funded (due by 11:00 next day) for qualified institutional investors
Spread Margin 스프레드증거금 Reduced rate for recognized spread positions (calendar, inter-commodity)

[Source: KRX, “파생상품시장 업무규정_전문_20250609”]

Options Margin

  • Buy Orders: Full premium must be deposited in cash.
  • Sell Orders: Margin is the greater of three calculations involving theoretical price + volatility scenarios.
  • Spread Trading: Reduced margin with substitute securities allowed within limits.

Eligible Collateral (대용증권)

Collateral Type Haircut / Acceptance Rate
Cash (현금) 100%
Listed Stocks (meeting liquidity criteria) 70-80% of market value (quarterly adjusted)
Government Bonds (국채) Varies by maturity; generally 95-98%
Corporate Bonds (BBB+ minimum rating) Varies by maturity and rating; generally 80-90%
ETFs and Index Securities Accepted with restrictions on leveraged/inverse products
Foreign Currency (10 types) USD, JPY, EUR, GBP, HKD, AUD, SGD, CHF, CAD, CNY — haircuts vary by currency

Concentration Limits: - Individual stock: Maximum 50% of average daily trading volume. - Corporate bonds: Maximum 10% of total issuance amount. - Fund securities: Maximum 10% of fund size.

Cash Requirement: Minimum 50% of total margin must be in cash; remainder may be substitute securities.

Qualified Institutional Investor Exception: May defer margin deposit until 10:00 next trading day.

[Source: KRX, “파생상품시장 업무규정 시행세칙”]

3.2 Margin Call Schedule

Type Korean Term Trigger Deadline
Regular Margin Call 정규증거금 Daily mark-to-market at close 11:00 next trading day
Intraday Margin Call 장중추가증거금 KOSPI 200 index volatility >= 80% of futures margin rate, OR member’s intraday requirement >= 120% of deposit 2 hours from notification
Additional Margin Call 추가증거금 Mark-to-market losses exceed thresholds beyond regular margin 1 hour after 2-hour buffer period
  • Intraday margin call is released if triggering conditions ease within 30 minutes of notification.
  • Failure to meet margin call within deadline results in position liquidation and potential clearing membership suspension.

[Source: KRX, “파생상품시장 업무규정_전문_20250609”]

3.3 Default Waterfall (손해배상 체계)

The default waterfall for derivatives clearing follows a “defaulter pays first” principle:

Layer Resource Korean Term Estimated Size
1 Defaulter’s margin deposits 위탁증거금 Per-position (varies)
2 Defaulter’s clearing deposit / contribution 회원증거금 Per-member (varies)
3 Joint Compensation Fund 손해배상공동기금 ~KRW 100 billion (~USD 75 million) [estimated]
4 KRX’s own capital contribution (SITG) 거래소 자기자본 [data not publicly disclosed]
5 Non-defaulting members’ additional contributions 회원 추가분담금 Assessed pro-rata based on clearing activity

Key Features: - KRX places Skin-in-the-Game (SITG) before non-defaulting members’ resources, consistent with PFMI guidance. - Joint Compensation Fund contributions follow a Fixed + Variable model: fixed base contribution plus variable component based on clearing volume/risk. - Replenishment of the Joint Compensation Fund is required after usage in a default event. - Recovery tools include: asset liquidation, securities sales, netting of claims against the defaulter, and assessment powers on surviving members.

[Source: KRX, “파생상품시장 업무규정_전문_20250609”; IMF, “Republic of Korea FSAP”, 2014]

Bonds (general) T+1 to T+2 3:30 PM

[Source: KRX, “증권시장 청산결제 업무규정_전문_20260304”]

4.2 DVP Model

  • KRX/KSD employ a DVP (Delivery versus Payment) model for securities settlement.
  • Securities leg: KSD (Korea Securities Depository) acts as CSD, handling book-entry transfer of securities.
  • Cash leg: Settlement through Bank of Korea (BOK) for large-value government bond transactions; settlement banks (KEB Hana Bank, KB Kookmin Bank, etc.) for equity and other settlement.
  • Central bank money: Used for government bond settlement to eliminate settlement bank credit risk.
  • Simultaneous exchange: Securities delivery and payment occur simultaneously to eliminate principal risk.

[Source: KRX, “증권시장 청산결제 업무규정 시행세칙_전문_20250304”]

4.3 Settlement Finality

Item Details

Finality Point Transfer orders entered into KRX/KSD system are legally enforceable and irrevocable
Insolvency Protection Netting and settlement finality provisions protect against insolvency claw-back under Korean law

[Source: Capital Market Act; IMF, “Republic of Korea FSAP”, 2014]

4.4 Settlement System Overhaul (2021-2026)

KRX has been progressively reforming its equity settlement system through “split settlement” (분할결제):

Year Change Impact
2021 Split settlement introduced Settlement obligations divided into KRW 10 billion units, settled sequentially throughout the day
2024 Units reduced from KRW 10B to KRW 5B 15-20% reduction in settlement deposit requirements for members
2026 (February) Operating hours expanded Settlement window extended from 9:00-16:10 to 7:00-16:10
2026 (Late April) Units reduced from KRW 5B to KRW 1B Further reduction in member deposit burden; more granular liquidity management

Rationale: Split settlement reduces the peak liquidity requirement for clearing members by distributing settlement obligations throughout the day, lowering systemic risk and member funding costs.

Market Context: Average daily equity trading value reached KRW 29.6 trillion (Jan-Mar 2026), up 138.7% YoY, necessitating the infrastructure upgrade.

[Source: KRX, “증권시장 청산결제 업무규정_전문_20260304”]

6. Regulatory Framework

Item Details
Primary Regulator Financial Services Commission (금융위원회, FSC) — policy-making and rule approval
Examination Authority Financial Supervisory Service (금융감독원, FSS) — on-site examination and off-site surveillance
Systemic Oversight Bank of Korea (한국은행, BOK) — oversight of systemically important payment and settlement systems
Governing Law Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act (자본시장과 금융투자업에 관한 법률, FSCMA)
Clearing Regulations 파생상품시장 업무규정 (Derivatives Market Business Regulations); 증권시장 청산결제 업무규정 (Securities Market Clearing & Settlement Regulations)
PFMI Compliance IMF FSAP 2014 assessment completed; Korea assessed as largely observant of PFMI principles
CCP Recovery & Resolution No dedicated CCP recovery and resolution regime as of 2026 (similar to Japan, Hong Kong, Singapore)
OTC Clearing Mandate KRW-denominated IRS (Interest Rate Swap) and CRS (Currency Rate Swap) mandated for central clearing through KRX
Self-Regulatory Function KRX has SRO status; conducts market surveillance and member supervision alongside FSS

Korea’s Dual Regulatory Structure: Korea’s FSC/FSS dual structure is distinctive — the FSC handles policy-making, licensing, and regulatory approvals while the FSS conducts day-to-day supervision, examination, and enforcement. This separation of policy from execution is unusual among major markets and provides a form of checks and balances in financial regulation.

[Source: IMF, “Republic of Korea FSAP”, 2014; Capital Market Act]

Key Observations: - KRX’s default fund size is notably smaller than global peers, reflecting the domestic-only scope and integrated exchange-CCP model where exchange revenue provides an implicit backstop. - KRX’s OTC clearing scope is narrower than peers, limited to KRW-denominated products — expansion could enhance systemic risk management. - The integrated exchange-CCP model provides operational efficiency (high STP, no trade matching delays) but raises governance questions about conflicts of interest between commercial and risk management functions. - KRX’s planned transition from SPAN to VaR-based margining would align it with global best practice trends.
