2. Clearing Model

2.1 CCP Services

Service Product Class Description Key Statistics
Cash Equities Equities, ETFs, Depositary Receipts Pan-European clearing across 52 venues and segments covering approximately 95% of all European equity trades 1.2 billion client trades cleared in 2024 (+5% YoY); EUR 49.3 billion average daily CCP protection value [Source: Cboe Clear Europe Annual Report 2024]
Equity Derivatives Options and Futures on European equities Clearing for Cboe Europe Derivatives (CEDX), a pan-European equity derivatives exchange launched 2021 210,000 lots cleared in 2024 (up from 94,000 in 2023) [Source: Cboe Clear Europe Annual Report 2024]
Securities Financing Transactions (SFT) Securities lending and borrowing Matching, CCP clearing, settlement, and post-trade lifecycle management for SFTs in European cash equities and ETFs Launched March 2025; nine initial participants including Bank of America and State Street; settlement across 19 European CSDs [Source: PR Newswire]

Covered Markets (Securities from 20+ European markets): Including but not limited to Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, United Kingdom, and Poland. [Source: Cboe Clear - Markets]

2.2 Novation Process

  • Open Offer Model: Cboe Clear Europe interposes itself as the central counterparty through novation at the point of trade registration. The original bilateral obligation between buyer and seller is replaced by two new obligations – one between the CCP and each counterparty.
  • Real-time Processing: Transactions are fed to the risk management system as soon as they are received from the trading platform. Margin and P&L are recalculated in real-time.
  • Trade Registration: Trades are accepted and novated upon receipt from connected trading venues, subject to risk validation checks.

[Source: CPMI-IOSCO Disclosure Framework - EuroCCP Sept 2020]

2.3 Netting

Multilateral Netting: - Cboe Clear Europe performs multilateral netting across all cleared positions per clearing participant, reducing gross settlement obligations to net amounts per ISIN per CSD. - Netting Efficiency: In 2024, netting eliminated 70.29% (EUR 34.6 billion) of the average daily value that would otherwise require settlement. This means that of the EUR 49.3 billion average daily CCP protection value, only approximately EUR 14.7 billion required actual settlement each day. - Cross-venue Netting: A key benefit of Cboe Clear Europe is that positions from multiple trading venues can be netted together into a single settlement obligation per ISIN, significantly reducing settlement volume and costs.

[Source: Cboe Clear Europe Annual Report 2024]

3. Risk Management

3.1 Margin Framework

Cboe Clear Europe uses a Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) based margin model, as required under EMIR.

Securities Margin Model Parameters:

Parameter Value
Model Type Historical simulation CVaR
Confidence Level 99% (with CVaR capturing tail risk beyond 99th percentile)
Margin Period of Risk (MPOR) 2 business days (for cash equities); 3 business days (for certain products) [unverified – MPOR may differ by service]
Lookback Window 700 historical scenarios (approximately 2.8 years of data)
Minimum Data Requirement At least 12 months, capturing a full range of market conditions including stress periods (per EMIR requirement)
Anti-Procyclicality Measure Stressed Historical Simulation (StressHS) margin, per EMIR RTS Article 28(1)(b)
Recalculation Frequency At least every 5 minutes in real-time

[Source: Cboe Clear Europe Margin Model Description - Securities; ECB Occasional Paper on CCP IM Models]

Margin Components:

Component Description
Initial Margin (IM) Potential future exposure based on CVaR model; covers worst-case losses over MPOR at 99% confidence
Variation Margin (VM) Mark-to-market / current exposure reflecting unrealized P&L
Total Margin Max [0; (Initial Margin - Variation Margin)]; i.e., if VM gains exceed IM, no additional margin is required
Add-ons Additional margin to cover risks not captured by core IM model (e.g., concentration risk, liquidity risk, wrong-way risk)

Cross-Product Margining: - Cboe Clear Europe offers cross-product margining across cash equities, derivatives, and SFT positions, recognizing offsets where correlations justify reduced margin.

[Source: CPMI-IOSCO Disclosure Framework - EuroCCP Sept 2020]

Real-Time Risk Management (RTRM): - The risk management system (COH/RTRM – Collateral Haircut / Real Time Risk Management) calculates margin requirements for Clearing Participants and Co-CCPs. - Prices are updated instantaneously upon receipt from data providers (Refinitiv). - P&L in portfolios is recalculated in real-time.

3.2 Margin Call Schedule

Margin Call Timing Description
End-of-Day Margin After market close Full recalculation based on closing prices
Intraday Margin Throughout trading hours Triggered when real-time monitoring detects that margin is insufficient; recalculated at least every 5 minutes
Interoperability Fund Before 09:00 CET Daily determination of required Interoperability Fund Deposits; if additional deposit required, notification by email before 09:00 CET, account debited via Direct Debit before 09:30 CET

[Source: Cboe Clear Europe - Risk Management]

3.3 Default Waterfall

The default waterfall defines the sequence in which financial resources are applied in the event of a clearing participant default:

Layer Resource Description
1 Defaulting Member’s Margin Initial margin and variation margin of the defaulting participant are applied first
2 Defaulting Member’s Default Fund Contribution The defaulting participant’s share of the Clearing Fund
3 CCP Skin-in-the-Game (First Tranche) Cboe Clear Europe’s own dedicated financial resources, amounting to 25% of minimum required capital. As of mid-2021 this was approximately EUR 6.8 million [Source: Cboe Insights - Dedicated Own Resources July 2021]
4 Non-Defaulting Members’ Default Fund Contributions Clearing Fund contributions of surviving (non-defaulting) clearing participants are mutualized
5 CCP Additional Dedicated Resources (Second Tranche) Additional dedicated own financial resources of Cboe Clear Europe, introduced per EU R&R Regulation Article 9(14)
6 Recovery Tools Calls beyond the default waterfall – including loss allocation to non-defaulting members, additional assessment powers, and other recovery measures

Note on Sizing: The exact current size of the Clearing Fund and CCP dedicated resources fluctuates. The 2021 disclosure indicated EUR 6.8 million in CCP skin-in-the-game (25% of minimum capital). The total Clearing Fund size varies based on stress-test outcomes and participant composition. For current figures, refer to the latest PQD disclosures. [Source: Cboe Clear Public Disclosures]

[Source: Cboe Clear Europe Clearing Rule Book, Article 9.1.2]

3.4 Default Fund / Guarantee Fund

Clearing Fund (Default Fund): - All clearing participants contribute to the Clearing Fund based on their risk profile and stress-test outcomes. - The fund is sized to cover the default of the largest member (Cover 1) or the two largest members (Cover 2) under extreme but plausible stress scenarios, as required by EMIR. - Contributions are recalculated periodically and can be called for replenishment after a default event. - Separate fund arrangements exist for the Interoperability Fund (for co-CCP exposures).

Interoperability Fund: - A dedicated fund to cover exposures arising from interoperable CCP arrangements (with LCH Ltd and SIX x-clear AG). - Daily recalculation of required deposits.

[Source: CPMI-IOSCO Disclosure Framework - EuroCCP Sept 2020]

3.5 Stress Testing & Back-Testing

Stress Testing: - Cboe Clear Europe conducts regular stress tests to assess the adequacy of financial resources under extreme but plausible market conditions, as required by EMIR Article 49. - Participates in ESMA’s EU-wide CCP stress tests. In ESMA’s 5th CCP Stress Test (2024), the European clearing system was found to be resilient overall. [Source: ESMA 5th CCP Stress Test Report] - Stress scenarios include historical scenarios (e.g., 2008 financial crisis, COVID-19 March 2020) and hypothetical extreme scenarios.

Back-Testing: - Daily back-testing of margin model adequacy to verify that collected margins would have been sufficient to cover actual observed losses. - EMIR requires CCPs to evaluate whether initial margin covered potential losses assuming a clearing member defaults on a specific historical day. - Results are reported to DNB/AFM and disclosed in CPMI-IOSCO PQD reports.

Anti-Procyclicality: - The Stressed Historical Simulation (StressHS) margin add-on ensures that margin levels do not decline excessively during calm market periods, maintaining a buffer that reduces sudden spikes during stress.

[Source: Cboe Clear Europe Margin Model Description; ESMA Opinion on CCP Backtesting]

3.6 Recovery & Resolution

Recovery Plan: - Cboe Clear Europe maintains a recovery plan and a plan for orderly wind-down, as required under the EU CCP Recovery and Resolution Regulation (CCP R&R). - Recovery tools include: - Calls for losses beyond the default waterfall (assessment powers on non-defaulting members) - Calls for additional liquidity resources - Calls for replenishment of the Clearing Fund - Variation Margin Gains Haircutting (VMGH) as an ultimate tool - Partial or full tear-up of contracts

Resolution: - The resolution authority for Cboe Clear Europe is the Single Resolution Board (SRB) or DNB [unverified – the specific resolution authority designation may depend on evolving EU CCP R&R framework implementation]. - Resolution tools mirror those available under the EU CCP R&R Regulation, including write-down of equity, position transfer, bridge CCP, and orderly wind-down.

[Source: CPMI-IOSCO Disclosure Framework - EuroCCP Sept 2020]

4.2 DVP Model

  • Cboe Clear Europe uses Delivery-versus-Payment (DVP) settlement, ensuring that the transfer of securities occurs simultaneously with the transfer of cash, eliminating principal risk.
  • Settlement instructions are submitted by Cboe Clear Europe to the relevant CSD on behalf of clearing participants (via CSD Power of Attorney model).

4.3 Settlement Finality

  • Settlement finality is governed by the EU Settlement Finality Directive (SFD), as implemented in Dutch law and the laws of each CSD’s jurisdiction.
  • Once settlement instructions are entered into the relevant CSD settlement system and become irrevocable, they cannot be unwound, even in the event of insolvency of a participant.
  • Cboe Clear Europe is designated as a system under the SFD in the Netherlands.

[Source: Cboe Clear - Regulation Settlement]

4.4 CSD / Settlement Infrastructure

  • Cboe Clear Europe does not operate its own CSD. It relies on national CSDs for settlement of securities.
  • Settlement is performed through the domestic CSD of the relevant market, ensuring that securities remain in the home market’s settlement infrastructure.
  • The CSD participant (clearing participant or its settlement agent) grants Cboe Clear Europe a CSD Power of Attorney (PoA) to submit settlement instructions on their behalf.

4.6 Securities Lending & Borrowing

SFT Clearing Service (launched March 2025): - Cboe Clear Europe introduced centrally cleared Securities Financing Transactions, a first for pan-European equity SFTs. - The service covers matching, CCP clearing, settlement, and post-trade lifecycle management for securities lending/borrowing in European cash equities and ETFs. - Participant categories: Principal lenders, Special Participant lenders (UCITS and non-UCITS), and borrowers. - Special Participant model: Beneficial owners (e.g., UCITS funds) can participate without paying margin or contributing to the default fund, with Required Value (RQV) models developed on a case-by-case basis [unverified – specific terms may vary]. - Cross-product margining: SFT positions can be margined together with cash equities and derivatives positions. - Initial launch participants: Nine firms including Bank of America and State Street.

[Source: Cboe Clear Europe SFT; PR Newswire]

7. Interoperability

Overview

CCP interoperability is a defining feature of Cboe Clear Europe and a core element of its competitive strategy. Under interoperability, trading firms – rather than the trading venue – choose which CCP clears their transactions. This “user choice” model promotes competition, lowers post-trade costs, and allows participants to consolidate clearing at a single CCP across multiple venues.

Interoperable CCP Partners

Co-CCP Scope Markets
LCH Ltd (EquityClear) Full interoperability Cboe Europe (CXE/BXE), Aquis Exchange, Turquoise, SIX Swiss Exchange, and others
SIX x-clear AG Full interoperability Cboe Europe, SIX Swiss Exchange, Aquis Exchange, and others

How Interoperability Works

  1. A trade is executed on an interoperable venue (e.g., Cboe Europe).
  2. The buyer and seller may each choose a different CCP (e.g., buyer chooses Cboe Clear Europe, seller chooses LCH Ltd).
  3. Each CCP novates the leg facing its own clearing participant.
  4. The two CCPs face each other for the inter-CCP leg, managing the resulting exposure through the Interoperability Fund and margin arrangements between the Co-CCPs.
  5. Settlement occurs through the normal CSD channels.

Preferred Clearing

In addition to full interoperability, Cboe Clear Europe offers a Preferred Clearing model for certain venues where full interoperability is not available (e.g., Euronext Amsterdam, Brussels, Dublin, Lisbon, Milan, Paris). Under Preferred Clearing, Cboe Clear Europe acts as an alternative CCP to the venue’s primary CCP.

  • Euronext Oslo: Cleared via the full interoperable model.
  • Euronext (other markets): Via Preferred Clearing arrangement.

[Source: Cboe - Preferred Clearing; Cboe - Interoperability]

Regulatory Framework for Interoperability

  • EMIR Title V establishes the regulatory framework for CCP interoperability arrangements.
  • Interoperable links require regulatory approval and must meet specific risk management standards.
  • The European Systemic Risk Board (ESRB) published a report in January 2019 examining the risks and benefits of CCP interoperability arrangements in Europe. [Source: ESRB Report on CCP Interoperability]

Cboe’s Advocacy for Interoperability

Cboe has been a vocal advocate for clearing competition and interoperability in Europe, arguing that Europe benefits from multiple competing CCPs rather than a single monopoly CCP. Cboe contends that interoperability has substantially lowered post-trade costs in European cash equity markets. [Source: Cboe Insights - Europe Needs Clearing Competition]

Competitive Positioning

Cboe Clear Europe has consistently positioned itself as a lower-cost alternative to incumbent CCPs, leveraging: - Economies of scale from pan-European coverage (52 venues) - Netting efficiencies that reduce settlement costs - Interoperability that allows participants to consolidate at a single CCP - Under Cboe ownership, venue coverage expanded from 37 to 52 venues, providing access to approximately 95% of all European equity trades
