2.2 Novation Process

  • Novation by open offer: For exchange-traded products and interoperable clearing, LCH becomes the buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer at the point of trade registration.
  • Novation for OTC derivatives: Upon trade registration/acceptance, the original bilateral contract is extinguished and replaced by two new contracts – one between the CCP and each original counterparty.
  • SwapClear real-time trade registration: Trades are novated within approximately 10 seconds of submission, supported by a dedicated GBP 600 million real-time trade registration (RTTR) default fund component. (RBA Assessment 2020)

2.3 Netting & Compression

Multilateral Netting: - LCH performs multilateral netting across all cleared positions, reducing gross bilateral exposures to a single net exposure to the CCP per clearing member. - RepoClear provides netting for cash bond and repo transactions, significantly reducing settlement obligations.

Portfolio Compression (SwapClear): - Compression reduces the number of line items and gross notional outstanding by combining or offsetting trades with compatible economics. - Eligible products for compression: IRS, OIS, FRAs, basis swaps, zero-coupon inflation swaps. - Techniques include blended rate compression and future cash flow netting. - In 2015 alone, SwapClear compressed $323 trillion in trades with $107 trillion net notional reduction. (LCH Compression) - LCH’s SwapClear has compressed more than $1 quadrillion of cleared notional cumulatively. (LeapRate) - Multilateral compression (via OSTTRA triReduce) generated approximately USD 1 trillion of additional gross notional reduction in a single month (September 2022). (OSTTRA)

3. Risk Management

3.1 Margin Framework

PAIRS Methodology (Portfolio Approach to Interest Rate Scenarios)

PAIRS is LCH’s proprietary margin methodology used for SwapClear, ForexClear, and Listed Rates services. (LCH Ltd Margin Methodology)

Parameter Specification
Model Type Expected Shortfall (ES) model based on filtered historical simulation incorporating volatility scaling
Confidence Level 99.7% (equivalent to ~2.75 standard deviations)
Holding Period (Members) 5-day close-out period
Holding Period (Clients) 7-day close-out period (5 days + 2 additional days for portfolio transfer)
Lookback Period 10 years of historical market data with volatility-scaled movements in yield curves and exchange rates
Floor Calibration Unscaled historical 10-year lookback plus an additional 2.5-year window covering the Global Financial Crisis (2007-2009)

Source: RBA Assessment 2020 Appendix B

PAIRS Process: 1. Collects 10 years of daily market data (yield curves, FX rates, credit spreads depending on service) 2. Applies volatility scaling (EWMA-based) to historical returns to reflect current market conditions 3. Generates a distribution of simulated portfolio P&L changes 4. Calculates Expected Shortfall at 99.7% confidence over the holding period 5. Determines initial margin as the expected loss conditional on losses exceeding the 99.7% VaR threshold

Margin Add-ons: - Credit Risk Add-on: Applied where a member’s credit exposure is not fully captured in the base PAIRS model - Liquidity Risk Add-on: Accounts for potential costs of liquidating large or concentrated positions - Concentration Risk Add-on: For portfolios with disproportionately large positions in specific tenors or currencies - Stress Margin Add-on: May be applied during periods of elevated market stress

3.3 Default Waterfall

LCH Ltd Default Waterfall

The default waterfall is operated on a per-service basis. Each clearing service has its own segregated default fund. The layers are consumed sequentially:

Layer Description Approximate Size
1 Defaulter’s Initial Margin Varies by member (total across all SwapClear: ~GBP 192 billion)
2 Defaulter’s Default Fund Contribution Varies by member (min GBP 10m for SwapClear)
3 LCH’s Skin-in-the-Game (SITG) 25% of LCH’s minimum regulatory capital requirement; approximately GBP 35 million [unverified – based on Clarus FT estimate]
4 Non-Defaulting Members’ Default Fund Contributions SwapClear: up to GBP 5.4 billion (core component, sized to Cover 2 standard); RepoClear Ltd and ForexClear: separate funds
5 RTTR Default Fund Component GBP 600 million (for real-time trade registration risk)
6 Assessment Powers (Unfunded) Up to the equivalent of each non-defaulting member’s last default fund contribution per default; maximum 3 defaults in any 6-month period
7 LCH Additional Capital / Recovery Tools Additional CCP resources; VMGH and tear-up as final resort

SwapClear Default Fund Sizing: - Core component capped at GBP 5.4 billion (as of 2020 assessment; cap raised from GBP 5 billion in September 2019) - RTTR component: GBP 600 million - Total Rates Service Default Fund cap: approximately GBP 6 billion - Sized to Cover 2 standard: able to withstand the simultaneous default of the two clearing participants (including affiliates and clients) with the largest exposures under extreme but plausible stress scenarios - Resized monthly on the first business day of each month, using the largest two participant stress test losses over initial margin (STLOIM) over a 60-day lookback period, plus a buffer

Source: RBA Assessment 2020; Clarus FT

Default Fund Contribution Methodology (SwapClear): - ~90% based on potential residual loss (stress test loss over initial margin) - ~10% based on gross notional swap positions - Recalculated monthly

3.5 Default Management Process

LCH’s default management process follows three stages:

  1. Risk Neutralization & Client Porting: Immediate hedging of the defaulting member’s portfolio to contain market risk; attempt to transfer (port) client positions and collateral to non-defaulting clearing members
  2. Portfolio Auction: The defaulting member’s portfolio is divided by product and currency, then auctioned to surviving clearing members via a structured auction process
  3. Loss Attribution: Losses from variation margin/hedging costs allocated pro-rata; auction losses allocated based on bidding outcomes

Proven Track Record: - Lehman Brothers (2008): LCH managed the default of Lehman Brothers’ $9 trillion IRS portfolio (66,390 trades) using only 35% of Lehman’s posted initial margin. No losses were imposed on non-defaulting members or the default fund. (LCH SwapClear Risk Management)

3.6 Recovery & Resolution

Recovery Tools (LCH Ltd)

Tool Description

Assessment Powers (Cash Calls) Unfunded contributions from non-defaulting members, up to the value of their last default fund contribution per default; maximum 3 defaults in any 6-month period
Partial Tear-up Termination of specific contracts to restore a matched book; used as a last resort
Full Tear-up In extreme circumstances, termination of all open contracts in the affected service
Reduced Gains Distribution (RGD) Haircuts on gains to reduce outflows

4.3 Settlement Finality

LCH Ltd: - Legal Basis: UK Financial Markets and Insolvency (Settlement Finality) Regulations 1999 (SI 1999/2979), as amended post-Brexit. LCH Ltd is designated as a “designated system” by the Bank of England. (Settlement Finality Regulations) - Point of Finality: Transfer orders entered into the system become irrevocable and cannot be unwound in the event of a participant’s insolvency proceedings. - Protection: The SF Regulations protect transfer orders and related collateral security from being invalidated by insolvency proceedings.

LCH SA: - Legal Basis: EU Settlement Finality Directive (Directive 98/26/EC) as transposed into French law; LCH SA is a designated system under French implementation. - Point of Finality: Upon entry into the system as defined by LCH SA’s rules; protected from insolvency proceedings of participants.

4.4 CSD / Settlement Infrastructure

Cash Settlement: - LCH Ltd: Primarily commercial bank money via the Protected Payments System (PPS) using settlement banks; Bank of England reserves for GBP settlement where applicable - LCH SA: Central bank money via Banque de France (TARGET system) for euro-denominated settlements; commercial bank money where applicable

Securities Settlement: - Settlement through relevant CSDs depending on the market and asset class

4.6 Securities Lending & Borrowing

  • LCH does not directly operate a securities lending facility.
  • Members may use external securities lending programs to source collateral for margin obligations.
  • LCH accepts a wide range of non-cash collateral (government bonds, covered bonds, etc.) which facilitates collateral optimization by members.
  • In 2024, HSBC and BNP Paribas were added as custodial banks for non-cash collateral, and eligible collateral expanded to include Danish covered bonds and Chinese government bonds (planned for 2025). (Risk.net)

7.3 Post-Brexit Dynamics

The most significant interoperability and cross-border issue for LCH is the post-Brexit regulatory dynamic:

  • LCH Ltd (UK) lost its EU-authorised CCP status after Brexit (31 January 2020 / end of transition period 31 December 2020). ESMA granted temporary recognition under EU EMIR Article 25 as a third-country CCP.
  • Tier 2 Classification: ESMA designated LCH Ltd as a Tier 2 (systemically important) third-country CCP, subjecting it to enhanced ESMA supervision and potential requirements to relocate certain euro-denominated clearing activities.
  • Equivalence Extensions: The EU has repeatedly extended equivalence decisions for UK CCPs, most recently to 30 June 2028, acknowledging the systemic risk of a sudden withdrawal of recognition.
  • EU Active Account Requirement: Under EMIR 3.0 (proposed), EU clearing members may be required to maintain active accounts at EU-authorised CCPs for certain systemically important products (particularly euro-denominated IRS), potentially shifting some volume from LCH Ltd SwapClear to EU-based alternatives.
  • LCH SA Repositioning: Following the loss of Euronext clearing, LCH SA has pivoted to focus on CDSClear, RepoClear SA, and the new DigitalAssetClear service, maintaining its EU-authorised CCP status.

9.5 Brexit Impact on Ltd vs SA Structure

The most consequential structural development for LCH in recent years has been the divergence of LCH Ltd and LCH SA following Brexit:

LCH Ltd (UK): - Retained its dominant position in OTC IRS clearing (SwapClear) despite loss of EU-authorised status - Gained temporary equivalence and Tier 2 recognition from ESMA, extended to 2028 - Faces ongoing political pressure from EU authorities to relocate euro-denominated clearing to the EU - Launched new business lines (Listed Rates/FMX) to diversify and strengthen its competitive position - Continued growth: $779 trillion notional cleared in H1 2024 for SwapClear alone

LCH SA (Paris): - Lost its largest revenue source (Euronext EquityClear and CommodityClear) to Euronext’s own CCP in 2023-2024 - LSEG acquired Euronext’s remaining 11.1% stake for EUR 111 million, taking full ownership - Pivoted to CDSClear (strong growth: 28 members, 88% client volume increase) and DigitalAssetClear as future growth vectors - Maintained EU-authorised CCP status, potentially positioning it as a venue for relocated euro clearing if EU active account requirements materialize - RepoClear SA continues serving eurozone government bond repo markets
