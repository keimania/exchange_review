1. Overview & Profile

1.1 Organization Profile

2. Clearing Model

2.1 CCP Services

HKSCC – Securities Clearing

HKSCC provides clearing, settlement, depository, and nominee services for securities listed on SEHK through CCASS. Two primary clearing modes exist:

Mode Description
CNS (Continuous Net Settlement) HKSCC acts as the CCP via novation, becoming the settlement counterparty to both buyer and seller. Positions in the same security are continuously netted, reducing settlement obligations. This is the default mode for Exchange Trades.
Isolated Trades (IT) Trade-for-trade settlement without HKSCC acting as CCP. Used for specific trade types where netting is not appropriate.

HKSCC also clears and settles non-exchange transactions including: Settlement Instructions (SI) for broker-custodian deliveries, Stock Borrowing and Lending transactions, Clearing Agency Transactions (options exercise settlements from SEOCH), and Investor Settlement Instructions (ISI).

China Connect Securities: HKSCC clears northbound Stock Connect trades as a participant of ChinaClear, and clears southbound trades as the host CCP with ChinaClear as its Clearing Agency Participant.

[Source: HKEX Clearing Services – Securities Overview; HKSCC PFMI Disclosure]

HKCC – Derivatives (Futures) Clearing

HKCC acts as the CCP for all derivatives contracts traded on Hong Kong Futures Exchange (HKFE), guaranteeing settlement of transactions effected by its Clearing Participants. Products cleared include:

Product Category Examples
Equity Index Futures & Options Hang Seng Index, Hang Seng China Enterprises Index, Hang Seng TECH Index, Mini-Hang Seng Index
Single Stock Futures Various Hong Kong-listed equities
Currency Futures & Options USD/CNH, EUR/CNH, JPY/USD, AUD/USD
Interest Rate Futures HIBOR futures
Commodity Futures London Metal Mini Futures (Gold, Silver, Copper, Zinc, Nickel, Aluminium)

Trade details are electronically and automatically transmitted to HKCC once validly concluded on the exchange.

[Source: HKEX Listed Derivatives Clearing Overview; HKEX Product Information]

SEOCH – Stock Options Clearing

SEOCH acts as the CCP for stock options contracts traded on SEHK. It guarantees settlement of all transactions between its Clearing Participants. SEOCH clears Hong Kong dollar (HKD) and Renminbi (RMB) denominated stock options on major Hong Kong-listed stocks.

Both HKCC and SEOCH share the DCASS infrastructure and are operationally integrated, though they maintain separate legal identities, separate Reserve Funds, and distinct rulebooks.

[Source: HKEX Listed Derivatives Clearing Services]

2.2 Novation Process

HKSCC (Securities): Upon acceptance of an Exchange Trade for CNS settlement, HKSCC interposes itself as the CCP through novation. The original bilateral obligation between the buying and selling brokers is replaced by two separate obligations: one between HKSCC and the buyer, and one between HKSCC and the seller. This process is continuous – as new trades are matched and accepted, they are netted against existing CNS positions in the same security.

HKCC and SEOCH (Derivatives): Upon registration of a trade, HKCC/SEOCH novates the contract, becoming the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer. The original contract between the two exchange participants is extinguished and replaced by two new contracts with the clearing house.

[Source: HKSCC Operational Procedures Section 9; HKCC Rules and Procedures]

2.3 Netting

HKSCC: Multilateral netting is applied daily under the CNS system. All CNS transactions in the same security are netted to produce a single net long or net short position per security per participant. Money obligations across all securities are then aggregated to determine a single net payment or receipt per participant per settlement day.

HKCC / SEOCH: Both clearing houses provide multilateral netting of obligations. Positions within each participant’s account are netted, and margin is calculated on a portfolio basis (net margining for house accounts; gross or net for client accounts depending on account type). Variation margin (mark-to-market) produces a single net pay/collect per settlement cycle.

[Source: HKSCC Operational Procedures; HKCC Rules]

3. Risk Management

3.1 Margin Framework

HKSCC – Securities Margin

HKSCC employs a two-tier margin framework based on the Hang Seng Composite Index classification:

Tier Securities Covered Methodology Margin Rate
Tier P Constituents of Hang Seng Composite LargeCap or MidCap Index; all equity ETPs and structured products with these underlyings Value-at-Risk (VaR) based portfolio margining Minimum 3.2% (effective August 14, 2023)
Tier N All non-Tier P instruments and related products Flat-rate margin by sub-group Group 1: 12% (eff. June 13, 2022); Group 2: 30% (eff. Dec 16, 2022); Group 3: 55% (eff. Oct 27, 2025)

HKSCC additionally imposes: - Mark-to-market margin: Daily mark-to-market of all CNS positions to reflect current market value - Concentration margin / margin add-ons: Discretionary additional margin on Clearing Participants with elevated or concentrated risk exposures - Dynamic Contributions: Risk-based contributions to the Guarantee Fund, calculated based on each participant’s Expected Uncollateralised Loss (EUL) over the most recent 60 business days

[Source: HKEX – HKSCC Margin page; HKSCC Initial Margin Calculation Guide]

HKCC & SEOCH – Derivatives Margin (PRiME)

Both HKCC and SEOCH use the Portfolio Risk Margining System (PRiME), a SPAN 1-compatible margining algorithm, implemented in DCASS:

Aspect Details
Methodology PRiME evaluates the maximum potential loss of a portfolio of futures and options over a one-day holding period under 16 simulated scenarios at a defined confidence level
Risk Array Represents how each derivative instrument will gain or lose value across defined price and volatility movements
Confidence Level Calibrated to provide adequate coverage; reviewed regularly
Clearing House Margin Calculated using the PRiME algorithm directly
Client Margin Clearing House Risk Margin x Client Margin Multiplier (1.33x, effective March 20, 2017)
Margining Basis House accounts: net margined; Omnibus Client, Sink, and Daily accounts: gross margined
SEOCH Premium Margin For premium-paid options in SEOCH, Mark-to-Market Margin is added to the PRiME margin to achieve total margin requirements

HKCC also applies: - Additional Margin for concentrated positions exceeding 30% of aggregate losses in stress scenarios - Capital-based position limits (CBPLs) linking allowable positions to each participant’s liquid capital

Comparison with KRX: KRX uses a SPAN-based margin system for derivatives (KOSPI 200 futures/options, etc.), similar in concept to HKEX’s PRiME. Both apply portfolio-based margining with scenario analysis. KRX additionally applies a net option value-based margin approach for certain products. The client margin multiplier differs – HKEX uses 1.33x while KRX applies different scaling depending on the product.

[Source: HKEX PRiME Margining Guide v1.5 (October 2023); HKEX Margin page – Listed Derivatives]

3.2 Margin Call Schedule

Entity Timing Details
HKSCC End-of-day (T) Provisional clearing statements issued around 18:00-20:00 on trade date; final clearing statements after 14:00 on T+1; margin and Marks payable on settlement date
HKCC End-of-day + Intraday Daily end-of-day mark-to-market and margin recalculation; intraday variation adjustments and additional margin calls may be issued during volatile market conditions
SEOCH End-of-day + Intraday Each day after close of trading, SEOCH marks positions to market with the fixing price of each option series; intraday margin calls may be triggered during extreme movements

Margin may be provided in cash (HKD, USD, RMB) or in acceptable forms of collateral as specified by each clearing house. CCMS (Common Collateral Management System) manages collateral holdings and obligations across all three clearing houses.

[Source: HKEX Clearing FAQs – Derivatives; HKSCC Operational Procedures]

3.3 Default Waterfall

HKSCC Default Waterfall (Securities)

Upon declaration of a Clearing Participant as defaulter, HKSCC applies the following resources sequentially, per Rule 2507 of the General Rules of HKSCC:

Layer Resource Description
1 Defaulting CP’s margin, Marks, and collateral All margin deposits, Marks (mark-to-market payments), collateral, admission fees, Mainland Settlement Deposit, and Mainland Security Deposit held by HKSCC
2 Defaulting CP’s Guarantee Fund contributions Both Basic Contribution and Dynamic Contribution of the defaulter
3 HKSCC’s own contribution (Skin-in-the-Game) Clearing house resources appropriated to the Guarantee Fund, equivalent to 10% of the Guarantee Fund size
4 Non-defaulting CPs’ Dynamic Contributions Pro-rata allocation of Dynamic Contributions from non-defaulting Relevant Clearing Participants
5 Non-defaulting CPs’ Basic Contributions Pro-rata allocation of Basic Contributions from non-defaulting Relevant Clearing Participants

Guarantee Fund Threshold: HKD 7.3 billion (effective June 13, 2022). When daily risk exposure exceeds 90% of the aggregate Guarantee Fund plus available credits, additional contributions are required from participants.

Guarantee Fund Risk Collateral (GFRC): When the Guarantee Fund threshold is breached, HKSCC collects GFRC in the amount of Expected Uncollateralised Loss (EUL) of the CP group in excess of 50% of the Guarantee Fund threshold. Each participant receives a HKD 1 million Dynamic Contribution Credit.

Minimum aggregate Basic Contributions: HKD 100 million.

Key protection: Customer segregated margin deposits held by the clearing house for one participant cannot be used to cover either a house or customer default of another participant.

[Source: HKEX – HKSCC Default Fund page; HKEX – HKSCC Default Management page; HKSCC General Rules Chapter 25]

HKCC Default Waterfall (Derivatives)

Layer Resource Description
1 Defaulting CP’s margin deposits All margin collateral of the defaulting participant
2 Defaulting CP’s Participant Deposit Basic deposit to the Reserve Fund
3 Defaulting CP’s Additional Deposit (HPAD) Risk-based additional deposit to the Reserve Fund
4 HKCC’s own contribution (Skin-in-the-Game) Clearing house resources equal to 10% of the Reserve Fund size
5 Non-defaulting CPs’ Reserve Fund contributions Applied “in layers” from non-defaulting participants’ deposits and additional deposits

Reserve Fund Threshold: HKD 7.3 billion (effective June 1, 2018).

Reserve Fund composition: Participant Deposits (HK$1.5M per CP, HK$7.5M per GCP) + HKCC Participant Additional Deposits (HPAD) + interest income + HKCC’s own resources (10% of fund size).

HPAD formula: Level of HPAD = (Projected Loss from stress testing – Defaulting CP Groups’ Margin and collateral) x 1.15 – Basic Elements of Reserve Fund – Clearing House contributions. As of August 2024, total HPAD requirement was HK$2,863 million.

Stress test assumptions for fund sizing: - Price movements: +/-20% for key markets; equity products range -56% to +96% - Volatility assumptions: -24% to +47% for options; equity products range -35% to +91% - Counterparty default: largest plus second-largest CP group simultaneous default (Cover 2 standard)

[Source: HKEX – HKCC Default Fund page; HKCC Reserve Fund Chapter 4; HKCC Rules and Procedures]

SEOCH Default Waterfall (Stock Options)

The SEOCH default waterfall follows a structure parallel to HKCC:

Layer Resource
1 Defaulting CP’s margin deposits
2 Defaulting CP’s Initial Contribution to Reserve Fund
3 Defaulting CP’s Variable Contributions
4 SEOCH’s own contribution (10% of Reserve Fund size)
5 Non-defaulting CPs’ Reserve Fund contributions (in layers)

Reserve Fund Threshold: HKD 3.6 billion (effective April 1, 2026).

Variable Contribution formula: Same as HKCC – (Projected Loss – Defaulting CP Groups’ Margin) x 1.15 – Basic Elements – Clearing House contributions.

Stress test assumptions for fund sizing: - Market movement: -48% to +96% - Implied volatility: -90% to +175% - Counterparty default: largest plus second-largest CP group simultaneous default (Cover 2 standard)

Key distinction: Participant contribution shares are based on that participant’s share of the average total margin requirement and net premium paid over the most recent 60 business days.

[Source: HKEX – SEOCH Default Fund page; SEOCH Rules]

3.4 Default Fund / Guarantee Fund Summary

Fund Entity Threshold HKEX Contribution Coverage Standard
Guarantee Fund HKSCC HKD 7.3 billion 10% of fund size Cover 2 (simultaneous default of two largest CP groups)
Reserve Fund HKCC HKD 7.3 billion 10% of fund size Cover 2
Reserve Fund SEOCH HKD 3.6 billion 10% of fund size Cover 2

All three funds are reviewed and recalculated monthly (on the first trading/business day of each month). Contributions are adjusted when existing amounts fall below required levels or when daily risk exposure exceeds 90% of total fund resources plus available credits.

Comparison with KRX: KRX as CCP maintains a Joint Compensation Fund for derivatives clearing, with contributions from members and KRX’s own capital. Both HKEX and KRX follow the Cover 2 standard. KRX’s fund structure is somewhat simpler as it operates as a single integrated CCP across equity and derivatives markets, whereas HKEX maintains three separate clearing houses with distinct funds.

[Source: HKEX Default Fund pages for HKSCC, HKCC, SEOCH]

4. Settlement

4.1 Settlement Cycle

Market Settlement Cycle Details
Securities (SEHK) T+2 Standard settlement for all SEHK Exchange Trades via CNS. Both securities and money settlement finalised on T+2.
Stock Connect – Northbound T+0 (securities) / T+1 (money) A-share trades settle with securities on trade day and cash on T+1, reflecting mainland China settlement conventions
Stock Connect – Southbound T+2 Follows Hong Kong T+2 settlement cycle
Derivatives (HKCC) Daily Variation margin (mark-to-market) settled daily; physical delivery contracts per contract specifications
Stock Options (SEOCH) T+1 (premium); T+2 (exercise) Premium settlement on T+1; exercise and assignment settled via CCASS on T+2

T+1 Settlement Transition: In July 2025, HKEX published a discussion paper on accelerating settlement from T+2 to T+1 for the cash market, with a feedback deadline of September 1, 2025. HKEX has confirmed its post-trade systems will be technically ready to support T+1 by end of 2025. The proposal covers all listed products (equities, ETFs, structured products, REITs, bonds) but excludes Northbound Stock Connect trades. No firm implementation date has been announced; this will be a multi-year journey with phased consultation and industry-wide testing.

Comparison with KRX: KRX operates on T+2 for equities (aligned with Hong Kong), T+1 for government bonds, and T+0 for general bonds. Both exchanges are studying T+1 settlement in the context of global trends (US, India already on T+1; Europe/UK targeting 2027).

[Source: HKEX Settlement page; HKEX Accelerated Settlement Discussion Paper (July 2025); Broadridge – Hong Kong T+1 Settlement Discussion]

4.2 DVP Model

HKSCC (Securities):

HKSCC operates a DVP settlement model within CCASS:

  • CNS settlement is always effected on a DVP basis
  • Securities are allocated and put “on hold” during the settlement day until good and irrevocable payment is made
  • Cash settlement is effected through electronic payment instructions sent by CCASS to participants’ designated banks via the Hong Kong Interbank Clearing Limited (HKICL) using the CHATS (Clearing House Automated Transfer System) real-time gross settlement system
  • Upon receipt of good and irrevocable intra-day payments from long CPs (or ChinaClear), HKSCC releases the corresponding allocated shares, achieving intra-day finality
  • Short CPs receive good and irrevocable payments in the end-of-day money settlement process

The model functions as a DVP Model 2 variant – securities settlement on a net basis with money settlement through RTGS (CHATS).

HKCC / SEOCH (Derivatives): As cash-settled derivatives, settlement is effected through daily variation margin payments. Physical delivery contracts (where applicable) settle through the relevant depository systems.

[Source: HKSCC Operational Procedures Section 13; BIS Red Book – Hong Kong; Clearstream Market Profile – Hong Kong]

4.4 CSD / Settlement Infrastructure – CCASS

CCASS (Central Clearing and Settlement System) is the core infrastructure operated by HKSCC, functioning as CSD, SSS, and clearing system:

Component Description
Depository Immobilised depository for all eligible securities; securities held in fungible book-entry form in HKSCC Nominees Limited (the nominee holding company)
Clearing Continuous Net Settlement (CNS) engine; automated matching and netting of Exchange Trades
Settlement Automated securities and money settlement; settlement instructions for non-exchange transactions
Connectivity Accessible via CCASS terminals, CCASS/IP (internet-based), and host-to-host interfaces via SDNet/2
Eligible Securities All securities listed on SEHK (equities, ETFs, warrants, debt securities, structured products); China Connect Securities
Operational Hours Settlement processing on business days; provisional clearing statements ~18:00-20:00 on T; final clearing statements after 14:00 on T+1

CCASS/3 is the current generation of the system, upgraded from earlier versions to support higher capacity and additional functionality including Stock Connect processing.

[Source: HKEX CCASS FAQ; HKEX Settlement and Depository pages; HKSCC Operational Procedures Section 9]

4.5 CSD Linkages – Stock Connect & Bond Connect

Stock Connect (Shanghai-Hong Kong & Shenzhen-Hong Kong)

Aspect Northbound (HK investors buying A-shares) Southbound (Mainland investors buying HK stocks)
Clearing Link HKSCC is a participant of ChinaClear; settles trades directly with ChinaClear on behalf of its CPs ChinaClear is a Clearing Agency Participant of HKSCC; settles southbound trades with HKSCC in Hong Kong
Securities Holding HKSCC maintains an omnibus stock account with ChinaClear (CSDC), holding A-shares on behalf of CCASS participants ChinaClear maintains an account with HKSCC in CCASS, holding HK securities on behalf of mainland investors
Settlement Cycle T+0 securities / T+1 money (following mainland convention) T+2 (following Hong Kong convention)

6. Regulatory Framework

6.1 Primary Regulation

Aspect Details
Primary Regulator Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) of Hong Kong
Primary Legislation Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap. 571) (SFO), enacted 2003
Key Provisions Part III, Division 3 of SFO sets out framework for Recognised Clearing Houses (RCHs): recognition, duties, statutory protections, settlement finality
RCH Status HKSCC, HKCC, SEOCH, and OTC Clear are all recognised clearing houses under SFO
Exchange Controller HKEX is the sole Recognised Exchange Controller under SFO Section 59(1), and is the sole shareholder of all three clearing houses
PFMI Compliance SFC requires all RCHs to observe and apply the CPMI-IOSCO Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures (PFMI)
Oversight Coordination SFC coordinates with HKMA (Hong Kong Monetary Authority), which oversees the payment and settlement infrastructure including CHATS; HKMA designated as the resolution authority for systemically important FMIs under the Financial Institutions (Resolution) Ordinance (FIRO) [unverified]

6.2 Self-Regulatory Functions

HKEX and its clearing subsidiaries exercise certain self-regulatory functions including: - Participant admission and ongoing compliance monitoring - Rulebook enforcement and disciplinary proceedings - Market surveillance (in coordination with SFC) - Financial resource monitoring of participants

The SFC retains ultimate supervisory authority and can direct HKEX entities to take specific actions in the public interest.

[Source: SFC website; SFO Cap. 571; HKEX Introduction to Regulatory Framework page]

7. Interoperability

7.1 Stock Connect (Shanghai-Hong Kong & Shenzhen-Hong Kong)

See Section 4.5 for detailed clearing and settlement arrangements. Stock Connect represents the most significant cross-border interoperability arrangement, linking HKSCC with ChinaClear (CSDC) for mutual market access between Hong Kong and mainland China equity markets.

7.2 Bond Connect

Northbound Bond Connect (since July 2017) and Southbound Bond Connect (since October 2024) link international investors with China’s interbank bond market. Settlement and custody involve CMU (HKMA’s Central Moneymarkets Unit), ChinaClear, and Shanghai Clearing House.

7.3 Swap Connect

Launched May 2023, linking OTC Clear and Shanghai Clearing House for interest rate swap clearing. See Section 4.5 for details.

7.4 ETF Connect

Launched July 2022, extending Stock Connect to include eligible ETFs traded on both SEHK and mainland exchanges.

7.5 Other CSD Linkages

HKSCC maintains Clearing Agency Participant arrangements with external clearing organisations to facilitate cross-border settlement. Clearstream and Euroclear maintain access to the Hong Kong market through their relationships with local custodians and CMU.

Comparison with KRX: KRX/KSD does not have an equivalent to Stock Connect. Cross-border access to the Korean market is primarily through the QFII-like Foreign Investor Registration system. KSD maintains CSD linkages with other Asian CSDs (e.g., through ASEAN CSD linkages) and Euroclear/Clearstream, but these are more conventional custodian/depository relationships rather than the direct CCP-CCP clearing links that characterise Stock Connect.

[Source: HKEX Stock Connect pages; HKEX Bond Connect pages; Clearstream Market Infrastructure – Hong Kong]
