Vertical Integration: ASX operates one of the world’s few fully vertically integrated exchange groups, spanning listings, trading (ASX Trade for cash equities, ASX 24 for derivatives), clearing (ASX Clear and ASX Clear (Futures)), settlement (ASX Settlement via CHESS), and depository services (Austraclear for debt securities). This vertical integration is a defining feature of the Australian financial market infrastructure. Unlike many global markets where trading, clearing, and settlement functions are operated by separate entities (e.g., US markets with exchanges, DTCC/NSCC, and DTC), ASX controls the entire post-trade value chain for the Australian market.

Comparison with KRX/KSD: Korea’s post-trade infrastructure is similarly concentrated but split across two entities: KRX (Korea Exchange) acts as the CCP for both securities and derivatives, while KSD (Korea Securities Depository) handles settlement and depository functions. ASX’s model consolidates both clearing and settlement under a single corporate group with separate legal entities for each function.

2. Clearing Model

2.1 CCP Services

ASX operates two separate CCPs, each serving distinct product classes:

ASX Clear (Futures) – Derivatives and OTC:

2.2 Novation Process

Upon acceptance of a trade for clearing, the relevant ASX CCP interposes itself as the legal counterparty to both sides of every transaction through the process of novation. The original bilateral contract between buyer and seller is extinguished and replaced by two new contracts: one between the CCP and the buyer, and one between the CCP and the seller.

  • ASX Clear: Novation occurs automatically for all trades executed on ASX Trade and Cboe Australia that are registered with ASX Clear. Novation takes place at the point of trade registration.
  • ASX Clear (Futures): Novation occurs when a trade is matched on ASX 24 or when an OTC transaction is submitted through an approved affirmation platform and accepted for clearing.

Through novation, each clearing participant only faces the CCP as its counterparty, eliminating bilateral counterparty credit risk. This is the standard CCP model consistent with PFMI Principle 4 (Credit Risk).

[Source: ASX Clearing, https://www.asx.com.au/about/regulation/clearing-and-settlement-of-cash-equities-in-australia/clearing]

2.3 Netting

ASX Clear (Cash Equities): - Employs multilateral netting of settlement obligations - For each settlement participant, all buys and sells in each individual cash market product are netted into one net buy or sell obligation per security - All payment obligations (pays and collects) are netted into one net pay or collect per settlement participant - Settlement occurs in a single daily multilateral net batch via CHESS (DvP Model 3) - Netting arrangements are approved under the Payment Systems and Netting Act 1998 - Netting efficiency is high – significantly reduces the gross value of settlement obligations

ASX Clear (Futures): - Multilateral netting of variation margin across all positions within a clearing participant’s account - Portfolio-based margining via CME SPAN recognises offsets between correlated positions - OTC interest rate derivative positions are also netted within the same clearing account

Comparison with KRX: KRX similarly employs multilateral netting for both securities and derivatives, settling net obligations through KSD. However, KRX’s netting occurs within a single CCP entity, whereas ASX maintains separate netting pools for cash (ASX Clear) and derivatives (ASX Clear (Futures)).

[Source: ASX Settlement services and pricing, https://www.asx.com.au/about/regulation/clearing-and-settlement-of-cash-equities-in-australia/settlement/settlement-services-and-pricing.html]

Capital Requirements (ASX Clear): - Two capital regimes: Risk-Based Capital Requirements (for participants clearing equities or options, unless an ADI eligible for Other Capital Regime) and Other Capital Regime (for authorised deposit-taking institutions / ADIs) - Risk-Based Capital Requirements calculated based on the participant’s risk exposures, including market risk, counterparty risk, and operational risk - Capital adequacy is monitored via end-of-day financial returns; intraday monitoring may apply during periods of high volatility - ASX Clear published a consultation in January 2026 on third-party clearing capital requirements

Note: Unlike many global CCPs (e.g., CME Clearing with its USD 5M minimum net capital for non-bank FCMs), ASX Clear’s capital requirements are more principles-based and risk-proportionate, reflecting the smaller number of clearing participants in the Australian market.

3. Risk Management

3.1 Margin Framework

ASX Clear (Cash Equities) – Cash Market Margining (CMM):

Component Methodology Details
Initial Margin Filtered Historical Simulation VaR (FHSVaR) Covers potential losses at a high confidence level; calibrated using historical price data
Mark-to-Market Margin Daily mark-to-market Reflects unrealised gains/losses on open positions
Additional Margin (Cash Equities) Concentration, liquidity, wrong-way risk add-ons Applied when positions are concentrated or in illiquid securities
CMM Methodology Update (Aug 2025) HSVaR parameters recalibrated Average increase in HSVaR margins across all clearing participants of ~12%, effective 22 August 2025

ASX Clear (Equity Derivatives / ETOs) – SPAN-based:

Component Methodology Details
Initial Margin CME SPAN (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk) Calculates maximum potential loss for a portfolio of derivatives grouped by product; adopted from the Chicago Mercantile Exchange
Calibration 3 standard deviations of 60-day historical price distribution For exchange-traded options and OTC equity/index options
Review Cycle Quarterly CME SPAN margin parameters reviewed on a three-monthly cycle

ASX Clear (Futures) – Exchange-Traded Derivatives:

Component Methodology Details
Initial Margin CME SPAN Calculates maximum potential loss for a portfolio; evaluates margin rates against multiple look-back periods (7 business days, 120 business days, and 12 months)
Calibration 3 standard deviations of historical price distributions Incorporates both short-term and long-term volatility
Review Cycle Quarterly Regular parameter reviews; ad-hoc reviews during high volatility

ASX Clear (Futures) – OTC Derivatives:

Component Methodology Details
Initial Margin Filtered Historical Simulation VaR (FHSVaR) Uses a distribution of simulated changes in portfolio value to calculate potential loss at a given confidence level
Variation Margin Daily mark-to-market Covers unrealised P&L changes on OTC positions

Comparison with KRX: KRX uses KOSCOM-TIMS (Theoretical Intermarket Margin System, based on OMS – Options Margin System) for derivatives margining, while ASX uses CME SPAN for derivatives. Both are scenario-based approaches, but SPAN is more widely adopted globally. For cash equities, KRX uses a similar VaR-based approach to ASX Clear’s CMM.

[Source: ASX Clear Margining, https://www.asx.com.au/markets/clearing-and-settlement-services/asx-clear/risk-management/margining] [Source: Understanding Margins – ASX Clear, https://www.asx.com.au/content/dam/asx/participants/clearing-and-settlement/asx-clear/understanding-margins.pdf] [Source: RBA Special Topic on Margin, 2021/22 Assessment, https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/financial-market-infrastructure/clearing-and-settlement-facilities/assessments/2021-2022/special-topics-margin.html]

3.3 Default Waterfall

ASX Clear (Cash Market) Default Waterfall:

Layer Description Size (approx.)
1 Defaulter’s margin (initial margin + mark-to-market) Variable (position-dependent)
2 ASX Clear’s own capital (SITG – Skin-in-the-Game) Included in AUD 250M default fund
3 ASX Clear Default Fund (100% funded by ASX Clear / ASX capital) AUD 250 million total
4 Recovery Assessments on non-defaulting participants Up to AUD 300 million (for one or multiple defaults)
5 Contract termination / partial tear-up As required

Key feature: ASX Clear’s default fund for cash equities is entirely funded by ASX’s own capital – clearing participants do not make upfront default fund contributions. This is unusual compared to most global CCPs (e.g., CME, Eurex, LCH) where participants contribute to the default fund proportional to their risk. The historical basis for this structure includes AUD 71.5M in restricted capital transferred from the National Guarantee Fund (NGF) in 2005, plus subordinated loans from ASX Clearing Corporation.

ASX Clear (Futures) Default Waterfall:

Layer Description Size (approx.)
1 Defaulter’s margin (initial margin + variation margin) Variable
2 Defaulter’s default fund contribution Proportional share of AUD 200M participant pool
3 ASX capital – Tranche 1 (SITG) Part of AUD 450M ASX capital
4 Non-defaulting participants’ default fund contributions – Tranche 1 Part of AUD 200M participant pool
5 ASX capital – Tranche 2 Part of AUD 450M ASX capital
6 Non-defaulting participants’ default fund contributions – Tranche 2 Remainder of AUD 200M participant pool
7 ASX capital – Tranche 3 AUD 180 million
8 Recovery Assessments on non-defaulting participants Up to AUD 200 million per default (max AUD 600M for multiple defaults)
9 Payment haircutting (variation margin haircutting) Reduces outgoing payments to allocate losses
10 Contract termination / partial tear-up As required

Total ASX Clear (Futures) Default Fund: AUD 650 million pre-funded - ASX capital contribution: AUD 450 million (across 3 tranches) - Participant contributions: AUD 200 million (across 2 tranches, commingled across futures and OTC products)

Juniorisation mechanism: In the auction process for the defaulter’s portfolio, participants’ default fund contributions are applied in order related to the size of their auction bids – the auction winner’s contribution is applied last (best protected), while the lowest bidder’s contribution is applied first. This incentivises aggressive bidding in the default management auction.

Comparison with other CCPs:

CCP Total Default Fund ASX Capital (SITG) Participant Contributions
ASX Clear (cash) AUD 250M AUD 250M (100%) AUD 0
ASX Clear (Futures) AUD 650M AUD 450M (69%) AUD 200M (31%)
CME Clearing (Base) ~USD 4.8B USD 100M ~USD 4.7B
LCH Ltd (SwapClear) ~GBP 6.0B+ GBP 35M+ ~GBP 6.0B

ASX’s model is notable for the very high proportion of CCP-funded resources relative to participant contributions, reflecting the vertically integrated ownership model.

[Source: RBA Standard 12, 2016/17 Assessment, https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/financial-market-infrastructure/clearing-and-settlement-facilities/assessments/2016-2017/asx-central-counterparties/standard-12.html] [Source: RBA Standard 12, 2015/16 Assessment of ASX Clear (Futures), https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/financial-market-infrastructure/clearing-and-settlement-facilities/assessments/2015-2016/asx-clear-futures/standard-12.html] [Source: FIA, “FIA supports ASX default management proposals”, https://www.fia.org/fia/articles/fia-supports-asx-default-management-proposals]

3.4 Default Fund / Guarantee Fund

ASX Clear: - Total size: AUD 250 million (100% funded by ASX) - No participant contributions to the default fund - Participants are instead subject to Recovery Assessments (up to AUD 300M) if the default fund is exhausted - Additional contingent resources: AUD 300 million available beyond the default fund [unverified – exact structure of contingent resources] - Review frequency: Reviewed periodically by ASX and assessed annually by the RBA

ASX Clear (Futures): - Total size: AUD 650 million pre-funded - ASX capital: AUD 450 million (3 tranches) - Participant contributions: AUD 200 million (2 tranches) - Contribution methodology: Participant contributions are proportional to risk brought to the CCP (based on initial margin requirements) - Contributions commingled across futures and OTC derivative products - Review frequency: Reviewed periodically; RBA assesses annually - Replenishment: After a default event and a 22-business-day cooling-off period, default funds are replenished to up to AUD 400 million for ASX Clear (Futures) and AUD 150 million for ASX Clear

[Source: RBA 2024/25 Assessment of ASX Clearing and Settlement Facilities] [Source: ASX PFMI Disclosure Framework]

3.5 Stress Testing & Back-testing

Credit Stress Testing (CST):

  • Both CCPs conduct daily credit stress tests to assess the adequacy of financial resources
  • Cover 2 standard: ASX Clear (Futures) sizes its financial resources to cover the default of the two participants with the largest exposures under extreme but plausible market conditions (consistent with PFMI Principle 4)
  • ASX Clear also targets a Cover 2 standard for its default fund sizing

Stress Test Methodology:

Scenario Type Description
Historical Based on observed market events (e.g., GFC 2008, COVID-19 March 2020)
Hypothetical Constructed by ASX risk management to cover extreme but plausible scenarios
Theoretical Mathematical/statistical extreme scenarios
Volatility Skew (Phase 3, Sep 2024) Scaling factors to account for equity volatility skew on out-of-the-money index and equity options
Specific Wrong-Way Risk (Nov 2024) Additional scenarios to address wrong-way risk exposures

Back-testing: - Regular back-testing of margin models to assess the adequacy of margin rates - Comparison of margin held against actual portfolio losses

RBA Concerns (2024/25 Assessment): - The RBA was “disappointed by the number of issues identified with ASX’s financial risk models” during the assessment period - Errors were identified in ASX’s credit stress tests, leading to the downgrade of the Credit Risk Standard to “partly observed” for both CCPs - RBA expects ASX to prioritise improving its model validation and control testing frameworks, particularly data and reporting controls

[Source: ASX Clear Credit Stress Test methodology, August 2024, https://asxonline.com/content/asxonline/public/notices/2024/august/0954.24.08.html] [Source: ASX Clear Credit Stress Test parameters, March 2024, https://www.asxonline.com/content/asxonline/public/notices/2024/march/0226.24.03.html] [Source: RBA 2024/25 Assessment Executive Summary, https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/financial-market-infrastructure/clearing-and-settlement-facilities/assessments/2024-2025/executive-summary.html]

3.6 Recovery & Resolution

Recovery Tools:

Both ASX Clear and ASX Clear (Futures) have formal ASX Recovery Rules (effective from November 2017, amended since), which provide the following tools:

Tool ASX Clear ASX Clear (Futures)
Recovery Assessments Up to AUD 300 million for one or multiple defaults Up to AUD 200 million per default; max AUD 600 million for multiple defaults within a defined default period
Variation Margin Haircutting Not available – variation margin is applied differently in cash equities (collected on losses but not paid out on gains in the same way as derivatives) Available – ASX Clear (Futures) can reduce (haircut) outgoing payments to participants to allocate losses
Partial Tear-up Available – forced termination of some open contracts to restore a matched book Available
Complete Termination Available – termination of all open contracts with haircutting of settlement payments Available
Auction / Default Management ASX can auction the defaulter’s portfolio ASX can auction the defaulter’s portfolio; juniorisation mechanism incentivises bidding

Resolution Authority: - Australia does not currently have a dedicated CCP resolution regime comparable to the EU’s CCP Recovery and Resolution Regulation (CCPRRR) or the US Dodd-Frank Title II - The RBA and ASIC would coordinate any resolution actions under existing powers in the Corporations Act 2001 and the Reserve Bank Act 1959 - The Council of Financial Regulators (CFR) has considered the need for a dedicated CCP resolution framework, but as of 2026 no formal legislation has been enacted [unverified]

[Source: ASX Recovery Rules Handbook, https://www.asx.com.au/content/dam/asx/rules-guidance-notes-waivers/asx-recovery-rules/asx-recovery-rules-handbook.pdf] [Source: ASX Recovery and Resolution, https://www.asx.com.au/markets/clearing-and-settlement-services/asx-clear/risk-management/recovery-and-resolution] [Source: RBA Special Topic on Default Management and Recovery, 2019/20, https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/financial-market-infrastructure/clearing-and-settlement-facilities/assessments/2019-2020/special-topic-default-management-and-recovery.html] [Source: RBA Recovery and Resolution Bulletin, December 2013, https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2013/dec/5.html]

4. Settlement

4.1 Settlement Cycle

Product Settlement Cycle Notes
Cash Equities T+2 Standard cycle since March 2016 (moved from T+3)
Equity Options (ETOs) T+1 (premium); exercise settlement varies Premium paid T+1; exercise settlement follows underlying equity cycle
Government Bonds (Austraclear) T+2 Settled via Austraclear RTGS
Futures Daily mark-to-market (variation margin); final settlement varies by contract No physical settlement for most financial futures
OTC Derivatives Daily variation margin; maturity settlement as per contract

T+1 Transition Plans: - ASX published an industry whitepaper in April 2024; T+1 Working Group established December 2023 - Industry consensus: T+1 is desirable but should not proceed until CHESS replacement is complete - No confirmed T+1 implementation date; earliest feasible timeline likely 2029+ (after CHESS Phase 2 delivery) - International context: US moved to T+1 in May 2024; EU targeting 2027; UK targeting 2027

[Source: ASX T+1 Settlement Cycle, https://www.asx.com.au/markets/clearing-and-settlement-services/t1-settlement-cycle]

4.2 DVP Model

ASX Settlement (CHESS) – Cash Equities: - BIS DvP Model 3: Multilateral net batch settlement - Settlement of all transactions due on a given business day occurs in a single daily batch that completes at approximately 12:30pm AEST - Securities and cash obligations are settled simultaneously on a net basis - CHESS also has functionality for DvP Model 1 (item-by-item RTGS settlement), but this is not currently used by any settlement participant

Austraclear – Debt Securities: - BIS DvP Model 1: Simultaneous exchange of assets (cash and securities) on an item-by-item basis in real time - Interbank cash element settled via RITS (Reserve Bank Information and Transfer System) using real-time gross settlement (RTGS) across Exchange Settlement Accounts (ESAs) at the RBA - Settlement in central bank money

Comparison with KRX/KSD: KSD uses DvP Model 1 for government bonds (real-time settlement via BOK-Wire+) and a combination of DvP Model 2/3 for equities (multilateral netting with same-day settlement). ASX’s single daily batch for equities (DvP Model 3) is less granular than some Asian markets but provides high netting efficiency.

[Source: RBA Standard 10, 2017/18 Assessment, https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/financial-market-infrastructure/clearing-and-settlement-facilities/assessments/2017-2018/asx-securities-settlement-facilities/standard-10.html] [Source: RBA Standard 7, 2017/18 Assessment, https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/financial-market-infrastructure/clearing-and-settlement-facilities/assessments/2017-2018/asx-securities-settlement-facilities/standard-07.html]

4.3 Settlement Finality

  • Legal basis: Payment Systems and Netting Act 1998 (PSN Act) – provides legal protections for payments and transfers of assets through approved RTGS systems
  • CHESS: The RBA has declared CHESS an approved RTGS system under the PSN Act, providing protection from reversal or invalidation under insolvency law
  • Point of finality (CHESS): Settlement is final and irrevocable upon completion of the daily batch settlement process (~12:30pm AEST)
  • Austraclear: Also an approved RTGS system; settlement is final when the security record amendments are made and RITS confirms the cash transfer has been effected
  • Irrevocability: Once settlement is completed, it cannot be unwound, even in the event of a participant’s insolvency

[Source: RBA Standard 7: Settlement Finality, 2017/18 Assessment, https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/financial-market-infrastructure/clearing-and-settlement-facilities/assessments/2017-2018/asx-securities-settlement-facilities/standard-07.html]

4.4 CSD / Settlement Infrastructure

System Function Details
CHESS (Clearing House Electronic Subregister System) CSD and settlement system for cash equities Launched 1996; handles settlement, subregistry, and corporate actions for all ASX-listed securities; operates as ASX Settlement Pty Limited
ASX Settlement Settlement facility licensee Licensed under the Corporations Act 2001; holds a CS facility licence
Austraclear CSD for debt securities and depository for government bonds Handles settlement and registry for fixed income instruments; uses RTGS via RITS
Cash Settlement Central bank money (via RITS/ESAs at RBA) Both CHESS and Austraclear settle the cash leg through ESAs at the Reserve Bank of Australia – settlement in central bank money, providing the highest level of safety

CHESS as Subregister: A unique feature of the Australian market is that CHESS acts as an electronic subregister for securities holdings. Rather than having a separate share registry system, CHESS maintains a subregister of all holdings that have been “CHESS-sponsored” (i.e., held in the CHESS system under a HIN – Holder Identification Number). This differs from most other markets where the CSD/depository function is separate from the share registry.

[Source: ASX Clearing and Settlement Services, https://www.asx.com.au/markets/clearing-and-settlement-services]

Linked CSD/ICSD Type of Link Products
NZX / NZ Clearing Limited Cross-border clearing link NZX-traded products cleared by ASX Clear (Futures)

ASX does not maintain extensive CSD linkages with international CSDs/ICSDs such as Euroclear or Clearstream. Australian securities are primarily held domestically within CHESS. International investors typically access the Australian market through custodian banks or nominee arrangements rather than direct CSD links.

4.6 Securities Lending & Borrowing

  • Facility available: Yes, through ASX Collateral
  • ASX Collateral provides:
    • Free of Payment (FoP) mechanism for collateralisation and maintenance of various exposure types, including securities lending and borrowing, CSA margining, and CCP margining with ASX Clear (Futures)
    • Delivery versus Delivery (DvD) mechanism for principal settlement of securities-versus-securities transactions, including securities lending and borrowing
  • The securities lending market in Australia is primarily an OTC bilateral market between institutional participants, facilitated by custodian banks and prime brokers
  • ASX’s role is primarily in providing the settlement infrastructure for securities lending transactions rather than operating a centralised lending facility

CHESS Replacement Project (Major)

The CHESS replacement is one of the most significant and troubled technology projects in global exchange history:

Phase 1 – DLT/Blockchain Approach (2015-2022): - 2015: ASX selected Digital Asset Holdings (DA) to explore DLT for CHESS replacement - 2017: ASX formally announced CHESS replacement using DA’s distributed ledger technology - 2018-2022: Repeated delays – go-live date pushed from 2020 to 2021, 2022, then 2023 - November 2022: ASX abandoned the DLT project after an independent Accenture review found software was only 63% complete with uncertain timeline and overly complex design - Financial impact: ~AUD 250 million write-off - August 2024: ASIC commenced Federal Court proceedings against ASX, alleging misleading statements about the DLT project’s progress (particularly the February 2022 claim that the project was “on-track” and “progressing well”)

Phase 2 – Conventional Technology with TCS (2023-present): - November 2023: ASX selected Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) as new implementation partner - Architecture: Modular, cloud-based platform using conventional technology - Release 1 (Clearing): Targeted go-live 20 April 2026; estimated cost AUD 105-125 million; replaces clearing component of CHESS; introduces more efficient netting process and FIX messaging for trade registrations - Release 2 (Settlement and Subregister): Targeted for 2029; estimated cost AUD 270-320 million; replaces remaining CHESS functionality - March 2026: ASX released the CHESS Release 1 Cutover Runbook and progress updates; appears on track

ClearStar Program

The ClearStar Program is a portfolio of projects to modernise and replace the technology underpinning ASX’s derivatives clearing services over a six-year period: - OTC Clearing (Calypso Upgrade): Upgraded from Calypso v13 to v18; delivered 26 May 2025 - Exchange-Traded Derivatives Futures Clearing: Platform upgrade planned as part of the program - Objective: Improve operational efficiency, enhance risk management capabilities, modernise clearing infrastructure

6. Regulatory Framework

6.1 Supervising Authority

Authority Role
ASIC (Australian Securities and Investments Commission) Primary regulator – market supervision, licensing of market operators and participants, enforcement of market integrity rules, consumer protection; commenced formal Inquiry into ASX Group in June 2025
RBA (Reserve Bank of Australia) Oversees clearing and settlement facility licensees under the Financial Stability Standards (FSS); assesses ASX Clear, ASX Clear (Futures), ASX Settlement, and Austraclear annually; focuses on systemic risk and financial stability
CFR (Council of Financial Regulators) Coordinating body comprising RBA, ASIC, APRA, and Treasury; oversees financial system stability
APRA (Australian Prudential Regulation Authority) Regulates ADIs that are clearing participants; sets capital requirements for bank participants’ CCP exposures

6.2 Key Regulations

  • Corporations Act 2001 (Cth): Part 7.3 – clearing and settlement facility licences; Part 7.3A – CS facility rules
  • Financial Stability Standards (FSS): Set by the RBA under Section 827D of the Corporations Act; based on CPMI-IOSCO PFMI principles
  • Payment Systems and Netting Act 1998: Provides settlement finality protections
  • ASIC CS Services Rules 2025: New regulatory framework for clearing and settlement service pricing; implements revenue-capping methodology reflecting ASX’s monopoly position in cash equity clearing and settlement
  • ASX Clear Operating Rules / ASX Clear (Futures) Operating Rules: Govern clearing participation, margining, default management
  • ASX Recovery Rules: Govern recovery tools including assessment powers, haircutting, and contract termination

7. Interoperability

7.1 CCP Interoperability Arrangements

ASX Clear and ASX Clear (Futures) do not participate in any CCP interoperability arrangements. This is a direct consequence of ASX’s vertically integrated monopoly model for cash equity clearing – there is no competing CCP for Australian cash equities, so interoperability has not been required.

The Australian government has periodically considered introducing competition in clearing (e.g., the 2015 Review of Competition in Clearing Australian Cash Equities by Treasury), but no competing CCP has been authorised.

7.2 Cross-border Arrangements

Arrangement Details
ESMA Recognition Both CCPs recognised as third-country CCPs under EU EMIR (effective 27 April 2015) – allows EU-based participants to use ASX CCPs with concessional capital treatment
APRA QCCP Recognition Both CCPs confirmed as Qualifying CCPs under APS 112 – allows Australian ADIs to apply concessional risk weightings
NZX Clearing ASX Clear (Futures) provides clearing services for NZX-traded products under a cross-border arrangement

Comparison with KRX: KRX also does not participate in CCP interoperability arrangements. Both ASX and KRX operate as monopoly CCPs in their respective domestic markets, reflecting the concentrated market structure of their regions.
