1. Overview & Profile

Item Details

Key Historical Milestones

  • 1973: OCC founded as a clearing house for listed equity options on five exchanges, coinciding with the launch of the Chicago Board Options Exchange (CBOE).
  • 1975: OCC began clearing put options (previously only calls were listed).
  • 1985: OCC began clearing index options.
  • 1993: OCC launched its Stock Loan/Hedge Program for securities lending transactions.
  • 2000: ENCORE clearing platform launched, replacing legacy systems.
  • 2000: Monthly volume record of 75.3 million contracts set in October.
  • 2006: STANS (System for Theoretical Analysis and Numerical Simulations) margin methodology implemented, replacing TIMS (Theoretical Intermarket Margin System).
  • 2011 (August): Record monthly volume of 550 million contracts; S&P lowered OCC credit rating to AA+ from AAA.
  • 2012 (July): Designated as SIFMU by FSOC under Dodd-Frank Title VIII.
  • 2015: OCC submitted Capital Plan to SEC for enhanced capitalization; subsequently challenged by non-shareholder exchanges.
  • 2019 (February): SEC disapproved OCC’s Capital Plan on procedural and substantive grounds, citing insufficient information and governance concerns.
  • 2019 (September): SEC and CFTC charged OCC with failing to establish and maintain adequate risk management policies.
  • 2019–2021: Clearing Fund underfunding incident — OCC’s Clearing Fund was underfunded by $200 million to $588 million at various times between October 2019 and May 2021 due to errors in the Liquidation Cost Model.
  • 2022 (October): SEC issued no-objection notice for OCC’s Renaissance Initiative cloud infrastructure proposal.
  • 2023 (February): OCC settled with SEC ($17 million penalty) and CFTC ($5 million penalty) over Clearing Fund underfunding and operational risk management failures.
  • 2024 (May 28): Transition to T+1 settlement cycle for underlying equity securities.
  • 2024: Record-breaking annual volume — 12.2 billion options contracts cleared (+10.6% YoY), 60.1 million futures contracts (+7.7% YoY).
  • 2025 (April 4): Daily volume record of 102.6 million contracts, the first time exceeding 100 million in a single day.
  • 2025 (October 10): New daily volume record of 110 million option contracts cleared.

Sources: Wikipedia — Options Clearing Corporation; OCC 2024 Volume Release; SEC Charges OCC (2023); CFTC Penalty Order (2023); SEC Capital Plan Disapproval (2019)

2. Clearing Model

2.1 CCP Services

OCC serves as the sole CCP for standardized equity options listed on U.S. national securities exchanges. It also clears futures, securities lending transactions, and certain OTC products.

Product Class Description Volume / Scale (2024)
Equity Options Puts and calls on individual equities, ETFs Majority of 12.2 billion total
Index Options Options on equity indexes (e.g., SPX, NDX, VIX) Included in total; SPX alone ~2+ billion
Futures Futures on CBOE Futures Exchange (VIX futures, etc.) 60.1 million contracts
Securities Lending Stock Loan/Hedge Program and Market Loan Program ~$139 billion average daily loan balance (June 2023 peak); ~9,800 transactions/day
Single-Stock Futures Futures on individual equities Minimal volume (largely dormant)

Sources: OCC 2024 Volume Release; OCC Stock Loan Programs; FOW — OCC Securities Lending 30th Anniversary

2.2 Short-Dated and 0DTE Options

Short-dated options (contracts with less than one week to expiration) represent a rapidly growing segment of OCC’s cleared volume:

  • Short-dated options represented approximately 40% of total volume in 2024
  • Zero-day-to-expiry (0DTE) options represented approximately 20% of total volume in 2024
  • In SPX options specifically, 0DTE accounted for 48% of volume in Q1 2024 (up from 11% five years prior)
  • This rapid growth creates significant intraday risk management challenges, as 0DTE positions are currently uncollateralized intraday under traditional end-of-day margining

Sources: OCC 2024 Volume Release; SEC Filing SR-OCC-2024-010

2.3 Novation Process

OCC uses an open offer model for novation. When an options trade is executed on a participant exchange and reported to OCC, OCC interposes itself as the CCP:

  • OCC becomes the buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer
  • This novation occurs at the point of trade acceptance
  • OCC guarantees the performance of every cleared contract
  • Positions are maintained at the clearing member account level

2.4 Netting

OCC performs multilateral netting:

  • Position Netting: Long and short positions in the same series are netted at the account level
  • Premium Netting: Daily premiums receivable and payable are netted into a single payment per clearing member
  • Exercise/Assignment Netting: Delivery obligations from exercise and assignment are netted with NSCC through the “Accord” agreement

2.5 Clearing Membership

Category Details

Minimum Net Capital $10,000,000

2.6 Securities Lending Programs

OCC operates two distinct securities lending programs, established in 1993:

Program Model Description
Hedge Program Bilateral Supports transactions executed bilaterally between counterparties; OCC acts as CCP guarantor
Market Loan Program Exchange-based Supports transactions originating through participating loan markets (e.g., AQS)
  • Since 2012, stock loan clearing volumes have grown at a CAGR of 16%, with open interest growing at 22%
  • OCC is the world’s largest U.S. equities lending CCP
  • OCC selected Axoni (DLT platform) to modernize securities lending infrastructure

Sources: OCC Stock Loan Programs; Axoni — OCC Modernization

3. Risk Management

3.1 STANS Margin Methodology

OCC’s System for Theoretical Analysis and Numerical Simulations (STANS) is the primary margin methodology, implemented in August 2006 to replace TIMS (Theoretical Intermarket Margin System). OCC was the first derivatives clearinghouse in the world to use a large-scale Monte Carlo-based risk management methodology.

STANS Technical Details

Parameter Details
Methodology Monte Carlo simulation — full portfolio margining
Risk Measure 99% Expected Shortfall (ES) — average of all losses beyond the 99% VaR point
Enhanced Measure 99.5% ES for the two single-factor sub-portfolios with greatest exposure; 99% ES for residual portfolio
Holding Period 2-day liquidation horizon (one day longer than U.S. competitors like CME)
Simulation Paths Large-scale Monte Carlo simulations across all risk factors
Risk Factors Equity prices, implied volatilities (by strike/maturity), interest rates, dividend yields, correlation structures
Pricing Models Sophisticated options pricing models for all listed derivatives
Margin Components Base component (ES) + Stress test add-on component
Stress Add-On Considers ES increases from especially large market moves, perfect/zero correlations replacing estimated correlations, and extreme adverse idiosyncratic movements
Calculation Frequency Daily (end-of-day), with intraday monitoring

STANS vs. SPAN Comparison

Feature OCC STANS CME SPAN
Methodology Monte Carlo simulation Scenario-based (16 risk scenarios)
Risk Measure Expected Shortfall (99%) Worst-case loss across scenarios
Portfolio Approach Full portfolio margining with correlations Product-group scanning with inter-commodity spreads
Holding Period 2 days 1 day (typically)
Non-linearity Fully captured through simulation Approximated via scanning range
Correlation Treatment Dynamically estimated from historical data Fixed inter-commodity spread credits
Computational Intensity High (Monte Carlo) Lower (parametric scenarios)
Historical Context Introduced 2006 Introduced 1988

Sources: OCC — Margin Methodology; Risk.net — Clearing House of the Year: OCC; SEC STANS Approval Order (2006)

3.2 Margin Call Schedule

  • End-of-Day Margin: Calculated daily after market close using STANS; margin calls issued overnight for next-morning settlement
  • Intraday Monitoring: OCC monitors positions and market movements intraday and may issue intraday margin calls
  • Intraday Risk Charge (proposed/approved 2025): New margin add-on to address uncollateralized intraday exposure, particularly from 0DTE options and overnight trading activity

Sources: SEC Approval of Intraday Risk Charge (April 2025)

3.3 Default Waterfall

OCC’s default waterfall describes the order in which financial resources are used to address losses arising from a clearing member default:

Layer Resource Approximate Size
1 Defaulting member’s margin deposits Varies by member (total margin pool ~$180–232 billion)
2 Defaulting member’s Clearing Fund contribution Proportional share of ~$19.5 billion total
3 OCC’s Skin-in-the-Game (SITG) — OCC’s own capital contribution Persistent minimum of $69 million (capital) / ~$76 million (including deferred compensation); at least 25% of target regulatory capital
4 Non-defaulting members’ Clearing Fund contributions (mutualized loss sharing) Remainder of ~$19.5 billion Clearing Fund
5 Assessments on non-defaulting members (powers of assessment) Up to the amount of each member’s required Clearing Fund contribution (effectively doubling the mutualized pool)
6 Recovery tools (VMGH, partial tear-up, etc.) See Section 3.5

Cover 2 Standard: OCC sizes its financial resources (margin + Clearing Fund) to cover the default of its two largest clearing members and their affiliates under extreme but plausible market conditions. This is consistent with CPMI-IOSCO PFMI Principle 4 for systemically important CCPs.

Sources: OCC — Default Rules & Procedures; SEC Filing on Default Waterfall Amendments (2024); Risk.net — OCC Cover 2

3.4 Stress Testing & Back-Testing

Stress Testing: - OCC performs daily stress testing of its financial resources - Two categories of scenarios: - Sizing Scenarios: Assess whether collected resources are adequate and inform the size of OCC’s financial resources - Sufficiency Scenarios: Measure potential exposures to determine whether additional margin or Clearing Fund calls are needed - Scenarios include historical (e.g., 2008 financial crisis rally/decline variations), hypothetical, and reverse stress tests - In 2024, OCC recategorized certain 2008-based Informational Scenarios as Sufficiency Scenarios after finding they consistently generated higher exposures - In January 2025, SEC approved enhancements to STANS and Comprehensive Stress Testing (CST) methodology to better capture risks from short-dated options

Back-Testing: - OCC conducts daily back-testing of its margin model - In September 2024, SEC approved OCC’s updated backtesting framework and establishment of a Resource Backtesting Margin Charge - Back-testing compares actual portfolio P&L against margin requirements to validate model coverage

Sources: SEC Approval of Stress Testing Amendments (July 2024); SEC Approval of STANS/CST Enhancements (January 2025); SEC Approval of Backtesting Framework (September 2024)

3.5 Recovery & Resolution Tools

OCC maintains a Recovery and Orderly Wind-Down (RWD) Plan that describes tools for managing losses and liquidity shortfalls beyond routine risk management:

Tool Description
Assessments OCC may assess non-defaulting clearing members up to their required Clearing Fund contribution amount
Variation Margin Gains Haircutting (VMGH) OCC can allocate losses to clearing participants that experience mark-to-market gains on their positions; provides incentive for members to participate in default management
Voluntary Partial Tear-Up Members may voluntarily extinguish contracts to help OCC return to a matched book
Mandatory Partial Tear-Up OCC authority to extinguish remaining contracts of a defaulted clearing member at OCC’s marking price; used after other default management tools have been exhausted
Orderly Wind-Down Planned cessation of critical services with transfer of positions to alternative clearing arrangements

The RWD Plan was most recently amended and approved by the SEC in August 2025.

Sources: OCC Recovery and Orderly Wind-Down Plan; SEC Approval of RWD Plan Modifications (August 2025); FSB OCC Questionnaire

4. Settlement

4.1 Settlement Cycle

Settlement Type Cycle Details
Premium Settlement T+1 Payment of options premium is settled the next business day after trade date
Exercise/Assignment (Equity Options) T+1 Following the transition on May 28, 2024; previously T+2
Exercise/Assignment (Index Options) Cash settlement on T+1 Cash-settled index options pay/receive cash difference
Futures Daily mark-to-market Variation margin settled daily; final settlement at expiration
Stock Loan Same-day or T+1 Depending on program and initiation time

4.2 DVP and Settlement Finality

  • Exercise/Assignment of Equity Options: When equity options are exercised/assigned, the resulting stock delivery obligation is processed through the Accord — an agreement between OCC and NSCC (National Securities Clearing Corporation)
  • Under the Accord, NSCC accepts “E&A/Delivery Transactions” resulting from OCC exercise and assignment for clearing and settlement through NSCC’s Continuous Net Settlement (CNS) system
  • Settlement of underlying shares occurs at the Depository Trust Company (DTC) on a DVP (Delivery versus Payment) basis
  • Settlement is considered final and irrevocable once completed through DTC’s book-entry system

4.3 Premium Settlement Process

  1. At end of each trading day, OCC calculates net premium obligations for each clearing member
  2. Net premiums receivable and payable are aggregated across all cleared trades
  3. Settlement occurs on T+1 through Fedwire or commercial bank payment systems
  4. OCC may call for intraday premium settlement during periods of high volatility

4.4 Exercise and Assignment Process

  1. Exercise Notice: Option holder’s clearing member submits exercise notice to OCC (either by instruction or automatic exercise for in-the-money options)
  2. Assignment: OCC uses a random assignment algorithm to distribute exercise notices fairly among clearing members with short positions in the assigned series
  3. Delivery/Cash Settlement: For equity options, the resulting buy/sell obligations flow to NSCC via the Accord for settlement at DTC. For index options, cash settlement amounts are calculated and settled directly through OCC
  4. Automatic Exercise: Options expiring in-the-money by $0.01 or more are automatically exercised unless contrary instructions are received

Sources: OCC Equity Options Product Specifications; OCC T+1 Info Memo #54580; SEC — OCC/NSCC Accord Modifications (2024)

5. Technology & Infrastructure

5.1 Core Systems

System Function Status

Ovation Next-generation cloud-based clearing, settlement, risk management, and data platform In testing; external beta testing began July 2024; launch targeted for mid-2025 [unverified if completed]

5.2 ENCORE Architecture

  • Monolithic architecture — tightly coupled components making change difficult
  • Runs in traditional on-premises data centers
  • Receives trade and post-trade data from exchanges on a transaction-by-transaction basis in real time
  • Maintains clearing member positions, calculates margin and Clearing Fund requirements
  • Provides reporting to OCC staff, regulators, and clearing members

5.3 Ovation Architecture (Renaissance Initiative)

OCC’s Renaissance Initiative is a multi-year technology modernization program to replace ENCORE with the Ovation platform:

Feature Details
Cloud Provider Amazon Web Services (AWS)
Architecture Modular, cloud-native; three pillars — Clearing & Settlement, Data, Risk Management
Real-Time Processing Stream processing using Apache Kafka and Apache Flink
Data Processing Prices for over 2 million financial instruments per second; billions of open option positions
Disaster Recovery Isolated Kafka + Flink infrastructure in both AWS and on-premises data center
Key Benefits Enhanced resiliency, security, scalability; real-time risk measurement using current prices (vs. prior-day close)

Renaissance Timeline: - October 2022: SEC no-objection notice for cloud infrastructure proposal - 2023: Ovation development and internal testing - July 2024: External beta testing commenced (Beta 1) - October 2024: Beta 2 launched with expanded functionality - January 2025: Beta 3 — collateral management testing - Mid-2025: Planned Ovation launch (ENCORE-to-Ovation conversion in single processing cycle)

  • Regulatory Reporting: Regular reporting to SEC, CFTC, and Federal Reserve on risk metrics, financial resources, and operational performance

7. Interoperability

7.1 Cross-Margining Programs

OCC maintains cross-margining agreements that allow hedged positions cleared at separate clearinghouses to be combined into a single portfolio for margin purposes:

Program Counterpart CCP Description
OCC-CME Cross-Margin Program CME Clearing Allows qualifying clearing members to offset equity option positions (OCC) against related equity index futures positions (CME); established after 1987 Black Monday crash; second amended and restated agreement approved in 2020

Cross-margining uses OCC’s STANS methodology to calculate the combined portfolio risk, typically resulting in lower margin requirements than if margins were calculated separately at each CCP.

7.2 Cross-Guaranty and Linkages

Arrangement Details
OCC-NSCC Accord Governs the processing of physically settled equity options; NSCC accepts exercise/assignment delivery transactions for settlement through its CNS system at DTC
Exchange Linkages OCC maintains Participant Exchange Agreements with all 16+ options exchanges; the Amended and Restated Participant Exchange Agreement was approved by the SEC in July 2025

7.3 Ongoing Coordination

OCC is evaluating adjustments to existing cross-CCP agreements and exploring potential new arrangements to support aligned margin cycles and risk management, particularly in light of: - Extended trading hours initiatives - T+1 settlement transition - Growing intraday risk from 0DTE options

Sources: OCC — Cross Margin Programs; Yale SOM — Cross-Margining and Financial Stability; SEC Approval of Participant Exchange Agreement (July 2025)

9. Recent Developments

9.1 Renaissance Initiative / Technology Modernization

OCC’s multi-year Renaissance Initiative is the most significant technology transformation in its history:

  • Ovation platform built on AWS cloud with modular architecture (clearing & settlement, data, risk management pillars)
  • Transition from batch processing (ENCORE) to real-time stream processing using Apache Kafka and Flink
  • Enables intraday risk calculation using current market prices rather than prior-day closing prices
  • External industry testing began July 2024 with progressive beta releases
  • Planned conversion from ENCORE to Ovation in a single processing cycle [target: mid-2025; completion status unverified as of April 2026]
  • SEC no-objection on cloud infrastructure received October 2022

9.2 Extended Trading Hours

  • Cboe and other exchanges have expanded or proposed extended trading sessions (pre-market and post-market) for equity options
  • Cboe extended trading includes 7:30–9:25 a.m. ET and 4:00–4:15 p.m. ET sessions for select options classes
  • OCC infrastructure must support clearing and risk management across extended hours
  • Creates challenges for margin calculation timing, intraday risk exposure, and settlement cycle alignment

9.3 0DTE Options Growth Impact

The explosive growth of zero-day-to-expiry options has driven significant risk management adaptations:

  • Intraday Risk Charge: SEC approved (April 2025) a new margin add-on applied to all clearing member accounts to mitigate risks from intraday and overnight trading activity
  • STANS Enhancements: SEC approved (January 2025) changes to STANS and CST methodology to better capture short-dated options risks; average daily margin impact estimated at +0.58%
  • Volatility Adjustments: OCC proposed (October 2024) a mechanism to adjust margin parameters during periods of high volatility
  • OCC’s intraday exposure to 0DTE options was previously uncollateralized — the new Intraday Risk Charge addresses this gap

9.4 Securities Lending Expansion

  • OCC selected Axoni (distributed ledger technology platform) to modernize its securities lending infrastructure
  • Stock loan clearing volumes have grown at a 16% CAGR since 2012
  • OCC is now the world’s largest U.S. equities lending CCP
  • Programs expanded to support broader market participation and operational efficiency

9.5 2021 Capital Plan / Governance Crisis and Resolution

This episode represents a significant chapter in OCC’s history:

Timeline: 1. 2015: OCC submitted Capital Plan to SEC proposing significant capitalization increase through dividends to stockholder exchanges, funded by clearing fee increases 2. 2017: SEC initially approved the Capital Plan 3. 2017: D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals remanded the decision back to the SEC following challenge by non-shareholder exchanges (e.g., MIAX) who argued they were denied fair notice and opportunity to present views 4. 2019 (February): SEC disapproved the Capital Plan, finding: - Insufficient evidence that the Plan was consistent with the Securities Exchange Act - Governance concerns — conflicts of interest between OCC management/board and stockholder exchanges - Failure to notify non-shareholder exchanges of the Plan’s competitive significance - Questions about whether fee-funded dividends to owner-exchanges were appropriate for a market utility 5. 2019 (September): SEC and CFTC separately charged OCC with risk management policy failures unrelated to the Capital Plan but occurring during the same period 6. 2019–2021: Errors in OCC’s Liquidation Cost Model led to Clearing Fund underfunding of $200M–$588M at various times 7. 2023 (February): OCC settled with SEC ($17M) and CFTC ($5M); total penalties of $22M 8. Post-2023: OCC implemented remedial measures including enhanced governance, improved internal controls, new Capital Management Policy with regulatory capital target of $303 million, and persistent minimum SITG of $69 million

Lessons: - Highlighted tensions in OCC’s ownership model — stockholder exchanges as both owners and competitors of non-shareholder exchanges - Led to enhanced regulatory scrutiny of OCC governance - Prompted OCC to adopt more transparent capital management and fee-setting processes - Reinforced the importance of robust internal controls at SIFMUs

9.6 Clearing Fund Methodology Reform (2025)

  • In October 2025, OCC filed a proposed rule change to reform its Clearing Fund allocation methodology to better align contributions with the stress risk each clearing member poses to OCC
  • In December 2025, SEC granted accelerated approval of the reformed methodology
  • The new methodology ensures that stress-based risk is “fairly allotted to market participants that expose OCC to such stress risk”
