Key Historical Milestones

  • 1852: Association of Brokers formed by Toronto businessmen on July 26, considered the precursor to the Toronto Stock Exchange
  • 1861: Twenty-four brokers gather at the Masonic Hall on October 25 to formally create the Toronto Stock Exchange
  • 1878: TSX is incorporated by an Act of the Ontario Legislature
  • 1934: TSX merges with the Standard Stock and Mining Exchange, consolidating Toronto’s equity trading
  • 1977: Introduction of CATS (Computer Assisted Trading System), one of the world’s first automated trading systems
  • 1997: TSX becomes a for-profit, demutualised exchange
  • 1999: TSX assumes responsibility for listing and trading all senior equities in Canada as part of market restructuring; junior equities move to the Canadian Venture Exchange (later TSX Venture)
  • 2000: TSX ceases floor trading; becomes fully electronic
  • 2001: TSX Group Inc. formed as the parent company
  • 2002: TSX Group acquires TSX Venture Exchange
  • 2005: TSX Group goes public via IPO on its own exchange (TSX: X)
  • 2008: TSX Group acquires Montreal Exchange (MX); renamed to TMX Group Inc. on May 1
  • 2012: Maple Group Acquisition Corporation (consortium of Canadian banks and pension funds) acquires majority stake in TMX Group for C$3.8 billion
  • 2013â2014: TMX Quantum XA trading platform rolled out across TMX Select, TSX, and TSXV
  • 2023: CIRO (Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization) formed from merger of IIROC and MFDA (effective January 1, 2023; renamed CIRO on June 1, 2023)
  • 2024: T+1 settlement implemented in Canada (May 27, 2024)
  • 2025: TMX Group reports record revenue of C$1.72 billion (17.61% increase year-over-year); post-trade modernization completed via CDS subsidiary

2.4 Lot Sizes (Board Lots)

Board lot sizes on TSX and TSXV are determined by the security’s previous day closing price:

Price Range Board Lot Size
Securities priced >= C$1.00 100 shares
Securities priced C$0.10 â C$0.99 500 shares
Securities priced < C$0.10 1,000 shares
  • Odd lots (less than a board lot): Treated separately; odd lots only trade as “All or None” (no partial fills); Participating Organizations are prohibited from splitting larger orders into odd lots
  • Mixed lots (board lot + odd lot portion): Effectively split into two separate orders at time of entry; board lot portion trades in the CLOB; odd lot portion treated as an odd lot order

2.5 Market Maker / Liquidity Provider Programs

Responsible Traders (RT) / Market Makers: - Every TSX-listed security (except debentures/notes) is assigned a designated Market Maker firm and Registered Trader (RT) - RTs have an optional “participation” feature enabling them to trade against a fraction of incoming orders with priority of execution in their assigned securities - Market Makers must maintain sufficient expertise, technology, and firm capital - Must maintain a minimum number of security assignments with a balanced ratio of highly liquid and less liquid securities - Must ensure trading is reasonable, consistent with just and equitable principles, and not detrimental to market integrity

Minimum Guaranteed Fill (MGF): - Market Makers provide a Minimum Guaranteed Fill obligation for small orders - For securities with a board lot of 100, the minimum MGF is 199 shares [Source: TSX Order Types and Functionality Guide] - Odd lot orders and small board lot orders benefit from MGF liquidity provided by RTs

TSX Alpha Exchange: - Uses a different priority model (size-time priority) to incentivize larger displayed orders - Does not have traditional RT/market maker assignments in the same structure as TSX

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Auction Mechanisms

Opening Auction (Market on Open / MOO): - Conducted at 9:30 AM ET as a single-price call auction - During the Pre-Open session (7:00 AM â 9:30 AM), the Calculated Opening Price (COP) is continuously broadcast and updated as orders are entered/modified - COP determination logic (in priority order): 1. Price at which the most shares may be executed 2. If tied, the price that leaves the least imbalance 3. If still tied, the price closest to the previous trading day’s closing price - When trades execute in the opening auction, the COP sets the last sale price - If no trades execute at the opening, the previous day’s closing price sets the opening price - Eligible order types: Market on Open (MOO), Limit on Open (LOO), and regular limit orders carried forward

Closing Auction (Market on Close / MOC): - Electronic call market that establishes the official closing price for eligible TSX-listed securities - MOC imbalance information disseminated beginning at 3:40 PM ET - At 4:00 PM ET, the Calculated Closing Price (CCP) is determined by combining the MOC Book orders with the Central Limit Order Book (CLOB) orders - CCP uses similar logic to the COP: maximum volume, minimum imbalance, closest to last sale - Price Movement Extension (PME): If the CCP would violate the PME range (greater of 5 trading increments or PME percentage from the VWAP of the last 20 minutes and the last board-lot sale price), a PME period is initiated between 4:00 PM and 4:10 PM to allow additional order entry and price discovery - MOC facility provides equal access in setting the closing price and reduces closing price volatility [Source: TSX MOC FAQ]

Volatility Auctions: - When a Single Stock Circuit Breaker (SSCB) is triggered, a 5-minute trading halt occurs across all Canadian marketplaces - Re-opening after a halt follows an auction-like process similar to the opening mechanism

3.2 Continuous Trading

  • Order-driven model: Fully electronic Central Limit Order Book (CLOB) with price-time priority
  • Priority rules:
    1. Price priority: Best-priced orders execute first
    2. Time priority: At the same price level, earlier orders execute first (FIFO)
    3. Broker preferencing: TSX supports intentional crosses and broker preferencing under certain conditions as defined by UMIR
  • RT participation: Registered Traders may receive a participation entitlement allowing them to trade against a fraction of incoming orders with execution priority in their assigned securities
  • Dark interaction: Dark orders (Dark Limit, Dark Pegged) are fully integrated within the order book but not displayed; they interact with incoming orders after all displayed volume at the same or better price

3.3 Price Discovery

  • Pre-open: COP broadcast continuously from 7:00 AM â 9:30 AM ET, providing indicative opening price and imbalance data
  • MOC imbalance: Imbalance information published from 3:40 PM ET; PME mechanism ensures closing price reflects genuine supply/demand
  • RT role: Registered Traders maintain continuous two-sided markets in their assigned securities, supporting orderly price discovery
  • Bypass orders: Orders with a bypass flag can avoid interaction with non-displayed volume (including undisclosed iceberg volume, dark orders, and RT participation), enabling transparent price discovery for participants who prefer displayed liquidity only

4.4 Order Modification / Cancellation

  • Orders can be modified or cancelled electronically via FIX or STAMP protocols at any time before execution
  • MOC/LOC restrictions: MOC and LOC orders have entry cutoff times and cancellation restrictions during the closing period to protect auction integrity
  • Cancel on Disconnect (COD): Mandatory feature that automatically cancels open orders if the trading session disconnects, reducing risk of unmanaged exposure; must be configured and submitted to a TMX Account Manager by each Participating Organization
  • Odd lot restrictions: Participating Organizations cannot split larger orders into odd lots or enter multiple odd lots from a single account on a given security on a given day

7. Connectivity & Access

7.1 Membership / Participation

  • Participating Organization (PO): Required to directly access TSX; must be a registered investment dealer and CIRO member
  • TSXV Member: Required for direct TSXV access; similar requirements to PO status
  • TSX Alpha Member: Required for direct TSX Alpha access
  • Direct Market Access (DMA): Non-member firms can access TSX/TSXV/TSXA through a sponsoring Participating Organization; DMA sessions require Cancel on Disconnect (COD) configuration
  • Sponsored access: Subject to pre-trade risk controls; the sponsoring PO retains responsibility for the DMA client’s activity

7.2 Connection Methods

  • FIX 4.3 Gateway: Standard FIX protocol-based order entry; widely compatible with trading systems globally
  • STAMP Protocol: Proprietary TMX order entry protocol; may offer performance advantages for integrated systems
  • TMX QuantumFeed (Market Data): Binary multicast feeds via XMT protocol for low-latency market data consumption
  • Certification requirement: All new customers must complete certification in the TMX testing environment before production access; existing customers may need re-certification prior to scheduled upgrades [Source: TMX Market Access page]

    • Available to market participants as well as technology/network service providers to market participants
  • Connectivity setup: Clients request TMX Service Request Form from the Technical Help Desk; completed forms submitted to establish connectivity
  • Third-party co-location: Equinix TR1/TR2 facilities in Toronto also provide proximity hosting with connectivity to TMX [Source: Options/Business Wire]

8. Risk Controls & Circuit Breakers

8.1 Single Stock Circuit Breaker (SSCB)

Time Period Trigger Threshold Action
9:30 AM â 9:50 AM (first 20 minutes) 20% price move in a 5-minute period (and >= 40 trading increments) 5-minute trading halt across all Canadian marketplaces
9:50 AM â 3:30 PM (core session) 10% price move 5-minute trading halt across all Canadian marketplaces
All hours 20% price move in a 5-minute period (and >= 40 trading increments) 5-minute trading halt across all Canadian marketplaces
  • SSCB applies to both upward and downward price movements
  • Administered by CIRO (Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization)
  • When triggered, the halt applies across ALL Canadian equity marketplaces simultaneously, not just the venue where the trigger occurred
  • After the halt period, trading resumes via an auction-like re-opening process [Source: CIRO Guidance on Single-Stock Circuit Breakers]

8.2 Market-Wide Circuit Breaker (MWCB)

Level Trigger Halt Duration
Level 1 7% decline in S&P/TSX Composite Index (or S&P 500 if U.S. markets open) 15-minute halt
Level 2 13% decline 15-minute halt
Level 3 20% decline Trading halted for remainder of the day
  • When U.S. markets are open, Canadian MWCBs are harmonized with U.S. triggers (based on S&P 500 declines)
  • When U.S. markets are not open, the S&P/TSX Composite Index is used to determine MWCB thresholds
  • Circuit breaker levels are recalculated daily based on the prior day’s closing price [Source: CIRO Market Volatility guidance, RBC]

8.3 Pre-Trade Risk Controls

  • Cancel on Disconnect (COD): Mandatory feature; automatically cancels all open orders if the trading session disconnects
  • Pre-Trade Risk Management Solution: TMX offers a comprehensive risk management system enabling participants to apply risk checks, trading limits, and controls to their order flow; provides real-time monitoring of committed capital, open order exposure, and intraday market risk across all major Canadian equity venues [Source: Markets Media]
  • Price reasonability controls: TMX systems validate incoming order prices against control parameters; orders significantly outside reasonable price ranges are rejected
  • Montreal Exchange controls (MX):
    • “X” limit: Price filter based on calculated control price; orders above/below the limit are automatically rejected
    • “Y” limit: Narrower price limit validating potential execution price of the next executable order
    • Maximum volume thresholds: Validate incoming order sizes [Source: MX Exchange Market Thresholds and Controls]
  • Short sale regulations: Subject to UMIR short sale rules; CIRO monitors short selling activity across Canadian marketplaces
  • Erroneous trade policy: TMX can cancel clearly erroneous trades under defined circumstances

8.4 Regulatory Halts

  • Material news halt: TSX can halt trading in individual securities pending dissemination of material information
  • CIRO-coordinated halts: CIRO can coordinate halts across all Canadian marketplaces
  • SSCB halt: Automatic 5-minute halt triggered by circuit breaker thresholds (see Section 8.1)
  • MWCB halt: Market-wide trading suspension (see Section 8.2)

9. Regulatory Framework

9.1 Regulatory Structure

Canada’s securities regulation is unique in that it operates under a provincial/territorial model rather than a single national regulator:

Regulator Role
Ontario Securities Commission (OSC) Lead regulator for TMX Group and TSX; recognizes TSX, TSXV, TSX Alpha, and MX as exchanges
Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA) Council of provincial/territorial securities regulators; coordinates and harmonizes regulation across Canada
Provincial/Territorial Securities Commissions Each province/territory has its own securities regulator (e.g., BCSC, ASC, AMF in Quebec); all participate in the CSA framework
Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) National self-regulatory organization (SRO); oversees investment dealers, mutual fund dealers, and market integrity; formed January 1, 2023 from merger of IIROC and MFDA; renamed CIRO on June 1, 2023

9.2 CIRO Oversight (formerly IIROC)

  • Market integrity: CIRO administers and enforces the Universal Market Integrity Rules (UMIR), which apply to all Canadian equity marketplaces
  • Cross-marketplace surveillance: CIRO oversees ALL equity market activity across Canadian marketplaces, including exchanges and alternative trading systems (ATS)
  • Cross-asset surveillance: By integrating equity, debt, and MX-listed derivatives data, CIRO can detect potential cross-market manipulation
  • Enforcement: When evidence of rule violations is found, CIRO refers cases to its Enforcement team or to provincial/territorial securities regulators for further investigation and possible disciplinary action
  • Insider trading: Potential insider trading matters are referred to provincial/territorial securities regulators
  • Registration delegation: Effective April 1, 2025, the OSC delegates the registration function for investment dealers, mutual fund dealers, and futures commission merchants to CIRO, though the OSC retains concurrent authority under an enhanced risk-based oversight framework [Source: OSC, CIRO]

9.3 TMX Self-Regulatory Functions

  • TMX Group exchanges operate under recognition orders issued by provincial securities regulators
  • TSX and TSXV maintain their own rule books (TSX Rule Book effective January 13, 2026; TSXV Policy Manual) governing listing standards, trading rules, and issuer obligations
  • Market regulation (trade surveillance and enforcement) is outsourced to CIRO under agreements with the marketplaces
  • TMX provides market surveillance data feeds to CIRO for monitoring purposes

9.4 Key Regulatory Instruments

  • UMIR (Universal Market Integrity Rules): National rules governing trading conduct on all Canadian equity marketplaces; includes provisions on manipulative trading, short selling, best execution, and market making
  • National Instrument 21-101 (Marketplace Operation): Governs the operation of exchanges and ATS in Canada
  • National Instrument 23-101 (Trading Rules): Establishes best execution obligations and order protection rules
  • TSX Rule Book: Exchange-specific rules governing listing, trading, and participation on TSX

11. Recent Developments

11.1 CIRO Formation (2023)

  • On January 1, 2023, the Investment Industry Regulatory Organization of Canada (IIROC) and the Mutual Fund Dealers Association of Canada (MFDA) amalgamated to form the New Self-Regulatory Organization of Canada
  • Renamed to the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) on June 1, 2023
  • Consolidates oversight of investment dealers, mutual fund dealers, and marketplace integrity under a single SRO
  • Effective April 1, 2025, CIRO takes on delegated registration functions from the OSC [Source: OSC, CIRO]

11.2 T+1 Settlement (2024)

  • Canada transitioned to T+1 (trade date plus one business day) settlement on May 27, 2024, in coordination with the United States and Mexico
  • Reduces counterparty and systemic risk by shortening the settlement cycle from T+2
  • CDS (TMX subsidiary) was central to the implementation for Canadian markets [Source: CDS T+1 initiative page]

11.3 Post-Trade Modernization (2025)

  • In April 2025, CDS implemented major upgrades to its foundational clearing technology
  • Replaced legacy clearing and settlement systems as well as depository and entitlement payment systems
  • New platform powered by TCS BaNCS for Market Infrastructure
  • Supports recent initiatives including the Canadian Collateral Management Service launched in 2024 [Source: TMX Group Press Release]

11.4 Record Financial Performance (2025)

  • TMX Group reported revenue of C$1.72 billion in 2025, an increase of 17.61% compared to C$1.46 billion in 2024
  • CEO John McKenzie described 2025 as TMX Group’s “best year ever”
  • TSX-listed issuers set an all-time record for market capitalization (exceeding C$5.8 trillion by August 2025) [Source: TMX Group, StockTitan]

11.5 Mining Sector Strength

  • As of December 31, 2025, TMX exchanges hosted 1,073 mining issuers with a combined market cap above C$1.1 trillion
  • Mining issuers completed 1,429 financings raising C$16 billion in equity capital in 2025
  • TSX and TSXV represent approximately 40% of the world’s public mining companies
  • The 2026 TSX Venture 50 featured a dominant cohort of resource companies with an average share price appreciation of 431% [Source: TMX Group PDAC 2026 announcement, StockTitan]

11.6 Trading Increment Harmonization

  • CIRO has proposed amendments to UMIR to harmonize trading increments for U.S.-interlisted securities with the U.S. market, ensuring continued fair competition between Canadian and U.S. marketplaces
  • TMX Group publicly supports this harmonization effort [Source: TMX comment letter to CSA/CIRO, December 2023]
