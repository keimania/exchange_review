Key Historical Milestones

  • 1954: Informal share trading begins in Saudi Arabia
  • 1984: Saudi Arabian Monetary Agency (SAMA) begins regulating the stock market; Saudi Share Registration Company (SSRC) established
  • 2001: Launch of Tadawul electronic trading, clearing, and settlement system (October 16), replacing manual processes
  • 2003: Capital Market Authority (CMA) established under the Capital Market Law (Royal Decree No. M/30)
  • 2007: Saudi Exchange (Tadawul) formally incorporated as a joint stock company on March 19, authorized as the sole securities exchange in Saudi Arabia
  • 2008: Tadawul begins using Nasdaq OMX’s SAXESS trading platform
  • 2015: Qualified Foreign Investor (QFI) program launched, opening direct market access to institutional foreign investors for the first time; Saudi Tadawul Group IPO strategy initiated
  • 2015: X-Stream INET trading platform launched (September 13), replacing SAXESS â first in the Middle East to adopt Nasdaq’s fastest trading engine
  • 2017: Nomu â Parallel Market launched (February 26) for SME listings with lighter requirements
  • 2018: QFI eligibility thresholds lowered; per-investor ownership cap raised from 5% to 10%
  • 2019: Saudi Aramco IPO (December 11) â raised USD 25.6 billion, the world’s largest IPO; Aramco reached ~USD 1.88 trillion market cap on debut, becoming the world’s most valuable listed company
  • 2019: MSCI Emerging Market and FTSE Russell EM index inclusion completed (phased from May-August for MSCI, March-December for FTSE Russell), catalyzing major foreign capital inflows
  • 2020: Closing Auction and Trade-at-Last session introduced; settlement cycle changed to T+2; trading hours updated
  • 2020: Volatility interruption mechanism introduced for newly listed securities (Main Market) and all Nomu securities
  • 2021: Saudi Tadawul Group restructured into holding company with four subsidiaries and listed on its own exchange (December 8); IPO 121x oversubscribed by institutions
  • 2023: Derivatives market developments including SF30 Futures Index; GCC Exchanges Committee (chaired by Tadawul) introduced 29 Unified ESG Metrics
  • 2025: Record IPO activity â 38 company listings (13 on TASI, 24 on Nomu, plus 1 ETF); tick size reform effective June 29
  • 2026: QFI regime abolished; market opened to all categories of foreign investors with barrier-free direct access from February 1, 2026

Strategic Significance â Vision 2030

Saudi Exchange is a cornerstone of Saudi Vision 2030 and the Financial Sector Development Program (FSDP). Key alignment areas include: - Diversification from oil: The Aramco IPO was designed to raise capital for non-oil economic diversification - Capital market deepening: New products (derivatives, sukuk, REITs, ETFs), market segments (Nomu), and progressive foreign investor liberalization - Global index inclusion: MSCI and FTSE Russell EM inclusion (2019) created a virtuous cycle of visibility, foreign flows, and institutional development - Private sector growth: Nomu parallel market supports SME access to capital, aligning with Vision 2030’s goal of increasing SME contribution to GDP

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Auction Mechanisms

  • Opening Auction: During the Pre-Open phase (09:30-10:00 AST), orders accumulate without matching. The system displays indicative opening prices. At 10:00, a randomized uncross occurs within a 30-second window (10:00:00-10:00:30), determining the opening price via a single-price auction that maximizes executable volume.

  • Closing Auction: The Pre-Close phase begins at 15:00 AST. Orders accumulate for 10 minutes. At 15:10, a randomized uncross within 30 seconds determines the closing price. This closing price is the official price used for index calculations, NAV computations, and settlement.

  • Trade-at-Last: After the closing auction uncross (15:10-15:20 AST), participants may execute orders only at the closing auction price. This session provides additional liquidity at the established closing price.

  • Volatility Auction (Interruption): When a security price reaches the +/-10% static price limit, continuous trading halts and a 5-minute call auction is initiated. The auction price becomes the new reference price, after which continuous trading resumes.

3.2 Continuous Trading

  • Type: Order-driven market
  • Priority: Price-time priority (best price first, then earliest entry time at the same price)
  • Session: 10:00 - 15:00 AST (5 hours)
  • Matching: Continuous automatic matching via the X-Stream INET engine

3.3 Price Discovery

  • Opening price: Determined by single-price auction algorithm maximizing tradable volume at the uncross
  • Closing price: Determined by closing auction uncross; if no closing auction trade occurs, the last traded price during continuous trading is used [unverified]
  • Reference price: Previous session’s closing auction price; serves as the baseline for daily price limits
  • Randomized uncross: Both open and close uncross times are randomized within 30 seconds to prevent manipulation and enhance price integrity

4.4 Order Modification / Cancellation

  • Orders can be modified or cancelled during Pre-Open and Continuous Trading sessions

  • During Non-Cancel (uncross) periods, cancellations are not permitted

9. Regulatory Framework

9.1 Supervising Authority

  • Capital Market Authority (CMA) â established in 2003 under the Capital Market Law (Royal Decree No. M/30, dated 2003)
  • CMA is an independent government authority reporting directly to the Prime Minister
  • Responsible for regulating and developing the Saudi capital market, protecting investors, and ensuring market fairness and transparency
  • Website: https://cma.org.sa

9.2 Key Regulations

Regulation Description
Capital Market Law (2003) Foundational legislation establishing CMA, authorizing the exchange, and setting framework for securities regulation
Rules for Foreign Investment in Securities Governed foreign access via QFI regime (2015-2026); amended January 2026 to grant barrier-free access to all foreign investors
Listing Rules Requirements for Main Market and Nomu listings; includes corporate governance, disclosure, and free float requirements
Market Conduct Regulations Prohibitions on insider trading, market manipulation, and fraud
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter-Terrorist Financing Compliance requirements for all market participants
Corporate Governance Regulations Board composition, audit committees, disclosure obligations for listed companies
ESG Disclosure Guidelines Voluntary guidelines launched by Tadawul (2021); growing adoption with companies reporting ESG performance almost tripling since launch

9.3 Market Surveillance

  • CMA conducts market surveillance to detect insider trading, market manipulation, and other violations
  • Nasdaq SMARTS surveillance technology utilized [unverified]
  • Real-time monitoring of trading patterns, unusual price and volume movements
  • CMA has enforcement powers including fines, trading suspensions, and criminal referrals

9.4 Reporting Requirements

  • Listed companies must file annual and interim financial statements
  • Material event disclosure requirements (continuous disclosure)
  • Major shareholder notifications (5% threshold)
  • ESG reporting increasingly encouraged (voluntary, with mandatory elements expected under Vision 2030 roadmap) [unverified]

11. Recent Developments

System Upgrades (Last 5 Years)

  • 2020: Introduction of closing auction mechanism and Trade-at-Last session
  • 2020: Settlement cycle updated to T+2 to align with international standards
  • 2025: Tick size band reform (effective June 29, 2025) â six-tier structure with lower minimums for sub-SAR 25 securities

New Products / Segments

  • Derivatives: SF30 Futures Index and ongoing development of equity derivatives; Saudi Exchange preparing new generation of debt and derivatives products
  • ESG: ESG Disclosure Guidelines launched (2021); GCC Unified ESG Metrics (29 metrics) introduced 2023 under Tadawul chairmanship of GCC Exchanges Committee
  • ETFs: Growing ETF market with new listings including Albilad MSCI Saudi Equity ETF (2025)
  • REITs: Expanding REIT market supporting real estate sector development

Regulatory Changes

  • February 2026: QFI regime abolished; market fully opened to all foreign investor categories with barrier-free access
  • 2025-2026: CMA introduced new Instructions for Foreign Strategic Investors’ Ownership, codifying 49% aggregate and 10% per-investor foreign ownership limits
  • 2025: Amendments to listing rules and tick sizes under FSDP roadmap

Strategic Initiatives

  • Vision 2030 / FSDP alignment: Ongoing reforms to deepen capital markets, increase market cap as percentage of GDP, and diversify the economy away from oil dependence
  • IPO pipeline: ~40 IPO applications under CMA review at end of 2025, spanning energy, healthcare, financial services, real estate, mining, and technology
  • Offshore Securities Business License: Launched to attract international intermediaries to operate in the Saudi market
  • Data as investment product: Saudi Exchange investing in data services as a standalone revenue stream
