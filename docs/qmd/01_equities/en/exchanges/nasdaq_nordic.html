2.3 Tick Sizes

Nasdaq Nordic and Baltic markets follow the MiFID II tick size regime (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/588, aka RTS 11), implemented since January 2, 2018.

  • Tick sizes are determined by a matrix of price level and liquidity band (based on Average Daily Number of Transactions, ADNT)
  • Six liquidity bands exist, ranging from fewer than 10 ADNT to 9,000+ ADNT
  • The tick size table is the same as used across all EU/EEA regulated markets (harmonized under MiFID II)
  • Nasdaq Iceland markets were excluded from the MiFID II tick size regime at initial implementation [unverified whether since adopted]
  • ETFs with equity underlyings also follow the MiFID II tick size table
  • Other products (warrants, certificates, equity rights) use exchange-specific tick size tables

2.4 Lot Sizes

  • Standard lot size: 1 share for most equities
  • Odd lot trading is supported
  • Block trade thresholds defined under MiFID II Large-in-Scale (LIS) waivers, varying by instrument liquidity

2.5 Market Maker / Liquidity Provider Programs

  • Nasdaq Nordic operates liquidity provider programs where market makers commit to maintaining continuous two-sided quotes within specified spread and size parameters
  • Registered market makers may receive reduced trading fees as incentives
  • First North Growth Market requires Certified Advisers (not market makers per se, but advisory role); some First North instruments also have liquidity providers
  • Less liquid instruments on First North Denmark have been moved to an auction-only trading model to support price discovery [unverified]

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Auction Mechanisms

  • Opening Auction: During the pre-open phase, orders accumulate. At 09:00 CET, the opening uncross determines the opening price by maximizing executable volume. Each order book uncrosses randomly within a 5-second window (09:00:00 - 09:00:05) to prevent manipulation
  • Closing Auction: At the end of continuous trading (~17:25 CET), the market enters a closing call period. The closing uncross at ~17:30 CET determines the official closing price, which is used by index providers, fund managers, and for NAV calculations
  • Scheduled Intraday Auctions: For less liquid instruments in the “Auction Only” trading segment, scheduled intraday auctions occur at set intervals (e.g., 11:00, 13:00, 15:00 CET) without continuous trading between them
  • Volatility Auctions: Triggered when a potential execution price would breach Volatility Guard thresholds (see Section 8). Continuous trading is halted and an auction call period of 60-180 seconds is initiated to allow orderly price discovery

3.2 Continuous Trading

  • Order-driven market with price/time priority
  • Continuous matching occurs between the opening and closing auctions (approximately 09:00 - 17:25 CET)
  • The INET matching engine processes incoming orders against the resting order book using strict price/time priority
  • Market makers (liquidity providers) can provide continuous quotes but the system is fundamentally order-driven, not quote-driven
  • Dark order functionality is available (hidden and midpoint orders)

3.3 Price Discovery

  • Primary price discovery occurs through the opening and closing auctions
  • The uncrossing algorithm maximizes executable volume, then minimizes surplus, then references the prior price
  • Reference prices for Volatility Guards are dynamically updated based on recent trades (Dynamic) and the day’s opening auction price (Static)
  • Trading@Closing session allows additional volume to trade at the established closing price, ensuring end-of-day liquidity

4. Order Types

4.1 Standard Order Types

Order Type Description
Limit Order Buy/sell at a specified price or better; rests on the order book if not immediately matched
Market Order Executes at the best available price; unmatched remainder is cancelled

4.2 Advanced Order Types

Order Type Description
Iceberg Order Only a visible portion (peak) is displayed; hidden reserve replenishes automatically as the peak is filled
Hidden Order Entire quantity is hidden from the visible order book; lower priority than displayed orders at the same price
Mid-Point Order Pegged to the midpoint of the best bid and offer; executes in the dark book [unverified]
Reserve Order Similar to Iceberg; configurable visible and hidden quantities [unverified]

4.3 Time-in-Force Options

TIF Description
Day (DAY) Valid for the current trading day; cancelled at end of day
Good till Cancelled (GTC) Remains active until executed or cancelled (subject to exchange maximum)
Good till Date (GTD) Valid until a specified expiry date
Immediate or Cancel (IOC) Execute available quantity immediately; cancel remainder
Fill or Kill (FOK) Must execute in full immediately or entire order is cancelled
At the Open (ATO) Valid only during the opening auction
At the Close (ATC) Valid only during the closing auction

4.4 Order Modification / Cancellation

  • Orders can be amended (price, quantity) or cancelled during trading sessions via OUCH or FIX gateway
  • Price improvements result in loss of time priority (new timestamp)
  • Quantity reductions preserve time priority
  • Mass cancellation functionality available through PRM Kill Switch

Technology Stack

  • Protocols: OUCH (native order entry, lowest latency), FIX (industry standard order entry), ITCH (market data dissemination via UDP multicast), GLIMPSE (snapshot recovery), AMD (market data), FIX Drop Copy
  • Market Data: TotalView-ITCH provides full order depth with market participant attribution; delivered via UDP/IP multicast with MoldUDP64 packet format
  • Network: TCP/IP for order entry gateways; UDP/IP multicast for market data
  • Timestamps: Nanosecond precision (nanoseconds since midnight UTC)
  • Protocol Versions (as of 2025): OUCH 5.02.6, FIX Order Entry 2.02.2, FIX DROP 2.01.6, TotalView-ITCH 3.04.5

Disaster Recovery / Business Continuity

  • Primary Data Center: Vasby (Upplands Vasby), north of Stockholm
  • Secondary Data Center: STOCKHOLM SOUTH (Equinix SK2) – relocated from PORT site during April-September 2025
  • Dual redundant market data feeds from two sites (co-location customers receive feeds from primary site only with redundant multicast flows)
  • Real-time replication between primary and secondary sites
  • Business continuity plans tested regularly as required by local regulators and MiFID II obligations
  • Dedicated gateways and co-location connections reside at primary Vasby site and retain their connectivity during failover

6.2 Data Levels

  • Level 1: Best bid/offer (BBO), last trade price, volume
  • Level 2: Aggregated depth at 5 price levels with quotes, orders, and trades
  • TotalView (Full Depth): Full order book depth, order-by-order granularity with attribution
  • Delayed Data: 15-minute delayed feeds available

6.3 Distribution Infrastructure

  • UDP/IP multicast from primary and secondary data center sites
  • MoldUDP64 packet format for reliable multicast delivery
  • Co-location customers receive multicast from Vasby primary site with redundant flows
  • TCP-based replay/re-request service for gap recovery

6.4 Historical Data

  • Historical tick data available through Nasdaq Data Link and third-party vendors
  • End-of-day data, reference data, corporate actions data available
  • Academic access available (e.g., Aalto University Datahub hosts Nasdaq Nordic Equity TotalView-ITCH)

8. Risk Controls & Circuit Breakers

8.1 Price Limits

  • Nasdaq Nordic does not impose fixed daily price limits (no hard up/down percentage limit)
  • Instead, uses Volatility Guards (dynamic and static) to detect and manage abnormal price movements

8.2 Circuit Breakers (Volatility Guards)

Type Trigger Response
Dynamic Volatility Guard Potential execution price deviates beyond a percentage threshold from the last traded price (continuously updated reference) Continuous trading halted; instrument enters a volatility auction lasting 60-180 seconds
Static Volatility Guard Potential execution price deviates beyond a percentage threshold from the reference price (typically the day’s opening auction price) Continuous trading halted; instrument enters a volatility auction lasting 60-180 seconds
Auction Safeguards Opening auction uncross price deviates beyond 2x the Dynamic Volatility Guard threshold; Scheduled intraday auction safeguard equals the Dynamic Volatility Guard value Auction period is extended
  • Volatility Guard thresholds are configured per instrument and reviewed periodically by the exchange
  • Specific percentage thresholds are defined in Appendix M of the Nasdaq Nordic Market Model and are not publicly disclosed in summary form [unverified]
  • After the volatility auction completes, the instrument returns to continuous trading

8.3 Pre-trade Risk Controls (PRM)

Nasdaq Nordic’s Pre-trade Risk Management (PRM) service validates orders entered via OUCH and FIX protocols:

Control Description
Blow-Through Protection Prevents orders from triggering Volatility Guards by checking price deviation before submission
Maximum Order Value Configurable per firm; orders exceeding the threshold are rejected
Maximum Order Quantity Limits on maximum shares per single order
Fat-Finger Protection Price and size reasonability checks to prevent erroneous orders
Message Throttling Rate limits on order submissions per second
FIX Drop On-Disconnect Automatically cancels all open orders and prevents new order entry upon client disconnect

PRM is designed for highly automated trading flows and adds no latency to order execution. It is compliant with ESMA guidelines on systems and controls in automated trading (MiFID II Article 48).

8.4 Kill Switch

  • Kill Switch for Mass Cancellation: Administrator function that cancels all outstanding orders for a firm or user
  • Available through PRM interface
  • Can be activated by the member firm or by Nasdaq Market Operations upon request
  • Supports cancellation at the CompID, user, or firm-wide level [unverified]

9. Regulatory Framework

9.1 Supervising Authorities

Country Regulator Role
Sweden Finansinspektionen (FI) Supervises Nasdaq Stockholm as a Regulated Market
Finland Finanssivalvonta (FIN-FSA) Supervises Nasdaq Helsinki
Denmark Finanstilsynet Supervises Nasdaq Copenhagen
Iceland Central Bank of Iceland (Sedlabanki Islands) / FME Supervises Nasdaq Iceland
Estonia Finantsinspektsioon (EFSA) Supervises Nasdaq Tallinn
Latvia Finansu un kapitala tirgus komisija (FCMC) Supervises Nasdaq Riga
Lithuania Lietuvos Bankas (Bank of Lithuania) Supervises Nasdaq Vilnius

Each exchange entity holds a Regulated Market license (Main Market) and/or MTF license (First North) in its respective jurisdiction. Although operated under common Nasdaq Nordic branding and harmonized rules, each entity is a separate legal entity supervised by its national regulator.

9.2 Key Regulations

  • MiFID II / MiFIR (Markets in Financial Instruments Directive II / Regulation): Core regulatory framework for all Nasdaq Nordic and Baltic markets as EU/EEA Regulated Markets; governs transparency, best execution, investor protection, algorithmic trading controls, and tick sizes
  • Market Abuse Regulation (MAR): EU-wide prohibition of insider dealing, market manipulation, and unlawful disclosure of inside information; directly applicable in all member states
  • Prospectus Regulation: EU regulation governing public offers of securities and admission to trading; prospectus must be approved by the home-country FSA
  • Transparency Directive: Disclosure requirements for issuers of listed securities (periodic financial reporting, major shareholding notifications)
  • EU Takeover Directive: Governs mandatory and voluntary takeover bids (transposed into local law)
  • CSDR (Central Securities Depositories Regulation): Governs settlement discipline and CSD authorization; Nasdaq CSD SE operates across Baltic and Icelandic markets
  • Nasdaq Nordic Main Market Rulebook for Issuers of Shares: Harmonized listing rules applied across Stockholm, Helsinki, Copenhagen, and Iceland with local supplements per jurisdiction

9.3 Market Surveillance

  • Nasdaq Nordic Market Surveillance: Centralized real-time surveillance monitoring trading activity across all Nordic and Baltic markets for potential market abuse, suspicious patterns, and rule violations
  • Nasdaq uses its own surveillance technology to monitor order flow, trading patterns, and price movements
  • Cooperation with national regulators: Suspicious Transaction and Order Reports (STORs) are filed with relevant FSAs
  • Nasdaq published its first European Financial Crime Report in 2025, and signed its first proof of concept with a Tier 1 European bank for anti-financial crime technology

9.4 Reporting Requirements

  • Transaction Reporting: Investment firms report to their national competent authority under MiFIR Article 26 (65 data fields per transaction, by T+1)
  • Post-trade Transparency: Real-time publication of trade details (MiFID II pre- and post-trade transparency regime)
  • Short Selling Disclosure: Per EU Short Selling Regulation; net short positions above 0.1% reported to NCA, above 0.5% disclosed publicly
  • Major Shareholding Notifications: Varies by country but generally aligned with the EU Transparency Directive thresholds

9.5 Cross-border Arrangements / Nordic Cooperation

  • Harmonized Rulebook: Nasdaq Nordic operates under a common Nordic Main Market Rulebook for Issuers of Shares, with local supplements (Supplement A for Stockholm, B for Copenhagen, C for Helsinki, D for Iceland) ensuring consistency while respecting local legal requirements
  • EU/EEA Passporting: Member firms authorized in one EU/EEA country can access other Nasdaq Nordic/Baltic markets under MiFID II passporting without additional local authorization
  • Nordic Cooperation: The Nordic and Baltic FSAs cooperate closely on cross-border supervision; joint memoranda of understanding facilitate information sharing
  • Nasdaq CSD SE: A single CSD entity authorized in Latvia, operating across Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, and Iceland under EU CSDR passporting, facilitating harmonized post-trade infrastructure
  • IOSCO MMOU: All relevant Nordic and Baltic regulators are signatories to the IOSCO Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding
