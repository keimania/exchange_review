3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Auction Mechanisms

  • Opening Call Auction (9:15â9:25):
    • Orders entered during 9:15â9:20 may be submitted and cancelled.
    • During 9:20â9:25, orders may be submitted but not cancelled (prevents manipulation of the indicative opening price).
    • At 9:25, the system determines the opening price via a single-price auction algorithm that maximizes executable volume â the price at which the greatest number of shares can be traded.
    • Unexecuted orders automatically carry forward into the continuous trading session at 9:30.
  • Closing Call Auction (14:57â15:00):
    • Introduced in 2006, replacing the previous method (last-trade closing price).
    • During this 3-minute window, orders are accepted but cannot be cancelled.
    • At 15:00, a single-price auction determines the closing price using the same volume-maximization algorithm as the opening auction.
    • This mechanism reduces susceptibility to closing price manipulation compared to last-trade methods.
  • Intraday Auction: Not applicable â SZSE uses continuous matching during regular trading hours.
  • Volatility Auction: Not currently implemented. China relied on market-wide circuit breakers briefly in January 2016 (linked to CSI 300 Index, with 5% and 7% triggers), but these were suspended after only 4 trading days due to exacerbating volatility. Individual stock price limits (Section 8) serve as the primary volatility control mechanism.

3.2 Continuous Trading

  • Order-driven market â all trades matched by the exchange’s electronic order book.
  • Price-time priority: Orders matched first by best price, then by earliest time of entry at the same price level.
  • No quote-driven or hybrid mechanism for equities (unlike HKEX or LSE).

3.3 Price Discovery

  • Opening price: Determined by call auction at 9:25; if no trades occur, the opening price is carried from the most recent transaction price.
  • Closing price: Determined by closing call auction at 15:00.
  • Intraday price: Continuous matching on price-time priority during regular sessions.
  • The T+1 settlement rule and daily price limits (Section 8) significantly constrain intraday price discovery compared to western markets, reducing speculative volatility but also limiting rapid price adjustment to new information.

4.1 Standard Order Types

8. Risk Controls & Circuit Breakers

8.1 Price Limits (Daily Price Movement Limits)

Type Threshold Applicable Securities
Standard daily limit +/-10% from previous closing price Main Board stocks, most securities
ChiNext daily limit +/-20% from previous closing price ChiNext stocks (widened from +/-10% in August 2020 reform)

New listing (ChiNext) No price limit for first 5 trading days ChiNext IPOs under registration-based system (from August 2020); +/-20% limit applies from 6th day onward

Notes: - Price limits are calculated based on the previous day’s closing price. - When a stock hits the daily limit (æ¶¨å/è·å), trading continues but only orders that would move the price back within the limit range are executable. The stock is not halted. - These static price limits serve as the primary volatility control mechanism in lieu of intraday circuit breakers.

8.2 Circuit Breakers (Market-Wide)

Level Trigger Status
Level 1 (5% decline in CSI 300) 15-minute halt Suspended since January 8, 2016
Level 2 (7% decline in CSI 300) Halt for remainder of day Suspended since January 8, 2016

Background: In January 2016, CSRC introduced market-wide circuit breakers linked to the CSI 300 Index with 5% (15-min halt) and 7% (full halt) triggers. They were activated on January 4 and January 7, 2016, exacerbating panic selling (the “magnet effect” â traders rushed to sell before the circuit breaker triggered). CSRC suspended the mechanism after just 4 operating days. Individual stock daily price limits remain the primary risk control mechanism.

8.3 Pre-trade Risk Controls

  • Fat-finger checks: Maximum order size of 1,000,000 shares per single order.
  • Price validation: Orders outside the daily price limit range are rejected.
  • Order throttling: Exchange-level controls on order submission rates [unverified â specific thresholds not publicly available in English].
  • Member-level controls: Brokers required to implement their own pre-trade risk management systems per CSRC requirements.
  • Abnormal trading monitoring: SZSE monitors for unusual trading patterns including suspected insider trading, market manipulation, and excessive order-to-trade ratios.

8.4 Additional Risk Measures

  • Mandatory margin trading controls: Margin financing subject to specific collateral ratios set by CSRC and brokers.
  • Short selling restrictions: Short selling only permitted through regulated securities lending programs; naked short selling prohibited.
  • Position limits: Applied to index futures and options that reference SZSE stocks (managed by CFFEX).

9.3 Registration-Based IPO System (æ³¨åå¶)

A critical regulatory development: - Pilot on ChiNext (April 2020): Registration-based system first trialed on ChiNext, shifting vetting power from CSRC to the exchange. The exchange reviews listing applications, while CSRC checks alignment with national industrial policy and board positioning. - Full rollout (February 2023): Extended to SZSE Main Board (and SSE Main Board), completing China’s transition from an approval-based (æ ¸åå¶) to registration-based system. - Under the new system, companies file registration statements with the exchange, which conducts inquiry-based review. CSRC performs registration (a formality) rather than substantive approval.

9.4 Market Surveillance

  • SZSE operates real-time market surveillance systems monitoring for:
    • Insider trading
    • Market manipulation (including spoofing and layering)
    • Abnormal trading patterns
    • Unusual price and volume movements
  • Automated alerts trigger investigation by SZSE’s enforcement division, which can refer cases to CSRC for formal action.

11. Recent Developments

System Upgrades (Last 5 Years)

  • 2020: Major technical upgrades for ChiNext registration-based IPO reform â supporting wider price limits (+/-20%), new order types, and enhanced surveillance systems.
  • 2016 (for context): Core trading system upgrade achieving 1.1ms matching engine latency and 300,000 orders/sec capacity.
  • Ongoing investment in trading system modernization, though specific recent upgrade details are not widely published in English.

New Products / Segments

  • ETF Options: SZSE listed ETF option contracts; as of 2025, a total of 9 ETF options listed across SSE and SZSE combined. QFIs admitted to options trading from October 2025.
  • REITs: Penghua Qianhai REIT became SZSE’s first publicly offered infrastructure REIT.
  • Cross-border ETFs: Expanded offerings including US market-tracking ETFs (S&P 500, S&P Healthcare, S&P Biotech, S&P IT).
  • Money Market ETFs: First transactional money market ETFs listed on SZSE.
  • Commodity Futures Fund: UBS Silver Fund LOF, SZSE’s first commodity futures fund.

  • 2023 (February): Full implementation of registration-based IPO system on Main Board, completing the nationwide reform.
  • 2023 (August): Stamp duty cut from 0.1% to 0.05% (sell side) â a significant fiscal stimulus for capital markets.
  • 2021 (April): SME Board merged into Main Board, simplifying market structure.
  • 2020 (August): ChiNext reformed with registration-based IPO system, +/-20% price limits, relaxed listing requirements.

Strategic Initiatives

  • Technology self-reliance: SZSE continues to develop all core trading technology in-house, aligned with China’s broader technology independence strategy.
  • Internationalization via Stock Connect: Continued expansion of Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock Connect scope, including ETF Connect (2022) and ongoing discussions about further broadening eligible securities.
  • Green finance and ESG: SZSE has promoted ESG disclosure and green bond listings as part of China’s sustainable finance agenda [unverified].
  • Support for “little giants” (ä¸ç²¾ç¹æ°): SZSE, particularly through ChiNext, actively supports the listing of specialized, innovation-focused SMEs that the Chinese government designates as “little giant” companies.
