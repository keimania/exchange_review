Key Historical Milestones

  • 1866: Original Shanghai Sharebrokers’ Association established during Qing Dynasty [historical predecessor]
  • 1920-1949: Shanghai Securities and Commodities Exchange operated during the Republic of China era; closed after 1949 revolution
  • 1990 (Nov 26): Shanghai Stock Exchange re-established as part of Deng Xiaoping’s economic reform program
  • 1990 (Dec 19): SSE commences operations with 8 listed A-share stocks; this date serves as the base day for the SSE Composite Index
  • 1991 (Jul 15): SSE Composite Index officially launched
  • 1992: First B-share listed on SSE (denominated in USD for foreign investors); Deng Xiaoping’s Southern Tour reinforces commitment to capital markets
  • 1997: State Council places SSE under direct management of CSRC
  • 2001: B-share market opened to domestic investors (previously restricted to foreign investors only)
  • 2002: QFII (Qualified Foreign Institutional Investor) scheme introduced, allowing foreign institutional access to A-shares
  • 2007: SSE market cap briefly becomes world’s largest emerging market exchange during China bull market
  • 2011: RQFII (RMB Qualified Foreign Institutional Investor) scheme launched
  • 2014 (Nov 17): Shanghai-Hong Kong Stock Connect launches, enabling cross-border trading with HKEX
  • 2016 (Jan 1): CSI 300 index circuit breaker mechanism implemented
  • 2016 (Jan 8): Circuit breaker suspended after triggering twice in its first week, causing market panic
  • 2018: SSE announces plans for Science and Technology Innovation Board (STAR Market)
  • 2019 (Jun): STAR Market (ç§åæ¿, Kechuangban) officially inaugurated with registration-based IPO system
  • 2020: QFII and RQFII schemes merged into unified Qualified Foreign Investor (QFI) scheme; quota limits abolished
  • 2023: Fourth-generation trading system development; continued internationalization efforts
  • 2024: SSE revises Shanghai-Hong Kong Stock Connect rules; formulates Three-year Action Plan for Index Business (2024-2026)
  • 2025: ETF market crosses RMB 4 trillion; REIT market value exceeds RMB 200 billion with 44 SSE-listed REITs (>70% of China’s REIT market); fee waiver notice for 2026 (~RMB 1.1 billion reduction)

2.4 Lot Sizes

  • Main Board: Standard lot = 100 shares; buy orders must be in multiples of 100 shares; sell orders for odd lots (<100 shares) must be sold in a single order; maximum order size = 1,000,000 shares
  • STAR Market: Minimum order = 200 shares for both limit and market orders; limit order max = 100,000 shares; market order max = 50,000 shares; odd lots (<200 shares) must be sold in a single order
  • Odd lot trading: Permitted only for sell-side; odd lots may result from partial fills

2.5 Market Maker / Liquidity Provider Programs

  • STAR Market: Market maker system introduced to enhance liquidity for STAR Market stocks; designated market makers provide two-sided quotes [unverified – details limited in public English sources]
  • Main Board: Primarily order-driven without formal market maker obligations for equities; ETF and options markets have designated liquidity providers

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Auction Mechanisms

Opening Call Auction (9:15-9:30): - Orders collected during 9:15-9:25 pre-open period - System calculates a single price that maximizes executable volume at 9:25 - Price determination principle: the price at which the maximum volume of shares can be traded; if multiple prices satisfy this condition, the price closest to the previous close is selected - Unmatched orders carry forward to continuous trading session - Orders submitted between 9:20-9:25 cannot be cancelled (to prevent manipulation)

Closing Call Auction (14:57-15:00): - Replaces continuous trading for the final 3 minutes of the session - Same single-price auction principle as opening auction - Orders accepted but cannot be cancelled during this period - Determines the official closing price of each security - Applicable to both Main Board and STAR Market

STAR Market After-hours Fixed-price Trading (15:05-15:30): - All trades executed at the day’s closing price - Time priority for order matching - Provides additional liquidity window for STAR Market stocks

3.2 Continuous Trading

  • Order-driven market: Pure order-driven (no designated market makers for Main Board equities)
  • Price-time priority: Orders are matched first by best price, then by time of entry at the same price level
  • Buy orders at the highest price and sell orders at the lowest price are matched first
  • Trade execution price is determined by existing resting orders (passive side)

3.3 Price Discovery

  • Opening price: Determined by opening call auction; if no trades occur during auction, the first trade during continuous trading becomes the opening price
  • Closing price: Determined by closing call auction (14:57-15:00)
  • T+1 trading rule significantly affects intraday price discovery – shares purchased on day T cannot be sold until day T+1, reducing intraday liquidity and creating asymmetric trading patterns (investors can buy shares they sold earlier the same day, but cannot sell shares purchased the same day)

4.5 Order Modification / Cancellation

  • Orders may be cancelled during the opening pre-open period (9:15-9:20) but not during 9:20-9:25
  • Orders cannot be cancelled during the closing call auction (14:57-15:00)
  • During continuous trading, unexecuted and partially executed orders may be cancelled
  • Order modification is generally implemented as cancel-and-replace

8. Risk Controls & Circuit Breakers

8.1 Price Limits

Market Segment Daily Price Limit Notes
Main Board (A-shares) +/-10% from previous close Applied to normal stocks; calculated based on previous trading day’s closing price
Main Board (ST / *ST stocks) +/-5% from previous close Special Treatment (ST) and delisting risk (*ST) stocks have tighter limits
STAR Market +/-20% from previous close Wider limits to accommodate higher-growth, higher-volatility tech stocks
STAR Market (first 5 trading days) No price limit New IPO listings have no price limit for the first 5 days of trading
B-shares +/-10% from previous close Same as Main Board A-shares

8.2 Circuit Breakers

Status Details
Currently: No market-wide circuit breaker China attempted an index-based circuit breaker mechanism in January 2016 but suspended it after only 4 trading days

2016 Circuit Breaker Episode: - Implemented: January 1, 2016, based on the CSI 300 Index - 5% decline threshold: Trading halted for 15 minutes; if triggered after 14:45, trading halted for remainder of day - 7% decline threshold: Trading halted for remainder of the day - Triggered: January 4, 2016 (both 5% and 7% thresholds hit) and January 7, 2016 (both thresholds hit within minutes) - Suspended: January 8, 2016 – CSRC suspended the mechanism permanently - Reason for failure: The circuit breaker created a “magnet effect” – investors rushed to sell before being locked in by the halt, creating a self-fulfilling prophecy that accelerated the decline. The proximity of the 5% and 7% thresholds (~2% gap) gave insufficient buffer time - Current status: Individual stock price limits (+/-10% / +/-20%) remain the primary volatility control mechanism; no plans to reintroduce market-wide circuit breakers as of 2026

8.3 Pre-trade Risk Controls

  • Price limit validation: Orders outside the daily price limit band are rejected by the system
  • Order size limits: Maximum order size of 1,000,000 shares (Main Board) / 100,000 shares for STAR Market limit orders / 50,000 shares for STAR Market market orders
  • Abnormal trading monitoring: SSE monitors for abnormal trading patterns including frequent cancellations, large orders intended to manipulate, and coordinated trading [unverified – specific thresholds not publicly disclosed]
  • Broker-level risk controls: Member brokers required to implement their own pre-trade risk checks per CSRC regulations

8.4 Kill Switch

  • SSE has authority to suspend trading in individual securities or halt market-wide trading under extraordinary circumstances
  • CSRC may direct SSE to implement emergency measures
  • Specific kill switch technical capabilities not publicly documented in English sources

9. Regulatory Framework

9.1 Supervising Authority

  • China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC): Primary regulator of China’s securities and futures markets; equivalent to the U.S. SEC
  • SSE is directly supervised by CSRC since 1997 (by State Council directive)
  • SSE also functions as a front-line self-regulatory organization (SRO), conducting listing review, trading surveillance, and member supervision under CSRC delegation
  • State Council of the PRC: Ultimate authority over capital market policy

9.2 Key Regulations

Regulation Description
Securities Law of the PRC (2019 revision) Fundamental law governing securities issuance, trading, and regulation; introduced registration-based IPO system framework
Company Law of the PRC Governs corporate governance requirements for listed companies
SSE Trading Rules (2023 Revision) Detailed rules for order submission, execution, settlement, and market conduct
SSE STAR Market Listing Rules Specific requirements for STAR Market IPOs under registration-based system
Measures for the Administration of Stock Exchanges CSRC rules on exchange governance and operations
Administrative Measures for QFI Unified rules for foreign institutional investor access (merged QFII/RQFII in 2020)

9.3 Market Surveillance

  • SSE operates real-time market surveillance systems monitoring for insider trading, market manipulation, and abnormal trading
  • Coordinated surveillance with CSRC and China Securities Depository and Clearing Corporation (CSDC)
  • SSE has authority to issue warnings, restrict trading, and refer cases to CSRC for enforcement
  • Cross-market surveillance coordination with Shenzhen Stock Exchange for cross-listed securities and index products

9.4 Reporting Requirements

  • Listed companies must comply with continuous disclosure obligations (annual, semi-annual, quarterly reports)
  • Major shareholders must report holdings changes (5% disclosure threshold)
  • Short selling and securities lending reporting requirements
  • Connected/related party transaction disclosure

9.5 Cross-border Arrangements

Stock Connect (Shanghai-Hong Kong Stock Connect): - Launched: November 17, 2014 - Northbound: International investors trade SSE-listed A-shares via HKEX brokers - Southbound: Mainland investors trade HKEX-listed stocks via SSE member brokers - Eligible stocks: Large/mid-cap A-shares (SSE 180, SSE 380 constituents, and dual-listed A+H stocks); scope has been gradually expanded - Daily quota: Northbound daily quota of RMB 52 billion (expanded from original RMB 13 billion); aggregate quota abolished in 2018 - Settlement: T+0 for Northbound (trades settle same day at HKSCC); underlying A-shares follow T+1 trading rule - ETF Connect: Expanded to include eligible ETFs since July 2022

Qualified Foreign Investor (QFI) Scheme: - Merged from QFII (2002) and RQFII (2011) into unified QFI scheme in 2020 - Investment quotas abolished in 2020 – no cap on investment amount - Requires CSRC license and SAFE registration - Provides broader access than Stock Connect (bonds, derivatives, private placement, etc.) - As of 2025, CSRC continues to optimize the QFI regime for further market opening

A-shares / B-shares Classification: | Share Type | Currency | Investor Access | Status | |———–|———-|—————-|——–| | A-shares | RMB | Domestic investors, QFI holders, Stock Connect participants | Primary market; vast majority of listings and volume | | B-shares | USD (SSE) / HKD (SZSE) | Originally foreign-only; opened to domestic investors in 2001 | Legacy market; minimal new activity; periodic discussions about B-share reform or phase-out |

11. Recent Developments

System Upgrades (Last 5 Years)

  • Fourth-generation trading system: Self-developed, independently controllable system achieving 2.5x order capacity increase, 7x sustained processing improvement, and 20-fold latency reduction vs. previous generation; meets Chinese cryptographic and cybersecurity standards
  • New-generation options trading system: Successfully launched on the fourth-generation platform
  • Technology self-sufficiency: Alignment with China’s national strategy for domestically developed core financial technology infrastructure

New Products / Segments

  • STAR Market maturation: ~606 listed companies by 2025; continued registration-based IPO reforms
  • REITs expansion: 44 SSE-listed REITs with total market value >RMB 200 billion (>70% of China REIT market); new consumer infrastructure REITs introduced; temporary fee waivers for REITs
  • ETF market growth: Domestic ETF market crossed RMB 4 trillion in April 2025, reaching RMB 4.3 trillion by June 2025; SSE ETFs span stocks, bonds, commodities, currencies, and cross-border assets
  • Stock Connect expansion: ETF Connect launched July 2022; scope of eligible securities continues to expand
  • Three-year Action Plan for Index Business (2024-2026): Building virtuous cycle among “listed companies – indexes – index products – investors”

Regulatory Changes

  • Registration-based IPO: Extended from STAR Market to Main Board as part of comprehensive capital market reform
  • QFI scheme optimization: CSRC continues to streamline foreign investor access
  • Fee reductions: RMB 1.113 billion fee waiver for 2026; prior stamp duty cut in 2023
  • Delisting reform: Tightened delisting standards to improve market quality
  • Bond market opening: Foreign institutional investors granted access to exchange bond repo business (December 2025)

Strategic Initiatives

  • Internationalization: Continued expansion of Stock Connect; deeper cooperation with HKEX; participation in global exchange organizations
  • ESG and green finance: Promotion of green bonds and ESG-related index products [unverified]
  • Digital transformation: Technology self-sufficiency strategy; exploration of fintech applications
  • Market quality improvement: Focus on investor protection, listed company governance, and “coordinated ecosystem for investment and financing” (2025 Lujiazui Forum theme)

China-Specific Features Summary

Feature Description
T+1 Trading Rule Shares purchased on day T cannot be sold until day T+1; this is asymmetric – shares sold on day T can be repurchased the same day. This fundamentally limits day-trading and affects market microstructure
Daily Price Limits +/-10% Main Board / +/-20% STAR Market replace Western-style circuit breakers as the primary volatility mechanism
State Ownership SSE is a state-owned, non-profit institution directly supervised by CSRC – unlike most major global exchanges which are publicly listed, for-profit corporations
A/B Share Dual Structure Legacy system of dual share classes by currency/investor type; B-shares largely obsolete but still traded
Foreign Access Restrictions Foreign investors cannot trade A-shares directly; access only through QFI scheme or Stock Connect; strategic/sensitive sectors may have additional restrictions
Registration vs. Approval IPO STAR Market uses registration-based system (similar to Western markets); Main Board historically used approval-based system but is transitioning to registration
CSRC Dominant Role CSRC exercises much more direct control over exchange operations, IPO approvals, and market interventions than Western regulators typically do
Stock Connect Mechanism Unique cross-border trading link with HKEX that enables foreign access while maintaining China’s capital controls
