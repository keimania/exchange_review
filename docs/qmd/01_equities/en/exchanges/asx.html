1. Overview & Profile

Item Details
Official Name ASX Limited (operating the Australian Securities Exchange)
Abbreviation ASX
Website https://www.asx.com.au
Year Established 1987 (merger of six state-based stock exchanges); ASX Limited formed in 2006 via merger of ASX and SFE (Sydney Futures Exchange)
Country / City Australia / Sydney (Exchange Centre, 20 Bridge Street, Sydney NSW 2000)
Ownership Structure Publicly listed on its own exchange (ticker: ASX); ~50% retail investors, ~49% institutional (top holders: UniSuper ~14%, BlackRock ~6.1%, AustralianSuper ~6.1%), insiders <1%
Parent Company / Group ASX Limited (self-listed holding company)
Regulatory Authority Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC); Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) for clearing and settlement stability
CEO Helen Lofthouse (appointed August 2022)
Market Capitalization (listed companies) ~AUD 3.4 trillion / ~USD 2.2 trillion (February 2026)
ASX Limited Market Cap (exchange operator) ~AUD 9.6 billion (2026)
Total Listed Instruments ~2,241 listed companies (as of 2025)
Average Daily Trading Volume ~AUD 4.7 billion average daily turnover
FY2025 Operating Revenue AUD 1,107.2 million (+7.0% YoY)
FY2025 Underlying Net Profit AUD 510.0 million (+7.5% YoY)

Vertical Integration

ASX is one of the world’s few vertically integrated exchange groups, operating across the full value chain: - Listings: ASX Listing Rules govern admission and ongoing obligations - Trading: ASX Trade (cash equities), ASX 24 (derivatives) - Clearing: ASX Clear (cash equities and equity options), ASX Clear (Futures) - Settlement: ASX Settlement (via CHESS – Clearing House Electronic Subregister System) - Registry: Austraclear (debt securities)

Key Historical Milestones

  • 1861: First stock exchange established in Melbourne
  • 1871: Sydney Stock Exchange established
  • 1987: Australian Stock Exchange (ASX) formed on 1 April through the merger of six independent state-based stock exchanges (Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide, Hobart, Perth) under the Australian Securities Exchange and National Guarantee Fund Act 1987
  • 1987: Fully electronic trading introduced (SEATS – Stock Exchange Automated Trading System), replacing floor trading
  • 1990: ASX became a national market; Australian Securities Commission (predecessor to ASIC) established
  • 1996: CHESS (Clearing House Electronic Subregister System) launched for electronic settlement and subregistry
  • 1998: ASX demutualised and listed on its own exchange (13 October), becoming one of the first exchanges globally to do so
  • 2006: ASX merged with the Sydney Futures Exchange (SFE) to form ASX Limited, creating a multi-asset exchange group
  • 2010: ASX Trade launched (powered by NASDAQ OMX Genium INET), replacing SEATS/ITS
  • 2010-2011: Proposed merger with Singapore Exchange (SGX) for AUD 8.4 billion; blocked by the Australian government on national interest grounds (April 2011)
  • 2012: Chi-X Australia (now Cboe Australia) launched as a competing lit market, ending ASX’s trading monopoly; ASIC assumed market supervision from ASX
  • 2015: ASX selected Digital Asset Holdings (DA) to explore distributed ledger technology (DLT) for CHESS replacement
  • 2017: ASX formally announced the CHESS replacement using DLT/blockchain technology
  • 2022: ASX abandoned the DLT-based CHESS replacement project in November, writing off ~AUD 250 million
  • 2023: ASX selected Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) as the new implementation partner for CHESS replacement using conventional technology
  • 2024: CHESS batch settlement failure on 20 December triggered major regulatory scrutiny
  • 2025: ASIC launched formal Inquiry into ASX Group (June 2025) following “repeated and serious failures”
  • 2025: Market structure changes (SR15) implemented on 23 June – single opening auction, new closing auction format, and post-close trading session

2. Market Structure

2.1 Market Segments

Segment Description Listing Requirements (Summary)

2.4 Lot Sizes

  • Standard Board Lot: 1 share (ASX does not enforce a minimum board lot for equities; orders are accepted in units of 1)
  • Odd Lot Trading: Fully supported in the main order book; no separate odd-lot market required
  • Minimum Order Value: Some brokers impose their own minimum order values (e.g., AUD 500), but this is not an exchange-level requirement

2.5 Market Maker / Liquidity Provider Programs

ASX operates several liquidity enhancement programmes:

  • ETF Market Making: Dedicated market makers provide continuous two-sided quotes for ETFs with obligations on spread width and minimum quote size; incentivised through reduced trading and clearing fees
  • Warrant Market Making: Issuers of structured warrants are required to make markets in their products
  • Cash Equity Liquidity: ASX has periodically offered incentive programmes for market makers in less liquid securities, though these are not as formalised as some other exchanges [unverified – specific current programmes may have changed]

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Auction Mechanisms

  • Opening Auction: Orders accumulate during the pre-open phase (from 07:00). At a randomised time between 09:59:00 and 09:59:15, the system executes a single-price auction that maximises tradable volume. The equilibrium price algorithm considers: (1) maximum executable volume, (2) minimum order imbalance at that volume, (3) reference price proximity. All securities open simultaneously (single open, effective June 2025).

  • Closing Single Price Auction (CSPA): The pre-CSPA phase begins at 16:00, allowing orders to accumulate. At a randomised time around 16:10-16:11, the closing auction executes using the same equilibrium price algorithm. The CSPA closing price is the official closing price used for index calculations, margin calculations, and NAV determinations.

  • Post-Close Session: Introduced June 2025, this 10-minute continuous trading session allows orders to match at the official closing price only. Provides additional liquidity for participants needing to execute at the close (e.g., index funds, benchmark-driven strategies).

  • Volatility Auction: Not a formal separate auction; instead, ASX uses Extreme Trade Range (ETR) and Anomalous Order Threshold (AOT) mechanisms to pause or restrict trading when price volatility is excessive (see Section 8).

3.2 Continuous Trading

  • Model: Pure order-driven Central Limit Order Book (CLOB) for ASX TradeMatch (lit market)
  • Priority: Strict price-time priority – orders matched first by best price, then by time of entry at the same price level
  • Dark Liquidity: ASX Centre Point operates as an integrated dark pool alongside the lit order book (see Section 4.2)
  • Session Duration: 6 hours continuous (10:00-16:00), no lunch break

3.3 Price Discovery

Equilibrium Price Algorithm (for auctions): 1. Calculate the price that maximises executable volume 2. If multiple prices yield the same maximum volume, select the price with the smallest imbalance 3. If still tied, select the price closest to the reference price (last traded price or previous close) 4. Market orders participate with highest priority

Continuous Session: - Price discovery through continuous matching of incoming aggressive orders against resting passive orders - ASX TradeMatch (lit) and ASX Centre Point (dark) operate as two integrated venues within the same matching engine - Centre Point orders execute at or within the NBBO (National Best Bid and Offer), with midpoint being the default execution price

4. Order Types

4.1 Standard Order Types (ASX TradeMatch – Lit Market)

Order Type Description
Limit Order Buy or sell at a specified price or better; rests on the order book if not immediately matched
Market Order Execute immediately at the best available price; any unfilled quantity converts to a limit order at the last execution price (market-to-limit behaviour)
Market-to-Limit Order Execute at the best available price; unfilled portion rests as a limit order at the price of the last partial fill

4.2 Advanced Order Types (ASX Centre Point – Dark)

Order Type Description
Centre Point Non-displayed order matched at the midpoint of the NBBO; provides price improvement over the lit market
Centre Point Block Centre Point order with a user-defined Minimum Acceptable Quantity (MAQ); only executes when the MAQ can be satisfied
Single Fill MAQ Executes only when the full MAQ can be satisfied by a single opposing order; designed for block discovery with minimal information leakage
Dark Limit Non-displayed order executed at any permitted dark price up to the specified limit price (not restricted to midpoint)
ASX Sweep Single transaction that sweeps dark liquidity in Centre Point first, then lit liquidity in TradeMatch; remaining volume rests in TradeMatch
ASX Sweep Dual Post Simultaneously posts orders in both Centre Point and TradeMatch at limit +1/2 tick; no risk of overfill
Pegged Order Order that automatically adjusts its price to track the best bid or offer [unverified – specific pegged order availability may vary]

4.3 Time-in-Force Options

TIF Option Description
Day Valid for the current trading day only; cancelled at end of day
IOC (Immediate or Cancel) Execute immediately (fully or partially); cancel any unfilled portion
FOK (Fill or Kill) Execute immediately in full or cancel the entire order
GTC (Good-till-Cancel) Remains active until executed or explicitly cancelled (subject to maximum order lifetime)
GTD (Good-till-Date) Remains active until specified date or until filled/cancelled
GTE (Good-till-Expiry) Remains active until the expiry date of the instrument

4.4 Order Modification / Cancellation

  • Orders can be amended (price, quantity) or cancelled during the pre-open and continuous trading sessions
  • Price amendments result in loss of time priority (new timestamp assigned)
  • Quantity reductions (downward amendments) preserve time priority
  • During the Non-Cancel period of auctions, no cancellations or amendments are permitted
  • ASX Operating Rules require participants to have adequate systems to manage and control orders

6. Market Data

6.1 Data Feed Protocols

Protocol Description Use Case
ASX ITCH Ultra-low latency binary multicast protocol; delivers full order book detail with nanosecond timestamps Primary feed for algorithmic/HFT firms; full depth of book
GLIMPSE Point-to-point TCP snapshot feed complementing ITCH Order book recovery/synchronisation; provides current state snapshot with sequence number for ITCH resync
FIX Market Data Industry-standard FIX protocol for market data delivery Broader participant community; lower-latency requirements
ASX MDF (Market Data Feed) [Legacy/alternative market data distribution] General market data consumption

6.2 Data Levels

  • Level 1 (Top of Book): Best bid/offer prices and sizes; last traded price, volume; daily high/low/open/close
  • Level 2 (Market Depth): Multiple price levels of the order book (typically 5-10 levels of aggregated depth)
  • Full Depth (ITCH): Complete order-by-order view of the order book; every order addition, modification, cancellation, and execution event; all price levels

6.3 Distribution Infrastructure

  • Direct Feeds: Available via co-location at the ALC or via ASX Net connectivity
  • Redistributors: ASX real-time data distributed through major vendors including Refinitiv (LSEG), Bloomberg, FactSet, and others
  • ASX Information Services: Manages licensing and distribution of all ASX market data products
  • Delivery: ITCH via UDP multicast; GLIMPSE via TCP (SoupBinTCP); FIX via TCP

6.4 Historical Data

  • ASX provides end-of-day data, tick-by-tick historical data, and reference data through ASX Information Services
  • Third-party providers (e.g., Refinitiv, TickData) offer processed historical ASX data products
  • Corporate actions and announcement data available through ASX’s company announcements platform

7. Connectivity & Access

7.1 Membership / Participation

  • Market Participants: Must hold an Australian Market Licence (AML) or be authorised by ASIC as a market participant under the Corporations Act 2001
  • Trading Participants: Must meet ASX Operating Rules requirements including capital adequacy, organisational capacity, and system certification
  • Clearing Participants: Separate membership required for ASX Clear (cash) and ASX Clear (Futures); subject to capital requirements and risk management obligations
  • Settlement Participants: Must be approved by ASX Settlement to participate in CHESS

7.2 Connection Methods

Method Description
Co-location (ALC) Direct rack space in the Australian Liquidity Centre; equal-length cross-connects to matching engine
ASX Net ASX’s high-performance dark fibre network connecting ALC to participant offices and other data centres in Sydney and Melbourne
Third-party Networks Carrier-neutral ALC allows connection via preferred network providers (e.g., BT Radianz, IPC, TNS)
Internet/VPN Available for non-latency-sensitive connectivity (e.g., surveillance, reporting)

7.3 Co-location / Proximity Services

  • Location: Australian Liquidity Centre (ALC), Gore Hill, Sydney, NSW
  • Facility: Purpose-built Tier III data centre, opened 2012; designed specifically for financial markets
  • Equal Access: All co-located participants receive identical-length cross-connects to the matching engine
  • Tenants: 100+ financial organisations including buy-side firms, sell-side firms, alternative liquidity venues, ISVs, and network providers
  • Peer-to-Peer: Cross-connect fabric enables direct participant-to-participant connectivity within the ALC

7.4 API Protocols

Protocol Use Case
FIX (Financial Information eXchange) Standard order entry and execution; widely supported; suitable for most participants
OUCH Ultra-low latency binary order entry protocol; optimised for algorithmic and high-frequency trading
ITCH Ultra-low latency binary market data protocol; full depth of book via multicast
GLIMPSE TCP-based order book snapshot recovery; complements ITCH for session recovery
OMNet / OMEX Legacy API interfaces [unverified – may have been retired with platform upgrades]

7.5 Certification / Testing

  • All market participants must complete ASX certification testing before connecting to the production environment
  • ASX provides a Customer Development Environment (CDE) for testing
  • Participants must re-certify following significant system changes (either ASX or participant-side)
  • Technical documentation available via ASX Online (asxonline.com)

8. Risk Controls & Circuit Breakers

8.1 Price Limits

Type Threshold Action
Anomalous Order Threshold (AOT) +/- 10% from a rolling 2-minute VWAP reference price (updated every minute) Orders priced beyond the AOT are rejected; prevents fat-finger and erroneous orders
Extreme Trade Range (ETR) Stock-specific price bands (calculated for each security based on its characteristics) If a potential trade would breach the ETR, trading in that security is paused for 2 minutes

8.2 Circuit Breakers

Mechanism Trigger Action
Extreme Trade Range (ETR) Pause Trade would execute outside the ETR band for a specific security 2-minute trading pause in the affected security; other securities continue trading normally
Market-wide Circuit Breaker ASX does not operate a formal market-wide circuit breaker based on index decline thresholds (unlike NYSE/NASDAQ) ASX relies on individual security-level controls (ETR/AOT) and has the discretion to halt the market under extraordinary circumstances
Trading Halt Requested by the listed entity or imposed by ASX for pending price-sensitive announcements Trading suspended until the announcement is released and the market has time to absorb the information
Suspension Imposed by ASX for non-compliance with listing rules, at ASIC direction, or for other regulatory reasons Trading suspended indefinitely until the issue is resolved

ASX’s Philosophy: ASX has publicly articulated a preference for individual security-level controls (AOT/ETR) over broad market-wide circuit breakers, arguing that targeted mechanisms are more effective at maintaining orderly markets without unnecessarily disrupting trading in unaffected securities.

8.3 Pre-trade Risk Controls

  • Anomalous Order Threshold (AOT): Prevents aggressive orders beyond +/- 10% of the reference price
  • Order Size Limits: ASX Operating Rules require participants to implement their own order size controls; ASX can also set system-level limits
  • Participant-level Controls: Trading participants are required to maintain pre-trade risk management systems including maximum order value checks and position limits
  • Kill Switch: ASX provides functionality for participants to cancel all resting orders (mass cancel) in emergency situations

8.4 Kill Switch

  • Participants can issue mass cancel requests via FIX or OUCH protocols to cancel all outstanding orders
  • ASX can also exercise its own authority to cancel or remove orders from the market in exceptional circumstances under the ASX Operating Rules

9. Regulatory Framework

9.1 Supervising Authority

  • ASIC (Australian Securities and Investments Commission): Primary regulator; responsible for market supervision, licensing of market operators and participants, enforcement of market integrity rules, and consumer protection
  • RBA (Reserve Bank of Australia): Oversees clearing and settlement facility licensees under the Financial Stability Standards; assesses ASX Clear and ASX Settlement annually for compliance
  • ASX Compliance: ASX’s internal compliance function monitors listing rule compliance by listed entities (separate from ASIC’s market supervision role)

9.2 Key Regulations

  • Corporations Act 2001 (Cth): Primary legislation governing financial markets, including Part 7.2 (market licences), Part 7.3 (clearing and settlement facility licences), and Part 7.10 (market integrity)
  • ASIC Market Integrity Rules (Securities Markets) 2017: Detailed rules governing trading conduct, best execution, order handling, and market manipulation
  • ASIC Market Integrity Rules (Competition in Exchange Markets) 2011: Rules governing competition between trading venues (ASX vs. Cboe Australia)
  • ASX Operating Rules: Exchange-specific rules governing trading participants, order entry, and market conduct
  • ASX Listing Rules: Govern the admission and ongoing obligations of listed entities, including continuous disclosure, related party transactions, and shareholder approval requirements
  • ASX Clear Operating Rules / ASX Settlement Operating Rules: Govern clearing and settlement participants
  • Financial Stability Standards (RBA): Standards that ASX Clear and ASX Settlement must meet to maintain their clearing and settlement facility licences

9.3 Market Surveillance

  • ASIC Market Supervision: ASIC assumed direct market supervision responsibility from ASX in August 2010 (formalised 2012), using the SMARTS surveillance system
  • Real-time Monitoring: ASIC monitors all trading activity across ASX and Cboe Australia in real time for market manipulation, insider trading, and other misconduct
  • ASX Listing Compliance: ASX retains responsibility for monitoring compliance with Listing Rules (continuous disclosure, corporate governance)
  • ASIC Inquiry into ASX (2025-2026): ASIC launched a formal inquiry into the ASX Group in June 2025, following the December 2024 CHESS settlement failure and other incidents; the inquiry examined governance, capability, and risk management across all ASX licensed entities

9.4 Reporting Requirements

  • Continuous Disclosure: Listed entities must immediately disclose price-sensitive information under ASX Listing Rule 3.1 and Section 674 of the Corporations Act
  • Transaction Reporting: Market participants must report all transactions to ASIC
  • Substantial Holder Notices: Required when ownership crosses 5% threshold (and for each 1% change thereafter)
  • Short Selling Reporting: Daily reporting of covered short sales; naked short selling is prohibited

9.5 Cross-border Arrangements

  • ASX has mutual recognition arrangements with several overseas exchanges and regulators
  • Foreign companies can list on ASX under the Foreign Exempt listing category
  • ASIC participates in IOSCO and has MoUs with numerous international regulators
  • The proposed ASX-SGX merger (2010-2011) was blocked by the Australian government under the Foreign Acquisitions and Takeovers Act on national interest grounds

11. Recent Developments

CHESS Replacement Saga (Major Story)

The failed CHESS replacement project is one of the most significant technology failures in global exchange history:

Phase 1 – DLT/Blockchain Approach (2015-2022): - 2015: ASX selected Digital Asset Holdings (DA) to explore DLT for CHESS replacement - 2017: ASX formally announced CHESS replacement using DA’s distributed ledger technology - 2018-2022: Repeated delays; go-live date pushed back multiple times (originally 2020, then 2021, 2022, 2023) - November 2022: ASX abandoned the DLT-based project after an independent review by Accenture found the software was only 63% complete with an uncertain timeline and overly complex integrated design - Financial impact: ~AUD 250 million write-off - Key lesson: Attempting to replace critical market infrastructure with unproven technology (enterprise blockchain) was identified as a fundamental strategic error

Phase 2 – Conventional Technology Approach (2023-present): - 2023: ASX selected Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) as the new implementation partner - Two-phased approach: Phase 1 (clearing) targeted for late April 2026; Phase 2 (settlement and subregistry) targeted for 2028-2029 - Complete pivot away from DLT to conventional technology stack - CHESS replacement Release One go-live targeted for late April 2026 [subject to change]

December 2024 CHESS Settlement Failure

  • 20 December 2024: CHESS batch settlement failed to complete, pushing all settlements to the following Monday
  • Root cause: A memory allocation coding error introduced in 2014 that went undetected for a decade until specific conditions triggered it
  • Impact: Disrupted settlement of all cash equity transactions for the Australian market
  • Triggered unprecedented joint RBA-ASIC intervention in March 2025

ASIC Inquiry into ASX Group (2025-2026)

  • March 2025: RBA and ASIC issued joint letter expressing “deep concerns” about ASX’s operational risk management
  • June 2025: ASIC launched formal Inquiry into the ASX Group covering governance, capability, and risk management across all ASX licensed entities (market licences and clearing/settlement facility licences)
  • The inquiry examined “repeated and serious failures” across the ASX Group
  • December 2025: ASIC announced a “transformational package” to safeguard financial markets based on the inquiry’s interim report
  • March 2026: ASIC published the ASX Inquiry Panel Final Report with observations and recommendations for change

Market Structure Changes – SR15 (June 2025)

  • Single Opening Auction: All securities now open simultaneously (replaced the previous 5-group staggered open)
  • Enhanced Closing Auction: Extended pre-CSPA window from 16:00 (was 16:07); improved handling of late price-sensitive announcements
  • Post-Close Trading Session: New 10-minute continuous lit session after the CSPA at the official closing price (16:11-16:21:30)
  • These changes align ASX more closely with global best practice (similar to NASDAQ, HKEX, LSE)

T+1 Settlement Plans

  • Australia currently settles on T+2
  • ASX published an industry whitepaper in April 2024; T+1 Working Group established December 2023
  • Industry consensus: T+1 is desirable but should not proceed until the CHESS replacement is complete
  • No confirmed T+1 implementation date; earliest feasible timeline likely 2029+ (after CHESS Phase 2)
  • International context: US moved to T+1 in May 2024; EU targeting 2027; UK targeting 2027

Other Developments

  • Tokenised Government Bond Settlement: ASX launched a pilot for tokenised government bond settlement in March 2026
  • ASIC CS Services Rules 2025: New regulatory framework for clearing and settlement service pricing, implementing revenue-capping methodology
  • Competition: Cboe Australia continues to operate as a competing lit venue; ASX maintains ~80%+ market share of lit on-exchange trading [unverified]
