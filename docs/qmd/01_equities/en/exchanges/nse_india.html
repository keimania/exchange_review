1. Overview & Profile

Item Details
Official Name National Stock Exchange of India Limited
Abbreviation NSE
Website https://www.nseindia.com
Year Established 1992 (incorporated); commenced operations 1994
Country / City India / Mumbai (Exchange Plaza, Bandra Kurla Complex, Mumbai 400051)
Ownership Structure Demutualized (ownership, management, and trading separated); unlisted. Major shareholders include LIC (~10.7%), SBI (~3.23%), and international investors (Temasek Holdings, Morgan Stanley, among others) [Source: Wikipedia, “National Stock Exchange of India”, accessed 2026-04-06]
Parent Company / Group Standalone entity (not part of a broader exchange group)
Regulatory Authority Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
Market Capitalization (listed companies) ~INR 438.9 lakh crore (~USD 5.13 trillion) as of December 2024; exceeded USD 5 trillion in May 2024; 5th largest stock exchange globally by market cap [Source: Wikipedia, “National Stock Exchange of India”, accessed 2026-04-06]
Total Listed Instruments 2,671 companies (2,084 mainboard + 587 SME platform / NSE EMERGE) as of December 2024 [Source: Wikipedia, accessed 2026-04-06]
Average Daily Trading Volume ~1.5â2.0 billion shares (equity cash segment) [estimated]
Average Daily Trading Value ~INR 70,000â80,000 crore (~USD 8â9 billion) equity cash segment [estimated]; derivatives notional turnover vastly larger

Key Historical Milestones

  • 1992: NSE incorporated on November 27, following recommendations of the Pherwani Committee and a push to modernize Indian markets after the Harshad Mehta securities scam [Source: Britannica Money, “National Stock Exchange of India”, accessed 2026-04-06]
  • 1993: Recognized as a stock exchange by SEBI in April 1993
  • 1994: Commenced operations â Wholesale Debt Market (WDM) segment launched June 30; Capital Market (equity cash) segment launched November 3; first exchange in India to introduce fully electronic screen-based trading
  • 1995: NSE surpassed BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange) in trading volumes, becoming India’s largest exchange by turnover
  • 1996: NIFTY 50 index launched on April 22; National Securities Depository Limited (NSDL) promoted by NSE for dematerialized settlement
  • 2000: Equity derivatives trading launched on June 12 with index futures (S&P CNX Nifty), making NSE one of the first exchanges in India to offer derivatives
  • 2001: Index options, stock options, and stock futures progressively introduced
  • 2008: Currency derivatives segment launched in August, the first exchange in India to offer currency futures
  • 2009: Interest Rate Futures launched
  • 2010: NSE launched Mutual Fund Service System (MFSS) platform
  • 2012: NSE EMERGE SME platform launched for small and medium enterprises
  • 2013: NIFTY 50 crossed 6,000 for the first time
  • 2015: Colocation controversy surfaced through whistleblower complaints to SEBI (first anonymous letter January 2015)
  • 2023: Social Stock Exchange launched on NSE platform
  • 2024: NSE declared world’s largest derivatives exchange by number of contracts for 5th consecutive year (CY2023 data) [Source: NSE Press Release, January 2024]
  • 2026 (April 11): Planned launch of nanosecond-level trading system upgrade with capacity of ~100 million transactions per second [Source: Business Today, February 22, 2026]

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Auction Mechanisms

  • Opening Auction (Pre-open Session): Call auction during 9:00â9:08 AM determines the opening price. Orders are accumulated and matched at a single equilibrium price that maximizes tradeable volume. Unmatched orders carry forward to continuous trading.
  • Closing Price: Determined as the volume-weighted average price (VWAP) of trades in the last 30 minutes of continuous trading (14:30â15:00 was previously used; current closing mechanism may involve a dedicated closing session) [unverified â exact current methodology should be confirmed with NSE]
  • Special Pre-open Sessions (SPOS): Used for stock re-listing, corporate action adjustments, or circuit-breaker recovery â the exchange initiates a call auction to re-establish equilibrium price
  • Periodic Call Auction (PCA): Applied to illiquid securities (those in the “Trade-to-Trade” segment or identified by surveillance); matches orders at fixed intervals rather than continuously

[Source: NSE Official Website, “Trading System”; Tandfonline, “Pre-open call auction and price discovery: Evidence from India”]

3.2 Continuous Trading

  • Order-Driven Market: NSE operates a fully order-driven, anonymous electronic market (no market makers required for main equities)
  • Price-Time Priority: The matching engine follows strict price-time priority â best-priced orders are matched first; among orders at the same price, the earliest order has priority
  • Passive orders rest in the order book; incoming aggressive orders are matched immediately against resting orders

[Source: NSE Official Website, “Trading System”]

3.3 Price Discovery

  • Pre-open session establishes the opening price via call auction (equilibrium price algorithm)
  • During continuous trading, price discovery is fully electronic and based on supply-demand interaction in the order book
  • NSE’s fully electronic system replaced the open outcry method previously used by BSE, vastly improving price transparency across India

8. Risk Controls & Circuit Breakers

8.1 Price Bands (Individual Securities)

Daily price bands are applied to individual securities to limit extreme single-day moves:

Band Application
2% Applied to select highly volatile or surveillance-flagged securities
5% Applied to certain securities under enhanced surveillance
10% Applied to moderately liquid securities
20% Applied to most other securities including debentures and preference shares
No band Securities in derivatives segment (F&O stocks) â no daily price band, but operating range limits apply

[Source: NSE Official Website, “Price Bands”; Kotak Neo, “NSE Price Band Changes”]

Notes: - Price bands are reviewed and revised periodically by NSE based on SEBI criteria. - Stocks in the Trade-to-Trade (T2T) segment or under additional surveillance measures (ASM/GSM) may have tighter bands. - F&O eligible stocks have no daily circuit limits but are subject to operating range (OR) checks by the exchange.

8.2 Circuit Breakers (Index-Based Market-Wide)

The index-based market-wide circuit breaker system halts trading across all equity and equity derivative markets nationwide. Triggered by movement of either BSE Sensex or Nifty 50, whichever is breached first:

Trigger Level Before 1:00 PM 1:00 PM â 2:30 PM After 2:30 PM
10% movement Trading halt for 45 minutes Trading halt for 15 minutes No halt; trading continues
15% movement Trading halt for 1 hour 45 minutes Trading halt for 45 minutes Remainder of day halt
20% movement Trading halted for remainder of day Trading halted for remainder of day Trading halted for remainder of day

[Source: NSE Official Website, “Circuit Breakers”; SEBI circular on market-wide circuit breakers]

Notes: - After a circuit breaker halt, the market reopens with a pre-open call auction session (special pre-open session). - The reference value for computing circuit breaker triggers is the previous day’s closing index value. - Circuit breakers apply uniformly to NSE, BSE, and all derivative segments across India.

8.3 Pre-trade Risk Controls

  • Quantity Freeze Limits: Maximum permissible order quantity per single trade in F&O contracts; orders exceeding this limit are automatically rejected by the exchange system [Source: ICICI Direct, “Revised Quantity Freeze Limits”]
  • Order Value Limits: Maximum order value checks to prevent fat-finger errors
  • Price Range Checks: Orders outside the allowed operating range are rejected
  • Broker-Level Controls: Brokers required to implement pre-trade risk checks auditable by NSE and SEBI (per SEBI circular, July 2021 and subsequent updates)
  • Algorithmic Trading Controls: All algo orders must be tagged; algo strategies require exchange approval; SEBI regulations effective August 1, 2025 mandate comprehensive risk checks for retail algo trading

8.4 Kill Switch

  • Brokers and trading members have kill switch capabilities to instantly halt all algo orders
  • Exchanges reserve the right to deactivate rogue algorithms affecting market integrity
  • SEBI mandates kill switch as part of algo trading risk management framework

[Source: Groww, “SEBI Regulations on Algorithmic Trading in India”; KillSwitch.in]

9. Regulatory Framework

9.1 Supervising Authority

  • Primary Regulator: Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), established under the SEBI Act, 1992
  • Governing Legislation:
    • Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992
    • Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 (SCRA)
    • Depositories Act, 1996
    • Securities Markets Code, 2025 â new unified code consolidating SCRA, SEBI Act, and Depositories Act into a single legislative framework [Source: Mondaq, “How India’s Securities Markets Code, 2025, Will Reshape Compliance”]
  • NSE operates as a recognized stock exchange under SCRA and is subject to SEBI’s regulatory oversight

9.2 Key Regulations

  • SEBI (Stock Brokers and Sub-Brokers) Regulations, 1992
  • SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015 (LODR)
  • SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015
  • SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices) Regulations, 2003
  • SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008
  • SEBI circulars on algorithmic trading, colocation, and market microstructure

9.3 Market Surveillance

  • Primary surveillance responsibility lies with NSE’s own surveillance department
  • SEBI maintains proactive oversight on market movements and trends
  • NSE conducts real-time surveillance using automated systems to detect abnormal trading patterns, price manipulation, and insider trading
  • Additional Surveillance Measures (ASM) and Graded Surveillance Measures (GSM) applied to securities exhibiting unusual price/volume patterns
  • Periodic surveillance reports circulated to SEBI and the Ministry of Finance

[Source: NSE Official Website, “Exchange Market Surveillance & Regulatory Actions”]

9.4 Reporting Requirements

  • Listed companies must comply with SEBI LODR for periodic and event-based disclosures
  • Trading members submit daily margin and risk reports
  • Large trader reporting and position limits for derivatives
  • Short-selling disclosure requirements

9.5 Cross-border Arrangements

  • SEBI is a signatory to the IOSCO Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding (MMoU) for cross-border cooperation
  • SEBI’s Office of International Affairs liaises with foreign securities regulators, central banks, and judicial authorities
  • Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) access NSE through SEBI’s FPI registration framework
  • NSE IFSC (International Financial Services Centre) subsidiary operates in GIFT City, Gujarat, for offshore trading

9.6 Colocation Controversy & Investigation

The NSE colocation case is one of the most significant regulatory controversies in Indian capital market history:

  • 2010â2014: Certain high-frequency trading (HFT) firms allegedly received preferential access to NSE’s colocation servers, gaining unfair speed advantages in receiving market data. A non-empanelled internet service provider was reportedly allowed to lay dark fiber cables on NSE premises for select brokers.
  • January 2015: Whistleblower complaint filed with SEBI exposing the alleged manipulation.
  • May 2017: SEBI issued show cause notices to NSE and 14 individuals, including former Managing Directors Chitra Ramakrishna and Ravi Narain.
  • 2019: SEBI directed NSE to disgorge INR 625 crore plus interest and imposed an additional penalty of INR 1,000 crore (~USD 131 million combined).
  • January 2023: Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT) largely quashed the 2019 SEBI order, citing contradictory findings and insufficient evidence, and remanded parts back to SEBI.
  • September 2024: SEBI dropped proceedings against NSE and several other officials in the colocation case, citing insufficient evidence of involvement and collusion.
  • Ongoing: CBI (Central Bureau of Investigation) filed a final chargesheet naming 43 accused including Chitra Ramakrishna. The case also involved broader governance concerns at NSE, including revelations about an unknown “Himalayan Yogi” who allegedly influenced NSE decisions through Ramakrishna.
  • IPO Impact: The controversy significantly delayed NSE’s planned IPO, which remains pending as of early 2026.

[Source: Wikipedia, “NSE co-location scam”; Business Standard; Moneylife; Business Today, September 2024; The Print]

11. Recent Developments

System Upgrades (Last 5 Years)

  • May 2024: Tick size reduced to 1 paisa (INR 0.01) for stocks priced below INR 250, first granular tick size in NSE history
  • April 2025: Comprehensive tick size revision across all price bands (6-tier structure)
  • November 2025: Stock options tick size revised separately
  • December 2025: Pre-open session introduced for F&O segment (9:00â9:15 AM)
  • January 2026: Lot size revisions for Nifty 50 (75 to 65) and Bank Nifty (35 to 30)
  • April 11, 2026 (planned): Nanosecond trading system launch â ~100 million TPS, ~1000x speed improvement over current system

New Products / Segments

  • NSE EMERGE SME platform continues to grow (587 companies listed as of December 2024)
  • Social Stock Exchange launched in 2023 for social enterprises and non-profits
  • NSE IFSC in GIFT City, Gujarat, for international trading

Regulatory Changes

  • SEBI algo trading framework effective August 1, 2025, requiring broker-level pre-trade risk controls and algo tagging
  • Securities Markets Code, 2025 â consolidated securities legislation replacing three separate acts
  • SEBI increased STT rates on derivatives effective April 2026 (futures: 0.02% to 0.05%; options premium: 0.10% to 0.15%)
  • Colocation case proceedings largely resolved by SEBI (September 2024), though CBI investigation continues

Strategic Initiatives

  • Colocation infrastructure expansion from ~2,000 to ~4,500 racks
  • NSE IPO remains a key strategic milestone, delayed by colocation controversy; steps resumed after major charges were dropped
  • India’s derivatives market undergoing structural changes with SEBI’s measures to curb speculative retail F&O trading (lot size increases, STT hikes, expiry day restrictions)
