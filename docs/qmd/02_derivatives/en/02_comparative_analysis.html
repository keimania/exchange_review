9. Key Observations & Insights

9.1 Regional Trends and Differences

Americas: - The U.S. dominates global derivatives by both volume and open interest, with CME Group, ICE, and Cboe collectively accounting for the majority of global risk transfer activity. - The U.S. is unique in maintaining a dual regulatory structure (CFTC/SEC), though the vast majority of derivatives activity falls under CFTC jurisdiction. - 0DTE options on Cboe (59% of SPX volume in 2025) represent a structural shift in how institutional and retail investors manage risk, with no comparable phenomenon outside the U.S. - Montreal Exchange (MX) serves as a strong template for a national-scale, single-asset-class derivatives exchange, demonstrating that specialization can drive competitiveness.

Europe: - Eurex and ICE Futures Europe are the two dominant venues, with strong vertical integration (exchange + CCP + data). - EMIR 3.0’s Active Account Requirement signals a political willingness to use regulation to influence clearing location – a development with global implications for CCP competition. - Euronext’s entry into fixed income derivatives (BTP, OAT, Bund futures in September 2025) directly challenges Eurex’s dominance in European government bond futures.

Asia-Pacific: - The region shows the greatest diversity in regulatory approaches, market structures, and product focus. - India’s NSE F&O is the most volume-intensive exchange globally by contract count, but SEBI’s 2024 measures (weekly expiry restrictions, increased lot sizes, higher margins) have deliberately reduced volumes. - China’s three commodity exchanges (SHFE, DCE, ZCE) collectively represent enormous volume but remain largely closed to international participation, with gradual internationalization of select products. - SGX has established itself as Asia’s derivatives gateway, offering offshore access to major Asian equity indices and serving as the key FX futures venue.

9.2 Technology Convergence and Divergence

Convergence: - Sub-100 microsecond latency is now the baseline expectation for Tier 1 exchanges. - Price-Time (FIFO) matching is the near-universal default algorithm. - Binary order entry protocols (CME iLink 3, Eurex ETI, Nasdaq OUCH, Cboe BOE) have replaced FIX-text for latency-sensitive participants. - Full-depth market data via multicast UDP (CME MDP 3.0, Eurex EOBI, SGX ITCH, Cboe Multicast PITCH) is standard.

Divergence: - Cloud migration: CME Group is pioneering cloud-hosted matching engines (Google Cloud, 2026-2028); no other major exchange has announced comparable plans. - Platform strategy: Exchanges are split between in-house development (CME, Eurex, ICE, Cboe, NSE, TAIFEX, Chinese exchanges) and Nasdaq technology licensing (HKEX, SGX, OSE, ASX). - Latency competition: Eurex’s T7 Release 14.0 claims ~2.8 microsecond minimum infrastructure latency, establishing a new benchmark; NSE targets nanosecond-level latency from April 2026.

9.3 Regulatory Harmonization Progress

Substantially harmonized: - OTC derivatives clearing mandates (all G20 jurisdictions) - Trade reporting (UTI, UPI, CDE standards adopted globally, 2022-2025) - CCP risk management (CPMI-IOSCO PFMI) - Cross-border CCP recognition frameworks

Still divergent: - Position limit frameworks (federal statutory in the U.S.; NCA-set in EU; exchange-set in Asia) - Margin methodology (SPAN, SPAN 2, Prisma, STANS, proprietary – no single global standard) - Retail participation controls (India’s SEBI has the strictest approach; China restricts access by net worth; most other jurisdictions have lighter touch) - Market access for foreign participants (ranging from fully open in the U.S./EU/UK to highly restricted in China)

9.4 Areas Where KRX Leads vs. Lags

KRX strengths (relative to global peers): - Vertically integrated exchange + CCP model (similar to HKEX, ASX) - High derivatives trading volume, particularly in KOSPI 200 options (historically the world’s most actively traded single equity index option) - Robust technology infrastructure with competitive latency - Strong retail investor participation and electronic trading culture

Areas for KRX to monitor or improve: - Extended trading hours: KRX now offers ~19 hours/day coverage since the June 2025 night session launch, narrowing the gap with CME (near-24-hour), Eurex (21 hours), SGX (22 hours), and OSE (21 hours with night session). OSE’s night session generating 40.5% of volume demonstrates the value of extended hours. - Product diversification: KRX could expand beyond equity index and single stock derivatives into commodity, FX, and interest rate derivatives more aggressively – following the SGX and HKEX multi-asset model. - Cross-margining: KRX’s vertical silo model limits cross-margining opportunities compared to CME (futures-to-OTC IRS) and Eurex (Prisma cross-market netting). - Margin methodology modernization: The global trend toward VaR-based margining (CME SPAN 2, Eurex Prisma, JSCC VaR) warrants evaluation if KRX still uses scenario-based approaches. - International connectivity: SGX’s GIFT Connect (India) and HKEX’s Swap Connect (China) demonstrate the value of structured cross-border access arrangements. KRX could explore similar connectivity with regional partners. - ESG/Carbon derivatives: Eurex, ICE, and ASX have launched carbon and ESG index derivatives. KRX could consider products aligned with Korea’s emissions trading and ESG mandates. - Crypto derivatives: CME, SGX, Eurex, and ICE have all launched regulated crypto derivatives. KRX could assess market demand for institutional-grade crypto products.
