1. Overview & Profile

Item Details
Official Name Taiwan Futures Exchange Corporation (臺灣期貨交易所)
Abbreviation TAIFEX
Website https://www.taifex.com.tw
Year Established 1997 (first product launched July 21, 1998)
Country / City Taiwan / Taipei
Ownership Structure Non-profit corporation; jointly established by the Taiwan Stock Exchange (TWSE), major domestic banks, and securities firms
Parent Company / Group Independent entity (TWSE is a major shareholder)
Regulatory Authority Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC), Taiwan
Listed Instruments 300+ products across equity index futures/options, single stock futures/options, ETF futures/options, FX futures, commodity futures/options
Clearing House TAIFEX Clearing Department — acts as central counterparty (CCP); designated as QCCP by FSC in November 2020

[Source: TAIFEX — Origin of Establishment; Wikipedia — Taiwan Futures Exchange; TAIFEX PFMI Disclosure Report; AFM — Taiwan Futures Exchange]

Key Historical Milestones

  • 1997 (March): Futures Trading Law enacted, establishing the legal framework for Taiwan’s domestic futures market
  • 1997 (September): Taiwan Futures Exchange Corporation established
  • 1998 (21 July): Launch of first product — TAIEX Futures (TX)
  • 1999 (July): Launch of Electronic Sector Index Futures and Finance Sector Index Futures on TAIFEX’s first anniversary
  • 2001 (9 April): Launch of Mini-TAIEX Futures (MTX)
  • 2001 (24 December): Launch of TAIEX Options (TXO)
  • 2003 (20 January): Launch of Equity Options — first non-index derivatives product
  • 2006 (27 March): Launch of USD-denominated Gold Futures
  • 2010 (25 January): Launch of Single Stock Futures
  • 2017 (15 May): Introduction of after-hours trading session, extending trading hours to 19 hours per day
  • 2016 (December): Upgrade to fifth-generation electronic trading system; new IT center in Banqiao, New Taipei City; capacity doubled to 48,000 orders/second, latency reduced from 800 to 200 microseconds
  • 2019 (24 June): FSC designates TAIFEX as OTC derivatives clearing house
  • 2020 (November): FSC grants TAIFEX Clearing QCCP (qualifying CCP) status
  • 2022 (27 June): Launch of Taiwan Semiconductor 30 Futures and Shipping and Transportation Sector Futures
  • 2024 (9 December): Launch of Taiwan Mid-Cap 100 Futures
  • 2025: Launch of sixth-generation electronic trading system with enhanced processing efficiency and optimized architecture

[Source: TAIFEX — Historical Development; WFE — TAIFEX Marks 25 Years; LSEG — Taiwan Futures Exchange; TAIFEX — Electronic Trading System]

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Auction Mechanisms

Opening Auction (Call Auction): - Pre-open phase (08:30 – 08:45) allows order entry; simulated opening price and best 5 bid/ask prices/volumes are revealed during each simulated match cycle - During the final 2 minutes prior to opening, submitted orders cannot be deleted or modified (to prevent manipulation of the simulated matching information) - At 08:45, accumulated orders are matched at a single equilibrium price via call auction - Calendar spread orders are not accepted prior to market open and do not participate in the opening auction - IOC market orders are accepted prior to open; FOK orders are not accepted during the call auction phase

Continuous Trading: - After the opening auction, orders are executed continuously upon entry into the order book - Price-time priority (FIFO) is the standard matching algorithm

Settlement: - Daily settlement prices determined at market close - Final settlement prices for expiring contracts based on the opening prices of the underlying index on the final settlement day

3.2 Order Types

TAIFEX accepts two primary order types for direct execution: - Market Orders: Execute immediately at the best available price - Limit Orders: Execute at the specified price or better

FCMs may accept additional order types at their own discretion for client-side management, including: market-if-touched, stop, stop limit, one-cancels-the-other (OCO), market-on-close, straight cancel, and cancel-replace orders.

3.3 Order Validity

  • ROD (Rest of Day): Valid for the remainder of the current session
  • IOC (Immediate-or-Cancel): Execute immediately for available quantity; unfilled portion is cancelled
  • FOK (Fill-or-Kill): Must be filled entirely and immediately, or the entire order is cancelled (not available during opening auction)

8. Risk Controls & Circuit Breakers

8.1 Daily Price Limits

TAIFEX imposes daily price limits on all products. For major equity index products:

Product Category Daily Price Limit Notes
Equity Index Futures (TAIEX, Mini-TAIEX, etc.) ±10% from previous settlement price Aligned with TWSE stock market daily limit
Equity Index Options Premium limits linked to underlying ±10% move Strike-dependent
Single Stock Futures/Options ±10% from previous settlement price Aligned with underlying stock daily limit
FX Futures Product-specific bands Varies by currency pair [unverified]
Commodity Futures (Gold, Brent) Product-specific bands Varies by contract [unverified]

8.2 Position Limits

  • TAIFEX sets position limits for each product category, with different limits for:
    • Individual accounts (natural persons)
    • Institutional accounts
    • Market makers
  • When irregularities are found in financial condition or open positions of clearing members, TAIFEX is authorized under Articles 6-1 and 7 of the Rules Governing Surveillance of Market Positions to impose limits on new positions
  • Hedge exemptions available upon application to TAIFEX

8.3 Intraday Risk Management

  • Mark-to-Market: Open positions are marked to market at current market prices or settlement prices to calculate real-time gains/losses
  • Intraday Margin Calls: Performed at 09:30, 11:00, 12:30, and 15:00, or more frequently during periods of drastic price volatility
  • Dynamic Price Banding: 2% outright rejection threshold based on the most recent closing price of the underlying index (for certain products)

8.4 Order Size Limits

  • TAIFEX imposes maximum order size limits per single order for each product
  • Limits are published on the TAIFEX website and updated periodically

[Source: TAIFEX — Position Limit and Exemption; TAIFEX — Intraday Margin Calls; TAIFEX — Order Size Limits; TAIFEX — FAQ Trading]

9. Regulatory Framework

9.1 Primary Regulator

  • Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC): Taiwan’s consolidated financial regulator overseeing banking, securities, futures, and insurance markets; supervises TAIFEX as the sole licensed futures exchange and derivatives clearing house in Taiwan

9.2 Key Legislation

Regulation Description
Futures Trading Law (1997) Primary legislation governing Taiwan’s futures market; established the legal framework for domestic futures exchanges, FCMs, clearing, and trading
Securities and Exchange Act Governs underlying securities markets; intersects with derivatives regulation for single stock futures/options
FSC Regulations on Futures Trading Detailed rules on margin, position limits, member requirements, and investor protection

9.3 Clearing Member Structure

TAIFEX Clearing offers three membership statuses:

Membership Type Description
Individual Clearing Member Clears only its own proprietary and customer trades
General Clearing Member Clears its own trades and trades of non-clearing trading members
Special Clearing Member Dedicated clearing entity that does not trade but provides clearing services

9.4 CCP and Default Waterfall

  • TAIFEX Clearing acts as the central counterparty (CCP) for all exchange-traded derivatives
  • TAIFEX was designated as a QCCP by the FSC in November 2020
  • Default waterfall includes: defaulted member’s margin/collateral, TAIFEX’s own reserves, and mutualized default fund contributions from non-defaulting clearing members (subject to cap)
  • TAIFEX publishes PFMI (Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures) disclosure reports in accordance with CPMI-IOSCO standards

9.5 Cross-Border Recognition

  • U.S. CFTC: TAIFEX has applied for DCO exemption with the CFTC, supported by detailed PFMI disclosure documentation
  • International Cooperation: TAIFEX maintains regulatory cooperation arrangements with other market regulators

D3. Margin Framework

Margin Methodology

Item Details
Methodology SPAN (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk)
Margin Calculation Risk coefficient based on price movements within certain periods, anti-procyclicality factors, and other considerations to cover 99% of two-day price volatility risk
Initial Margin Clearing margin multiplied by a TAIFEX-determined ratio (typically ~115–135% of clearing margin) [unverified]
Maintenance Margin Set as a percentage of initial margin (typically ~75–88% of initial margin) [unverified]
Variation Margin Daily mark-to-market settlement of gains and losses
Margin Announcement Initial and maintenance margin amounts are periodically announced by TAIFEX and may be adjusted during periods of high volatility

Margin Call Process

  1. End-of-Day Settlement: TAIFEX Clearing calculates clearing margin, variation margin, and clearing fees for each clearing member after market close
  2. Intraday Mark-to-Market: Performed at 09:30, 11:00, 12:30, and 15:00 (or more frequently during high volatility)
  3. Margin Deficit: If a client’s equity falls below the maintenance margin level, the FCM issues a margin call requiring the client to restore equity to the initial margin level
  4. Margin Call Deadline: Typically by market open of the next trading day; positions may be liquidated if margin calls are not met [unverified]

Acceptable Collateral

  • Cash (NTD and major foreign currencies)
  • Government bonds
  • Other securities approved by TAIFEX (subject to haircuts)

Day Trading Margin

  • Day trading positions can be included with the rest of the portfolio for SPAN margin calculations
  • Reduced margin rates may apply for confirmed intraday positions [unverified]
