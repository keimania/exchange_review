Key Historical Milestones

  • 1870: New York Cotton Exchange (NYCE) founded – predecessor of ICE Futures U.S.
  • 1882: Coffee Exchange of the City of New York founded
  • 1980: International Petroleum Exchange (IPE) founded in London as an open-outcry energy exchange
  • 1998: New York Board of Trade (NYBOT) formed by the merger of NYCE and the Coffee, Sugar & Cocoa Exchange (CSCE)
  • 2000: Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) founded in Atlanta, Georgia, by Jeffrey Sprecher as an electronic over-the-counter (OTC) energy marketplace
  • 2001: ICE acquires the International Petroleum Exchange (IPE) in London, entering the exchange-traded futures market
  • 2005 (April 7): IPE fully transitions from open-outcry to electronic trading; renamed ICE Futures (later ICE Futures Europe)
  • 2005: ICE goes public on the NYSE (ticker: ICE)
  • 2007: ICE acquires the New York Board of Trade (NYBOT); renamed ICE Futures U.S.
  • 2007: ICE acquires ChemConnect (chemical marketplace) and Winnipeg Commodity Exchange (now ICE Futures Canada)
  • 2008: ICE Clear Europe established as a central counterparty clearing house in London
  • 2010: ICE acquires the Climate Exchange (including European Climate Exchange and Chicago Climate Exchange)
  • 2012 (December): ICE announces acquisition of NYSE Euronext for $8.2 billion
  • 2013 (November 13): ICE completes acquisition of NYSE Euronext (~$11 billion total value); acquires LIFFE (London International Financial Futures and Options Exchange) and NYSE
  • 2014 (June): ICE spins off continental European Euronext exchanges via IPO; retains LIFFE operations, integrating them into ICE Futures Europe
  • 2016: ICE launches ICE Benchmark Administration (IBA), becoming administrator of LIBOR, ICE Swap Rate, and other benchmarks
  • 2018: ICE acquires MERSCORP Holdings (mortgage electronic registry); launches Bakkt digital asset platform
  • 2020: ICE acquires Ellie Mae (mortgage technology) for $11 billion; acquires Simplifile
  • 2022: ICE acquires Black Knight (mortgage technology) for $13.1 billion
  • 2025: Record derivatives volumes (2.4 billion contracts); launch of EUA 2 (EU Carbon Allowance 2) futures; launch of battery materials futures (lithium, cobalt, spodumene); ICE invests up to $2 billion in Polymarket
  • 2026 (ongoing): Launch of regulated cryptocurrency futures based on CoinDesk Indices (seven new contracts); strategic investment in OKX ($200 million); datacenter DR migration from Atlanta to Mahwah

2.5 Market Maker / Liquidity Provider Programs

ICE operates liquidity provider programs for both IFEU and IFUS:

  • Market Maker Programs: Designated market makers provide continuous two-sided quotes in specific products in exchange for fee rebates and incentives
  • Liquidity Provider Programmes (IFEU): Published in the IFEU fee schedule; programmes cover energy, soft commodities, interest rate, and equity index products with tiered rebate structures
  • Fee Reduction Incentives: Volume-based and quoting obligation-based fee reductions for qualifying participants
  • eRFQ (Electronic Request for Quote): Available for options and less-liquid products; market makers obligated to respond within specified parameters

[Source: ICE, “ICE Futures Europe Exchange & Clearing Fees 2026”, https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures/IFEU_Exchange_Clearing_Fees.pdf]

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Continuous Trading

ICE’s derivatives markets operate on a continuous electronic order-matching basis during regular trading hours:

  • Price-Time Priority: Orders are matched on a strict price-time priority basis – best price first, then earliest timestamp at the same price
  • Central Limit Order Book (CLOB): All orders enter a central limit order book; visible (displayed) and non-displayed portions handled according to order type
  • Anonymous Trading: Counterparty identities are not disclosed on the order book; all trades are cleared through ICE’s clearing houses

3.2 Auction Mechanisms

  • Opening Auction (selected products): Pre-open period where orders accumulate without matching, followed by a single-price uncrossing auction
  • Closing/Settlement Period: Products such as Brent crude use a weighted average of trades during a defined settlement window (e.g., Brent settlement based on VWAP during a 2-minute window at 19:28 London time)
  • Intraday Auctions: Not a standard feature for most ICE products [unverified]

3.3 Spread Trading

  • Calendar Spreads: Directly tradable as instruments; separate order book for spread markets
  • Inter-Commodity Spreads: Supported for related products (e.g., Brent/WTI spread, crack spreads)
  • Strategy Trading: Complex multi-leg strategies can be submitted as a single order

3.4 Implied Pricing

  • Implied-in and Implied-out: ICE supports implied pricing for spread markets, deriving synthetic prices from outright and spread orders to enhance liquidity [unverified]

[Source: ICE, “ICE Futures Europe Trading Procedures”, https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/contractregs/185_XX_TRADING_PROCEDURES.pdf]

4. Order Types

4.1 Standard Order Types

Order Type Description
Limit Order Order to buy/sell at a specified price or better
Market Order Order to buy/sell at the best available price; immediate execution
Stop Order Triggered when market price reaches the stop price; becomes a market order
Stop Limit Order Triggered at stop price; becomes a limit order at the specified limit price

4.2 Advanced Order Types

Order Type Description
Iceberg / Reserve Order Only a portion of the order is displayed; remainder hidden and replenished
Market-if-Touched (MIT) Converts to a market order when a specified price is touched
Discretionary Order Limit order with a hidden discretionary range for price improvement [unverified]

4.3 Time-in-Force Conditions

Condition Description
Day Valid for the current trading session only
Good Till Cancelled (GTC) Remains active until filled or cancelled
Good Till Date (GTD) Remains active until a specified date
Fill or Kill (FOK) Must be filled entirely and immediately or cancelled
Immediate or Cancel (IOC) Any portion not immediately filled is cancelled

4.4 Order Modification & Cancellation

  • Orders can be modified (price, quantity) or cancelled at any time during the trading session
  • Modifications that change price lose time priority
  • Quantity reductions retain time priority
  • Mass Cancellation: Participants can cancel all orders via a single “cancel all” message (kill switch functionality)

[Source: ICE, “ICE Futures U.S. Trading Rules”, https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/rulebooks/futures_us/4_Trading.pdf]

Market data subscriber fees are published annually (denominated in USD). Fees apply per terminal/user for real-time data access. Separate fees for display and non-display (algorithmic) usage.

8. Risk Controls & Circuit Breakers

8.1 Interval Price Limits (IPL) – Circuit Breaker

ICE uses Interval Price Limits (IPLs) as a temporary circuit breaker mechanism on its electronic platform:

  • If an order crosses the IPL level for a particular futures contract, a market hold (trading halt) is triggered for a pre-set period
  • IPLs slow the rate of price movement in a given direction, preventing aberrant price spikes
  • All IPL parameters (price bands, hold duration) may be adjusted by the exchange based on prevailing market conditions
  • After the hold period expires, trading resumes and new IPLs are recalculated

8.2 Daily Price Limits (DPL)

  • ICE Futures U.S. applies Daily Price Limits to selected contracts:
    • Cotton No. 2 – DPL with expandable limits (~50% wider than standard)
    • FCOJ-A – DPL with expandable limits
    • Canola (ICE Futures Canada) – DPL
  • Most energy and interest rate products do not have daily price limits

8.3 Reasonability Limits

  • Hard Limits: Pre-determined price parameters that reject orders outside the specified range on entry (buy orders above the upper limit and sell orders below the lower limit are rejected)
  • No Cancellation Ranges: Soft limits where trades executed within the specified range will not normally be adjusted or cancelled by the exchange

8.4 Pre-Trade Risk Controls

  • Maximum Order Size: Configurable per product/participant
  • Maximum Position Size: Monitored in real-time
  • Credit Controls: Clearing member-level risk limits enforced at the matching engine level
  • Cancel-on-Disconnect: Automatic cancellation of resting orders if a participant’s connection drops [unverified]
  • Kill Switch: Mass cancellation functionality allowing participants or clearing members to cancel all resting orders immediately

8.5 Position Limits

ICE Futures U.S.: - Exchange-established speculative position limits per CFTC Part 150 and exchange rules - Position limits apply on both an intraday and end-of-day basis - Hedge exemptions available upon application - Aggregation of positions under common ownership or control required - Violations constitute breach of Exchange Rule 6.13 or 6.20

ICE Futures Europe: - Position management controls under UK FCA rules and EU/UK MiFID II framework - Position limits and accountability levels set per contract - ESMA opinions on position limits for significant contracts (e.g., Dutch TTF)

[Source: ICE, “ICE’s supervision of markets and price controls”, https://www.ice.com/insights/exchanges/why-financial-participants-matter-to-the-commodity-markets/ice-supervision-of-markets] [Source: ICE, “Guidance on Position Limits”, September 2025, https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures_us/ICE_Futures_US_PositionLimitGuidance.pdf]

  • UK: Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (FSMA); UK MiFID II / MiFIR; UK EMIR (for clearing)
  • U.S.: Commodity Exchange Act (CEA); CFTC Regulations Parts 1-190; Dodd-Frank Act Title VII (swaps/OTC derivatives oversight)
  • Cross-border: ICE Futures Europe is registered with CFTC as a Foreign Board of Trade (FBOT), allowing direct access by U.S. participants for certain products

9.3 Self-Regulatory Obligations

  • Both IFEU and IFUS maintain self-regulatory responsibilities under their respective jurisdictions
  • Each exchange maintains its own rulebook, compliance, and market surveillance functions
  • ICE Futures Europe Market Regulation: Dedicated market surveillance team monitoring for market abuse, manipulation, and rule violations
  • ICE Futures U.S. Regulatory Division: Enforces exchange rules; conducts trade practice surveillance; oversees position limit compliance

9.4 Reporting

  • Large Trader Reporting: Required under CFTC rules for U.S. markets; IFEU reports to FCA under UK transaction reporting regime
  • EMIR Trade Reporting: Cleared trades reported to trade repositories under UK EMIR
  • Dodd-Frank Reporting: Swaps and OTC derivatives reported to Swap Data Repositories (SDRs) in the U.S.

B. Margin Framework

ICE Risk Model (IRM) – Legacy

  • Methodology: Scenario-based margining (similar in concept to SPAN); evaluates portfolio risk across a range of price and volatility scenarios
  • Application: Used across most ICE clearing houses for futures and options initial margin calculation

ICE Risk Model 2 (IRM 2) – New Standard

  • Methodology: Filtered Historical Simulation Value at Risk (FHS VaR)
  • Confidence Level: 99% (calibrated to cover expected close-out cost under normal market conditions)
  • Key Features:
    • Full portfolio-based margining with diversification benefits
    • Responsive to changing market conditions via GARCH-type volatility filtering
    • Replaces scenario-based approach with empirically-driven risk measurement
  • Rollout Status:
    • January 2022: Launched for equity index futures (ICE Clear U.S.)
    • 2025: Extended to energy futures and options (1,000+ contracts including oil, natural gas, LNG, power, emissions, freight)
    • Further asset class expansion ongoing

Variation Margin

  • Collected/paid daily based on mark-to-market changes
  • Intraday margin calls may be issued during periods of high volatility

Margin Call Process

  • Initial margin posted by clearing members on behalf of their clients
  • Intraday margin calls if positions move adversely beyond threshold
  • Clearing members responsible for collecting from end-clients

C. Block Trading and EFP/EFS Rules

Block Trades

  • Privately negotiated transactions executed apart from the public auction market
  • Must meet minimum quantity thresholds (vary by product)
  • Must be executed at a price that is fair and reasonable in light of transaction size and comparable market prices
  • Submitted via the ICE Block system
  • Reported to the exchange and cleared through the relevant clearing house

Exchange for Physical (EFP)

  • Two-legged transaction: one leg is a futures position, the other is a corresponding physical commodity position
  • Both parties must have a bona fide physical position
  • Must be submitted via ICE Block system

Exchange for Swap (EFS)

  • Two-legged transaction: one leg is a futures position, the other is a corresponding OTC swap/derivative position
  • Related position must bear genuine economic risk

Exchange for Risk (EFR) / Exchange of Futures for Related Positions (EFRP)

  • Umbrella category covering EFP, EFS, and EOO (Exchange of Options for Options)
  • Key Requirements:
    • Genuine economic risk must be present
    • “Transitory” EFRPs (where execution of one is contingent on another between same parties to offset related positions) are prohibited
    • Registration may be refused if ICE is not satisfied with the commercial rationale
    • EFRP transactions must be submitted via ICE Block system
