2.5 Market Maker / Liquidity Provider Programs

Euronext operates liquidity provision programs tailored per market and product class:

  • Primary Market Makers (PMM): Continuous two-sided quoting obligations across assigned products; eligible for fee rebates and stipends
  • Competitive Market Makers (CMM): Supplementary liquidity providers with reduced obligations
  • MiFID II Market Maker Schemes: Members meeting quoting obligations during Stressed Market Conditions (SMC) receive incentives of EUR 50/month per instrument (80% time threshold), capped at EUR 550/month
  • Commodity Options LP: 100% trading fee rebate; top performer receives EUR 5,000 monthly stipend
  • Power Futures LP: Trading fee rebate up to 100% and/or monthly stipend up to EUR 10,000
  • Mass Quote functionality: Allows market makers to submit batches of two-sided orders across multiple series in a single transaction
  • Pivot method: Flexibility to simultaneously adjust volume and spread within 50% limit for continuous quoting and RFQ obligations

[Source: Euronext, “Liquidity provision”, https://www.euronext.com/en/trading/membership/liquidity-provision] [Source: Euronext, “Market Making Scheme for Derivatives Markets”, https://connect2.euronext.com/sites/default/files/documentation/membership/Euronext%20Market%20Making%20Scheme%20for%20Derivatives%20Markets%20-%20Nov19%20(1).pdf]

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Auction Mechanisms

Euronext derivatives markets employ the following auction types within the Optiq platform:

  • Opening Auction: Orders collected during a call phase; indicative prices displayed. Uncrossing occurs at the price maximizing executable volume. Applies to all derivatives products at market open
  • Closing Auction: Determines official closing/settlement prices; similar call-and-uncross mechanics
  • Volatility Interruption (Circuit Breaker Auction): Triggered when potential trade price breaches dynamic or static price collars. Trading enters a reservation period (auction call phase) allowing orderly price discovery before resumption
  • Intraday Auctions: Scheduled auctions for specific products to provide additional price discovery points [unverified for derivatives – primarily used in cash equities]

3.2 Continuous Trading

  • Central Limit Order Book (CLOB): Fully electronic order-driven market for all derivatives products
  • Continuous matching: Orders matched throughout the trading session at the best available price
  • Market makers: Supplement order book liquidity via continuous quotes; market is order-driven, not quote-driven
  • Request for Cross (RFC): Facility allowing members to cross client orders on-exchange with pre-arranged counterparties, subject to price improvement rules and minimum exposure to the order book

3.3 Price Discovery

  • Primary price discovery through opening auction and continuous order book
  • Auction uncrossing: Maximum executable volume, then minimum surplus, then reference price as tiebreaker
  • Settlement prices: For index futures, calculated using averaging methodology near close (e.g., CAC 40: arithmetic mean of all index values between 15:40 and 16:00 CET on expiry day, rounded to two decimal places)
  • Indicative prices: Displayed during auction call phases

3.4 Order Priority Rules

  • Price-time priority: Standard priority for all Euronext derivatives products – best price first, then earliest timestamp at the same price
  • Market orders have priority over limit orders at any price level
  • Visible over hidden: At the same price, visible quantities take priority over iceberg reserve quantities
  • Pro-rata allocation: Not standard on Euronext derivatives; price-time priority is the default matching algorithm [unverified – specific products may have alternative allocation]

4. Order Types

4.1 Standard Order Types

Order Type Description
Market Order Executed immediately at the best available price; no price limit specified
Limit Order Specifies maximum buy / minimum sell price; unfilled portion rests in the order book
Stop Market Order Triggered when a specified price is reached; released as a market order
Stop Limit Order Triggered when a specified price is reached; released as a limit order at the specified limit price

4.2 Advanced Order Types

Order Type Description
Iceberg Order Only a portion of total order volume is visible in the order book
Market-to-Limit (MTL) Market order that, after partial execution, converts remaining quantity to a limit order at the last executed price
Spread / Strategy Orders Calendar spreads, straddles, strangles, and other multi-leg strategies submitted as a single order
Opening-Auction-Only Active only during the opening auction; cancelled if unfilled
Closing-Auction-Only Active only during the closing auction

4.3 Time-in-Force (TIF) Options

TIF Description
Day Valid for the current trading day only
GTC (Good-Till-Cancelled) Remains active until explicitly cancelled
GTDate (Good-Till-Date) Remains active until a specified date
IOC (Immediate-Or-Cancel) Executes immediately as much as possible; unfilled quantity is cancelled
FOK (Fill-Or-Kill) Entire order must be filled immediately or the entire order is cancelled

4.4 Order Modification & Cancellation

  • Orders can be modified (price, quantity) or cancelled during appropriate trading phases
  • Price changes or quantity increases result in new time priority
  • Quantity decreases preserve existing time priority
  • Mass cancellation available for all orders in a product or across all products
  • Self-Trade Prevention (STP) functionality available to prevent unintended self-trading

5. Trading System Technology – Optiq

5.1 Overview

Item Details
System Name Optiq
Developer Euronext (in-house development)
Architecture Modular, partition-based architecture with Order Entry Gateway (OEG) and Matching Engine (ME) components
Persistence Layer Confluent Platform with Apache Kafka
Protocols SBE (Simple Binary Encoding) and FIX 5.0 for order entry; SBE over UDP/IP for market data
Latency ~15 microseconds average order roundtrip; ~15 microseconds for market data dissemination
Throughput 1 million+ messages per second ingestion capacity
Jitter Minimal standard deviation – architecture designed for predictable, stable latencies
Asset Coverage Unified platform for cash equities and all derivatives (financial, commodity, fixed income)

5.2 Architecture

  • Modular service structure: Flexible, modular design allowing independent scaling of components
  • Harmonised messaging: Unified cash and derivatives messaging model on a single platform
  • Order Entry Gateway (OEG): Entry point for order submission; supports SBE and FIX 5.0 protocols
  • Matching Engine (ME): Core matching component processing orders with price-time priority
  • Market Data Gateway (MDG): Disseminates real-time market data via SBE over UDP multicast
  • Kafka-based persistence: Confluent Platform/Apache Kafka underpins the persistence layer, providing reliable message streaming and replay capabilities

Since going live for derivatives, Optiq has processed more than 14 billion messages and 18.8 million trades. The platform achieved a tenfold reduction in latency compared to the legacy LIFFE CONNECT system and unlimited overall capacity scaling.

  • The Bergamo data center is entirely powered by renewable energy (solar photovoltaic and hydroelectric)

8. Risk Controls & Circuit Breakers

8.1 Trading Safeguards Philosophy

Euronext’s approach is to only halt trading under extreme circumstances, relying on a comprehensive set of trading safeguards rather than circuit breakers alone. The philosophy is to detect unusual events and prevent disorderly markets while maximizing continuous trading.

8.2 Price Collar Mechanisms

  • Dynamic Collar: Based on the dynamic reference price (last traded price); defines a permitted price range around the most recent trade. The dynamic reference price updates after each trade
  • Static Collar: Based on the static reference price (typically the previous day’s closing price or opening auction price); defines a wider permitted price range. The static reference remains fixed unless manually changed by Euronext Market Operations
  • Reservation Period: When the potential trade price breaches a collar, trading enters a reservation period (volatility interruption) – effectively an intraday auction. This gives market participants time to review orders before trading resumes
  • Trade Price Validation (TPV): Additional safeguard layer for derivatives markets; validates trade prices before execution to prevent aberrant trades

8.3 Pre-Trade Risk Controls – Euronext RiskGuard

Euronext RiskGuard is a pre-trade risk management service for derivatives (and cash) markets, available via FIX 5.0 API and web-based GUI:

Control Description
Order Size Limit Maximum number of contracts per order; applies to new orders, revisions, and quotes; orders exceeding the limit are rejected
Max Order Quantity Per-contract limits for buy and/or sell orders, configurable per member or trader group
Daily Maximum Exposure Position Position monitoring with email alerts when limits are approached or breached
Kill Switch Clearing Members can immediately halt all trading activity for a trading firm; all open orders and quotes are pulled; new orders rejected until explicitly reinstated by the Clearing Member
Self-Trade Prevention (STP) Prevents unintended matching of orders from the same member/trader

Scope: RiskGuard controls apply to financial and commodity derivatives markets. Delta-neutral trades, RFCs, and wholesale trades are excluded from certain controls.

[Source: Euronext, “Trading safeguards on the Euronext markets”, https://www.euronext.com/en/trading/market-quality/trading-safeguards-euronext-markets] [Source: Euronext, “Euronext RiskGuard Service General Overview”, February 2019, https://www.euronext.com/sites/default/files/2019-04/euronext_riskguard_service_general_overview_feb_2019_0.pdf] [Source: Euronext, “Euronext RiskGuard”, https://www.euronext.com/en/trading/trading-services/riskguard]

9. Regulatory Framework

9.1 College of Euronext Regulators

Euronext’s unique multi-jurisdictional structure is overseen by a College of Euronext Regulators established via a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU). The college coordinates supervision across all Euronext markets:

Regulator Jurisdiction Market
AMF (Autorite des Marches Financiers) France Euronext Paris, MATIF
AFM (Autoriteit Financiele Markten) Netherlands Euronext Amsterdam
FSMA (Financial Services and Markets Authority) Belgium Euronext Brussels
CMVM (Comissao do Mercado de Valores Mobiliarios) Portugal Euronext Lisbon
CONSOB (Commissione Nazionale per le Societa e la Borsa) Italy Euronext Milan / Borsa Italiana
Finanstilsynet Norway Euronext Oslo / Oslo Bors
CBI (Central Bank of Ireland) Ireland Euronext Dublin

9.2 Key Regulations

  • MiFID II / MiFIR: Euronext derivatives markets are Regulated Markets under MiFID II. Key implications include:
    • Pre- and post-trade transparency requirements for derivatives
    • Position limits on commodity derivatives (set by national competent authorities with ESMA coordination)
    • Transaction reporting obligations
    • Algorithmic trading requirements (including market-making obligations during stressed conditions)
    • Best execution obligations
  • EMIR (European Market Infrastructure Regulation): Governs CCP supervision (Euronext Clearing supervised by CONSOB with ESMA coordination); mandates central clearing for certain OTC derivatives classes
  • MAR (Market Abuse Regulation): Prohibits insider trading and market manipulation; Euronext operates market surveillance systems
  • Benchmark Regulation (BMR): Applies to indices used as derivatives underlyings

9.3 Surveillance & Reporting

  • Euronext operates real-time market surveillance across all markets
  • Transaction reporting to national competent authorities via Euronext ARM (Approved Reporting Mechanism) service
  • Trade reporting for MiFID II post-trade transparency via Euronext APA (Approved Publication Arrangement)
  • Suspicious transaction and order reports (STORs) submitted to regulators

9.4 Cross-Border Coordination

  • The College of Regulators ensures consistent rule application across Euronext’s seven markets
  • Euronext Rule Book (Harmonised Rules) applies uniformly; supplemented by market-specific rules for each national market
  • Single operational framework across markets facilitates cross-border trading and clearing

[Source: Euronext, “Regulation”, https://www.euronext.com/en/regulation] [Source: Euronext, “Regulatory bodies”, https://www.euronext.com/en/trade/join-euronext/regulatory-framework]

11. Recent Developments

11.1 Euronext Clearing Migration (2023-2024)

The most transformative recent development was the migration of derivatives clearing from LCH SA to Euronext Clearing (formerly CC&G):

  • November 2023: Cash equity clearing migrated to Euronext Clearing
  • July 15, 2024: Commodity derivatives clearing migrated
  • September 9, 2024: All Euronext financial derivatives (including CAC 40, AEX futures, index options, single name options) migrated to Euronext Clearing – completing the full transition from LCH SA
  • Key benefit: ~20% margin efficiencies through VaR-based methodology and unified equity/derivatives default fund enabling cross-margining
  • Strategic significance: Euronext now controls end-to-end trading and clearing infrastructure, internalizing clearing revenue

D3. Margin Framework

Euronext Clearing Margin Methodology

Following the migration from LCH SA (completed September 2024), all Euronext derivatives are cleared through Euronext Clearing (formerly CC&G), which employs a VaR-based margin methodology:

Item Details
Methodology Dynamic (auto-updating) VaR-like portfolio margining
Risk Measure Historical Simulation Expected Shortfall (ES) based on cash flow mapping on sovereign zero curves
Confidence Level 99.0% for most products [unverified – standard EMIR requirement]
Lookback Period Based on historical market data [specific period not publicly disclosed]
Update Frequency Dynamic/auto-updating based on market conditions
Procyclicality EMIR-compliant measures to prevent excessive procyclical margin increases

Margin Add-Ons

Euronext Clearing applies additional margin add-ons to address portfolio-specific risks beyond mid-price risk:

  • Idiosyncratic risk: Risk specific to individual securities
  • Liquidity risk: Accounts for varying liquidity across products
  • Concentration risk: Additional margins for large concentrated positions
  • Settlement risk: Risk related to settlement timing
  • Repo risk: For positions involving repo transactions

Methodology Timeline

Date Change
Pre-October 2023 MARS (SPAN-like methodology) used for Italian equity and derivatives
June 2022 VaR-based methodology goes live for Italian, Portuguese, Spanish, Irish government bonds
October 2023 VaR-based methodology goes live for Italian equity, ETF, and equity derivatives (replacing MARS/SPAN-like model)
September 2024 VaR-based methodology extended to all Euronext financial derivatives following migration from LCH SA

Cross-Margining

  • Unified default fund: Single equity and derivatives default fund facilitating cross-margining between cash and derivatives positions
  • Margin efficiency: ~20% margin reduction achieved through portfolio margining across equity and derivatives products within the same default fund

D5. Block Trading / EFP / EFS / Wholesale Trading

Euronext operates several off-book and wholesale trading facilities for derivatives:

Facility Description
TCS (Trade Confirmation System) Publication of regulated market trades including negotiated deals, block trades, and VWAP trades; used for post-trade registration and reporting
Block Execution Service (BES) Enables trading members to report off-book wholesale trades to the exchange for registration
EFP (Exchange for Physical) Simultaneous exchange of a futures position for a basket of underlying shares weighted according to the index; can be entered directly on the Optiq order book via the AMR (Automated Market Reference) strategy
EFS (Exchange for Swaps) Exchange of futures for swap positions; eliminates execution risk associated with trading in the Central Order Book
RFC (Request for Cross) Facility for crossing client orders on-exchange with pre-arranged counterparties; subject to price improvement rules (the cross must be at a price equal to or better than the best bid/offer in the order book)
Auto-match RFC Automated matching of RFC orders, introduced March 2024

Key Rules: - All wholesale trades must be reported through TCS and are subject to post-trade transparency requirements under MiFID II - Minimum block trade sizes apply per product (defined in Euronext Trading Procedures) - EFP/EFS transactions must involve a genuine related position in the underlying or swap

[Source: Euronext, “Wholesale trading & RFC”, https://www.euronext.com/en/for-investors/financial-derivatives/wholesale-trading] [Source: Euronext, “Euronext Derivatives Markets: Trading Procedures”, https://www.euronext.com/sites/default/files/2025-02/trading_procedures_for_derivatives_-_dsp_fair_value_and_deferred_publication.pdf]

Summary

Euronext Derivatives is a pan-European derivatives exchange operating across seven markets (Amsterdam, Brussels, Dublin, Lisbon, Milan, Oslo, Paris) on the unified Optiq trading platform. Its product range spans equity index futures/options, single stock derivatives, commodity futures/options (notably Milling Wheat as a European benchmark), dividend derivatives, total return futures, and newly launched fixed income derivatives.

The period 2023-2024 marked a transformative era with two landmark migrations: (1) Borsa Italiana’s derivatives markets moved to the Optiq platform (April 2024), and (2) all derivatives clearing migrated from LCH SA to Euronext Clearing (completed September 2024). These changes gave Euronext full vertical control over its trading and clearing stack, enabling ~20% margin efficiencies through VaR-based portfolio margining and a unified default fund.

Optiq delivers ~15 microsecond order roundtrip latency with 1M+ messages/second capacity, using SBE and FIX 5.0 protocols from the Bergamo, Italy data center. The exchange continues to expand its product offering with fixed income derivatives (launched September 2025), mini options, and a retail derivatives strategy.
