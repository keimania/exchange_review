1. Overview & Profile

Item Details
Official Name Singapore Exchange Derivatives Trading Limited (SGX-DT) / Singapore Exchange Derivatives Clearing Limited (SGX-DC)
Abbreviation SGX Derivatives
Website https://www.sgx.com/derivatives
Year Established 1984 (as SIMEX — Singapore International Monetary Exchange); 1999 merged into SGX; SGX-DT and SGX-DC as separate entities within SGX Group
Country / City Singapore / Singapore (2 Shenton Way, SGX Centre 1)
Ownership Structure Part of SGX Group, which is publicly listed on its own exchange (ticker: S68)
Parent Company / Group Singapore Exchange Limited (SGX Group)
Regulatory Authority Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)
Annual Revenue (FY2025) Equities — Derivatives: SGD 345.9 million (+13.8% YoY); Currencies & Commodities: SGD 312.5 million (+8.6% YoY); SGX Group total: SGD 1.30 billion (record)
Annual Derivatives Volume (CY2025) 329 million contracts (record) across equities, FX, and commodities
Average Daily Volume (Feb 2025) ~1.4 million contracts/day (derivatives DAV +8% YoY)
Listed Instruments Futures and options across equity indices (>15 Asian markets), FX (6+ currency pairs), commodities (iron ore, freight, rubber, coking coal, steel), interest rates (JGB, APAC bonds), and digital assets (BTC, ETH perpetual futures)
Clearing House SGX Derivatives Clearing Limited (SGX-DC) — central counterparty

[Source: SGX Group FY2025 Annual Report; SGX Group market statistics press releases, February 2025; TipRanks, “SGX Ends 2025 With Record Derivatives Volumes”, January 2026]

Key Historical Milestones

  • 1984: Singapore International Monetary Exchange (SIMEX) established as Asia’s first financial futures exchange; launched Eurodollar futures via mutual offset with CME
  • 1986: SIMEX launches Nikkei 225 futures — pioneering offshore access to Japanese equity markets; first offshore listing of a major Asian equity index
  • 1999 (1 December): SGX formed through merger of Stock Exchange of Singapore (SES), SIMEX, and Securities Clearing and Computer Services Pte Ltd, enabled by the Exchanges (Demutualisation and Merger) Act 1999
  • 2000: SGX listed on its own Mainboard (23 November); established SGX Derivatives Trading (SGX-DT) and SGX Securities Trading (SGX-ST) as separate arms
  • 2002: Launched Japanese Government Bond (JGB) futures
  • 2003: Technology partnership with Nasdaq for trading systems commenced
  • 2006: Launched FTSE China A50 Index Futures — the first and only USD-denominated A-share futures contract globally
  • 2007: Launched AsiaClear, OTC clearing service for commodities; launched iron ore swaps
  • 2009: Launched SGX iron ore futures — now the global benchmark for seaborne iron ore derivatives
  • 2010: Proposed merger with ASX for AUD 8.4 billion; blocked by Australian government in 2011
  • 2013: Acquired 24.9% stake in the Baltic Exchange (full acquisition in 2017)
  • 2014: Launched FX futures (USD/CNH, INR/USD, and other Asian currency pairs)
  • 2015: Announced next-generation derivatives platform (Titan) using Nasdaq Genium INET technology
  • 2016–2017: SGX Titan DT/DC derivatives trading and clearing platform goes live, replacing legacy systems
  • 2018: Launched FTSE-SGX indices; expanded iron ore and commodity suite
  • 2019: Upgraded securities trading engine to Nasdaq multi-matching engine
  • 2020: Extended FX derivatives product suite; record iron ore volumes
  • 2022: Signed GIFT Connect agreement with NSE (India) for Nifty derivatives transition
  • 2023 (3 July): NSE IX-SGX GIFT Connect became fully operational — Nifty derivatives migrated to NSE International Exchange in GIFT City, India
  • 2025 (November): Launched institutional-grade Bitcoin and Ethereum perpetual futures tied to iEdge-CoinDesk Cryptocurrency Indices
  • 2025: Record annual derivatives volume of 329 million contracts; record FX futures volumes; record OTC FX ADV of USD 143 billion; record MSCI Singapore OI
  • 2026 (April, planned): Launch of Asia Pacific Government Bond Futures covering India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, and the Philippines
  • 2027 (planned): New securities trading engine “Iris-ST” expected to go live in second half of 2027

[Source: SGX website, “About SGX”; Wikipedia, “Singapore Exchange”; SGX press releases 2022–2026; SGX FY2025 Annual Report; PR Newswire, “SGX Derivatives breaks new ground with institutional-grade crypto perpetual futures”, November 2025; Markets Media, “SGX Derivatives to Launch Asia Pacific Government Bond Futures”, March 2026]

2.5 Market Maker / Liquidity Provider Programs

SGX operates designated market maker programs across its derivatives markets:

Program Structure: - Designated Market Makers (DMMs): Obligated to provide continuous two-sided quotes for assigned products; must meet quoting obligations in terms of spread width, minimum quantity, and time in market - SGX-DT may reduce or waive fees, levies, and charges for qualifying DMMs as incentive - Performance measured against KPIs typically tied to volume, spread maintenance, and market presence

Key Features: - Fee discounts and rebates for qualifying market makers - Volume-based incentive tiers — higher volumes unlock greater incentives - Programs cover equity index derivatives, FX futures, commodity derivatives, and options - Market maker participation was critical during market stress events (e.g., Covid-19 pandemic), supporting liquidity in key contracts

Coverage: - Equity index futures and options (FTSE China A50, Nikkei 225, MSCI suite) - FX futures (USD/CNH, INR/USD) - Iron ore and commodity derivatives - Options markets across asset classes

[Source: SGX Rulebook, “Chapter 5 — Designated Market-Maker”; SGX Rulebook, “6.6 Obligations of a Designated Market-Maker”; The Edge Singapore, “Where’s the liquidity?”]

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Auction Mechanisms

Opening Auction (Pre-Open / Non-Cancel): - Pre-Open phase allows order entry, amendment, and cancellation - In the final 2 minutes before session open, only new order placements are accepted (no modifications or cancellations) - At session open, accumulated orders are matched at a single equilibrium price computed by SGX’s matching algorithm - The algorithm maximizes matched volume at the equilibrium price

Closing / Settlement: - Daily settlement prices are determined based on trading activity during defined settlement windows - For equity index contracts, final settlement price is typically based on the official closing price of the underlying index on the last trading day

3.2 Continuous Trading

  • Primary mechanism: continuous electronic matching on the SGX Titan DT platform
  • Orders matched in real-time during T and T+1 sessions
  • Supports outright orders and spread strategies (calendar spreads, inter-commodity spreads)
  • T+1 sessions provide extended hours with continuous matching but typically have thinner order books

3.3 Price Discovery

  • Extended trading hours (up to ~22 hours/day for key products) provide continuous price discovery spanning Asian, European, and U.S. market hours
  • SGX’s position as the primary offshore listing venue for major Asian indices (Nikkei 225, FTSE China A50) makes it a key price discovery venue outside home market hours
  • Nikkei 225 futures on SGX often lead price discovery during non-Japan trading hours
  • FTSE China A50 futures serve as the primary price discovery mechanism for China A-share exposure when mainland markets are closed

3.4 Order Priority Rules / Matching Algorithms

All orders on SGX Derivatives are matched using Price-Time Priority (FIFO) as the default algorithm:

Algorithm Description Application
Price-Time Priority (FIFO) Strict price priority, then time priority at each price level; earliest order at best price is filled first Default for most futures products
Price Point Maker (PPM) Alternative algorithm giving priority to resting passive orders Selected products [unverified]
Pro-Rata Allocation proportional to order size at each price level Selected options markets [unverified]
Market Maker (MM) Market maker receives priority allocation Applicable where DMM program is in effect

Key Rules: - Price priority is absolute — better-priced orders always fill first - Within the same price, time priority determines allocation (FIFO) - During opening/closing routines, orders are matched at a single equilibrium price - Self-Trade Prevention (STP) functionality available to prevent matching of orders from the same firm

[Source: SGX Rulebook, “Practice Note 8.2.1 — Application of Market Phases and Algorithm”; SGX Rulebook, “Regulatory Notice 8.2.1 — Trading Hours, Market Phases, Application of Market Phases and Principles and Rules for Trade Matching”; Trading Technologies, “SGX Overview”]

4. Order Types

4.1 Standard Order Types

Order Type Description
Limit Buy/sell at a specified price or better; unfilled quantity rests on the book
Market Executes immediately at the best available price; any unfilled portion is cancelled or rests depending on configuration
Market to Limit Executes at the best available price; unfilled quantity converts to a limit order at the last execution price

4.2 Advanced Order Types

Order Type Description
Stop Limit Triggered when the stop price is reached; once triggered, becomes a limit order at the specified limit price
Stop Market Triggered when the stop price is reached; once triggered, becomes a market order
Stop Market to Limit Triggered when the stop price is reached; once triggered, becomes a market-to-limit order
Spread Orders Calendar spreads and inter-commodity spread strategies
Mass Quote Allows market makers to submit multiple two-sided quotes in a single message via OUCH protocol

4.3 Time-in-Force (TIF) Conditions

TIF Description
Day Valid for the current trading session only; cancelled at session end
Good Till Cancel (GTC) Remains active until filled or explicitly cancelled
Good Till Date (GTD) Remains active until a specified date
Fill and Kill (FAK) Executes immediately for any available quantity; unfilled portion is cancelled
Fill or Kill (FOK) Must be filled entirely and immediately, or the entire order is cancelled

4.4 Order Modification & Cancellation

  • Orders can be modified (price, quantity) or cancelled at any time during trading sessions via the OUCH protocol
  • Replace Order: Modifies an existing order (price/quantity change) while maintaining or updating time priority
  • Cancel Order: Cancels a specific order by order token
  • Cancel by Order ID: Alternative cancellation method using the exchange-assigned order ID
  • Self-Trade Prevention (STP): Enhanced in 2024/2025 to allow configurable prevention of self-matching within the same firm

[Source: Trading Technologies, “SGX Supported Order Types”; OnixS, “SGX Titan OUCH Trading Handler — EnterOrder”; SGX Titan OUCH Protocol Specifications; Trading Technologies, “SGX Self-Trade Prevention Enhancement”]

5. Trading System Technology — SGX Titan

5.1 System Overview

Item Details
Platform Name SGX Titan DT (Derivatives Trading) / SGX Titan DC (Derivatives Clearing)
Technology Vendor Nasdaq (Genium INET version 4)
First Announced December 2014
Go-Live 2016–2017 (phased migration from legacy platform)
Architecture High-performance, low-latency matching engine with product-level partitioning
Primary Data Center Co-location facility in Singapore
Order Entry Protocol OUCH (Nasdaq native binary protocol for asynchronous order entry)
Market Data Protocol ITCH (Nasdaq native binary multicast protocol for market data)
Recovery Protocol GLIMPSE (order book snapshot recovery for ITCH)
Average Latency Sub-90 microseconds (benchmark average); sub-200 microseconds round-trip in production
Throughput >1 million messages per second

5.2 Architecture

  • Matching Engine: Product-partitioned matching engines running on dedicated hardware optimized for deterministic, low-latency processing
  • Order Entry Gateway: OUCH protocol servers providing direct, asynchronous order entry with minimal overhead
  • Market Data Dissemination: ITCH multicast feed providing real-time, full-depth order book data to all connected participants
  • Clearing Engine (Titan DC): Integrated clearing platform processing trades in near-real-time for risk management and settlement
  • Deterministic Processing: Designed for consistent, predictable latency with minimal jitter

5.3 Latency

  • Benchmark Latency: Sub-90 microseconds average (gate-to-gate) — among the fastest derivatives trading platforms globally at time of launch
  • Production Round-Trip: Sub-200 microseconds for co-located participants
  • Order Routing Latency: Approximately 100–150 microseconds via OUCH protocol
  • Compared to Predecessor: Significant improvement over previous SGX derivatives platform; Nasdaq marketing described Titan as “the world’s fastest trading platform” at announcement

5.4 Platform History & Evolution

Year Milestone
1984 SIMEX trading floor opens — open outcry for financial futures
1999 SGX formed; electronic trading introduced alongside open outcry
2003 Nasdaq technology partnership begins for SGX trading systems
2014 (Dec) SGX announces next-generation Titan platform using Nasdaq Genium INET v4
2015 Development and testing phase; originally targeted for late 2015 go-live

2027 (planned) New securities trading engine “Iris-ST” planned; derivatives platform evolution TBD

5.5 Technology Stack

Component Technology
Order Entry OUCH — Nasdaq native binary protocol for low-latency, asynchronous order submission; supports Enter Order, Replace Order, Cancel Order, Cancel by Order ID messages
Market Data ITCH — Nasdaq native binary multicast protocol; provides full order-by-order depth-of-book data; supports incremental updates and order book reconstruction
Order Book Recovery GLIMPSE — snapshot recovery protocol for ITCH data; allows participants to rebuild full order book state after connection loss
ITCH Rewinder Replay of historical ITCH messages for recovery of lost packets
FIX Gateway FIX protocol access available for participants preferring standard FIX connectivity [unverified]
Clearing Integration Titan DC provides integrated clearing with real-time position and risk management

5.6 Disaster Recovery & Business Continuity

  • Primary Data Center: Co-location facility in Singapore
  • DR Site: Geographically separate backup site in Singapore [unverified]
  • Recovery Model: Real-time data replication between primary and backup environments
  • Operational Support: SGX operates follow-the-sun support model given extended trading hours (up to ~22 hours/day)
  • System Resilience: Nasdaq Genium INET platform designed with built-in redundancy and failover capabilities
  • Regulatory Requirement: MAS requires all approved exchange operators to maintain robust BCP and DR capabilities under its Technology Risk Management Guidelines

[Source: Nasdaq Inc., “NASDAQ OMX to Deliver World’s Fastest Trading Platform to Singapore Exchange”, December 2014; OnixS, “SGX Titan DT ITCH and OUCH API SDKs”; WatersTechnology, “SGX Plans Titan for Derivatives”; SGX Titan ITCH & GLIMPSE Protocol Specifications, Release 1.4; Markets Media, “Object Trading Conforms to Upgraded SGX Titan Platform”]

6. Market Data

6.1 Market Data Platform — ITCH

Feature Details
Protocol ITCH (Nasdaq native binary protocol)
Encoding Fixed-length binary messages for ultra-low-latency processing
Transport UDP multicast (primary dissemination)
Recovery GLIMPSE protocol for order book snapshots; ITCH Rewinder for packet replay
Depth Full order-by-order depth (Market by Order)

6.2 Feed Types / Data Levels

Feed Type Description Transport
ITCH Real-Time Full depth, order-by-order market data including every order addition, modification, execution, and cancellation UDP multicast
GLIMPSE Point-in-time snapshot of the full order book; used for recovery and initialization TCP (request/response)
ITCH Rewinder Replay of historical ITCH messages from a specified sequence number; used for gap recovery TCP
End-of-Day Files Settlement prices, volumes, open interest published daily File-based download

6.3 Data Distribution

  • Direct connectivity: Co-located participants receive ITCH multicast data within the SGX data center at the lowest possible latency
  • Managed Network: Participants not co-located can access via SGX-approved network service providers or managed network services
  • Redistributors: Major data vendors (Bloomberg, Refinitiv, ICE Data Services, etc.) redistribute SGX market data under license
  • SGX Data Services: SGX provides historical data, analytics, and index data through its data products division
  • Delayed Data: Available through various financial portals and SGX’s own website

6.4 Market Data Licensing

  • SGX charges separate fees for real-time market data, delayed data redistribution, and historical data
  • Professional and non-professional fee tiers apply
  • Specific fee schedules available from SGX upon request or through the SGX website

[Source: SGX Titan ITCH & GLIMPSE Protocol Specifications, Release 1.4; OnixS, “SGX Titan DT ITCH and OUCH API SDKs”; SGX website, “Data & Connectivity”]

7. Connectivity & Access

7.1 Membership / Access Types

Membership Type Description Key Requirements
Derivatives Trading Member Direct electronic access to SGX-DT for trading derivatives MAS Capital Markets Services (CMS) license; meet SGX fit and proper criteria; minimum financial requirements
Derivatives Clearing Member — General Can clear own trades and trades of third parties (non-clearing members and customers) Higher capital requirements; participation in SGX-DC clearing fund; MAS approval
Derivatives Clearing Member — Bank Bank-specific clearing membership; can clear own and third-party trades; access to Mutual Offset Systems Must be a licensed bank; additional financial requirements
Non-Clearing Member Trades through a General Clearing Member or Bank Clearing Member Must establish clearing arrangement with a clearing member

Membership Statistics: - SGX derivatives clearing is supported by major global banks and brokers - Eight prominent clearing members supported the November 2025 Bitcoin/Ethereum perpetual futures launch

[Source: SGX website, “Memberships”; SGX Rulebook, “SGX-DC Clearing Rules”; SGX RegCo, “Memberships”]

7.2 Connection Methods

Connection Type Description
Co-Location Direct physical proximity to SGX Titan matching engines within the SGX data center; lowest latency option
Managed Network Service Connectivity via SGX-approved network service providers
Offshore Hubs Access points in key financial centers (London, Chicago, Tokyo) for international participants [unverified]
Approved Network Service Providers Third-party network providers certified by SGX for derivatives market access

Note: SGX derivatives connectivity is effectively co-location only for participants requiring the lowest latency. All market access routes ultimately connect to the Titan DT platform within the SGX data center.

7.3 Co-Location Services

  • Location: SGX data center in Singapore
  • Facility: Purpose-built co-location environment with proximity to Titan DT matching engines
  • Benefits: Unparalleled speed and efficiency for direct market access; minimized network latency
  • Cabinet Options: Standard rack space with power and cooling; specific configurations available on request
  • Cross-Connects: Direct fiber connections within the facility to SGX infrastructure

7.4 API Protocols

API / Protocol Purpose Direction
Titan OUCH Order entry, modification, cancellation, execution reports Inbound (client to exchange) and outbound (exchange to client)
Titan ITCH Real-time market data dissemination Outbound (exchange to client) — UDP multicast
GLIMPSE Order book snapshot recovery Request/response — TCP
FIX Alternative order entry for participants not using native OUCH [unverified] Bidirectional — TCP

7.5 Certification (Conformance Testing)

  • Mandatory: All client systems connecting via Titan OUCH and ITCH must complete SGX conformance testing before production access
  • Test Scope: Covers OUCH order entry (enter, replace, cancel, cancel by order ID), ITCH market data processing, and GLIMPSE recovery
  • Environment: SGX provides a dedicated conformance test environment
  • Recertification: Required for significant system changes or protocol version upgrades
  • ISV Support: Multiple independent software vendors (OnixS, Trading Technologies, Object Trading, etc.) offer pre-certified SDKs and trading handlers for SGX Titan

[Source: SGX website, “Market Access”; SGX website, “Co-Location”; SGX Titan ITCH and OUCH Conformance Test Scope, Version 1.0; OnixS, “SGX Titan DT ITCH and OUCH API SDKs”]

8. Risk Controls & Circuit Breakers

8.1 Circuit Breaker Mechanism

SGX’s derivatives circuit breakers operate as dynamic price bands:

  • Price Band Thresholds: Upper and lower thresholds set as a prescribed percentage from a reference price
  • Trigger: When an incoming order seeks to match against a resting order at a price outside the threshold, a Cooling-Off Period is activated and the incoming order is rejected
  • Cooling-Off Period: A brief pause (typically 5 minutes) allowing the market to analyze conditions before resuming; designed to moderate the pace of large price movements without halting price discovery entirely
  • Reference Price Calculation:
    • At the start of each Trading Phase: derived from the prior session’s settlement or last traded price
    • After the first 5 minutes: rolling reference price based on the last traded price as of 5 minutes prior to each potential trade
    • If no trade in the preceding 5 minutes, the reference price at the start of the Trading Phase applies

Purpose: The circuit breaker guards against disorderly situations from rapid, unchecked market movements. It is not intended to halt price movement but rather to allow market participants and regulators a pause to assess conditions. It also helps avert contagion risk to other markets.

D3. Margin Framework

Methodology: SPAN (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk)

SGX-DC uses SPAN as its primary margining system — a risk-based, portfolio approach to computing margin requirements.

Key Features:

Feature Description
Model SPAN (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk), originally developed by CME Group
Approach Evaluates the worst possible portfolio loss over a specified time horizon (typically 1 day for futures, 1–2 days for options) under multiple market scenarios
Risk Scenarios 16 standard scenarios combining price moves and volatility shifts
Price Scan Range Set based on a confidence level (typically 99% or higher), calibrated using historical price data with volatility scaling
Volatility Scan Range Scenarios include shifts in implied volatility
Portfolio Offsets Cross-margining credits for correlated positions within the same combined commodity group

SGX-DC’s Enhanced SPAN Parameters

SGX-DC has enhanced the standard SPAN methodology with specific modifications:

  1. Volatility Scaling: SGX replaced the legacy standard deviation approach (which gave equal weight to all price observations during the prior year) with a volatility-scaling methodology. Margins are now more responsive to the prevailing volatility regime — increasing during high-volatility periods and decreasing during calm periods.

  2. Anti-Procyclicality Floor: A floor ensures that margins cannot dip below those that would be calculated using a 10-year lookback period, even during extended benign/low-volatility periods. This prevents the buildup of under-margined risk during calm markets.

  3. Stress Testing: SGX-DC regularly performs stress tests beyond SPAN scenarios to ensure clearing fund adequacy under extreme but plausible market conditions.

Margin Types

Margin Type Description
Initial Margin (Performance Bond) Required deposit to open a position; covers the estimated maximum one-day loss under SPAN scenarios
Maintenance Margin Minimum margin level that must be maintained; typically set at a lower level than initial margin
Variation Margin Daily mark-to-market settlement; calculated at the end of each trading day and settled in cash through Settlement Banks
Intraday Margin Additional margin calls during the trading day if positions incur significant unrealized losses or volatility spikes
Spread Margin Reduced margin for spread positions (calendar, inter-commodity) that benefit from offsetting risk

Margin Call Process

  1. End-of-Day: Settlement prices determined; variation margin and any margin shortfalls calculated
  2. Margin Call Issued: Clearing members notified of margin obligations via SGX-DC systems
  3. Settlement: Cash transfers through approved Settlement Banks
  4. Intraday Cycles: Additional intraday margin calls may be issued (and must be met promptly) to cover shortfalls arising from market movements during the trading session
  5. Accepted Collateral: Cash (multi-currency), government securities, bank guarantees, and other approved collateral forms

Industry Context

While the broader derivatives industry is migrating from SPAN to VaR-based margin models (e.g., CME SPAN 2, Eurex PRISMA), SGX-DC continues to use its enhanced SPAN framework. The anti-procyclicality measures and volatility scaling represent meaningful improvements over basic SPAN, providing margin stability while maintaining risk responsiveness.

D4. Position Limits & Large Position Reporting

Position Limits

SGX imposes position limits on derivatives contracts to manage systemic risk and prevent excessive concentration:

  • Position limits are defined per contract in the SGX Rulebook (Section 18.4.2) and vary by product
  • Members must have automated processes in place to monitor at the firm level if they are at risk of breaching prudential limits on exposures to a single customer and a single contract
  • Position limits apply to speculative positions — positions held for trading/investment purposes without an underlying commercial hedging need

Hedge Exemptions

  • Market participants with bona fide hedging needs may apply for exemptions from speculative position limits
  • Applications for exemption must be submitted to SGX-DT, demonstrating the hedging rationale and underlying commercial exposure
  • Exempted positions must be monitored and reported on an ongoing basis

Large Position Reporting

  • Clearing members are required to report large positions to SGX-DC when positions exceed specified thresholds
  • Large position reports enable SGX to monitor concentration risk and potential market manipulation
  • Thresholds are set on a product-by-product basis

Accountability Levels

SGX may set accountability levels (as distinct from hard position limits) for certain contracts, requiring participants exceeding these levels to provide additional information about their positions and trading intentions upon request.

[Source: SGX Rulebook, “18.4.2 Position Limits”; SGX Rulebook, “Practice Note 7.22 — Margins of Third Parties”; SGX website, “Risk Management”]

D5. Block Trading / Negotiated Large Trades (NLT) / EFP / EFS

Negotiated Large Trades (NLT)

SGX’s equivalent of block trades is the Negotiated Large Trade (NLT) facility:

Key Rules: - NLTs are privately negotiated transactions executed off the central order book at a price agreed between two parties - Each NLT must meet the minimum volume threshold specified per contract in Appendix A of Regulatory Notice 4.1.11 - Representative minimum thresholds: - Nikkei 225 Options: 25 lots - Other products: varies by contract (specific thresholds published in the Regulatory Notice) - A multi-leg NLT within the same underlying is deemed compliant if at least one leg meets the minimum volume threshold - Members shall not combine individual customers’ orders solely to meet the minimum volume threshold - However, individual orders that each independently exceed the threshold may be combined to match a larger NLT counter bid/offer

Reporting and Registration: - NLTs must be reported to SGX within the prescribed timeframe - Both the seller and buyer must have satisfied the credit and position thresholds prescribed by their respective clearing members - NLTs are registered with SGX-DC for clearing upon successful validation

Exchange for Physical (EFP)

  • EFP transactions allow the exchange of a derivatives position for a corresponding physical or cash market position
  • Must meet procedures prescribed by SGX
  • The physical leg must be a bona fide transaction in the underlying or related commodity/instrument
  • Clearing member credit and position thresholds must be satisfied

Exchange for Swap (EFS)

  • EFS transactions allow the exchange of a listed derivatives position for an equivalent OTC swap position
  • Subject to similar rules as EFP transactions regarding procedures, credit thresholds, and reporting

Registration Requirements

For NLT, EFP, and EFS transactions involving contracts listed on a “Relevant Market” (i.e., contracts that are also listed on another exchange), Rule 7.04A requires compliance with additional registration procedures to ensure cross-market integrity.

D6. Delivery & Settlement Process

Daily Settlement (Mark-to-Market)

  1. Daily Settlement Prices: SGX-DC determines daily settlement prices for all listed derivatives contracts at the end of each trading day
  2. Variation Margin: The difference between the current day’s settlement price and the prior day’s settlement price (or the trade price for new positions) is settled in cash
  3. Settlement Banks: Cash transfers are effected through approved Settlement Banks; clearing members must maintain accounts with approved banks
  4. Intraday Settlement: Intraday margin calls and mark-to-market may be triggered during volatile market conditions

Final Settlement — Cash-Settled Contracts

The majority of SGX derivatives are cash-settled:

Product Final Settlement Price Methodology
FTSE China A50 Futures Based on the official closing price of the FTSE China A50 Index on the last trading day
Nikkei 225 Futures Based on the Special Quotation (SQ) of the Nikkei 225 Index
Iron Ore Futures Arithmetic average of TSI Iron Ore CFR China 62% Fe Fines assessments during the contract month
FX Futures (USD/CNH) Based on the USD/CNH(HK) fixing published by TMA
FX Futures (INR/USD) Based on the RBI Reference Rate
MSCI Index Futures Based on the official closing price of the respective MSCI Index

Final Settlement — Physically Delivered Contracts

A small number of SGX commodity contracts (e.g., rubber futures — TSR20, RSS3) settle via physical delivery:

  • Delivery obligations fall to the trading counterparties, not to SGX-DC
  • SGX-DC acts as the central counterparty for financial settlement but has no liability for the actual delivery process
  • Delivery locations, quality specifications, and procedures are defined in each contract’s specifications
  • Clearing members must ensure their customers are prepared to make or take delivery if holding positions into the delivery period

Settlement Currency

  • Most SGX derivatives contracts are denominated and settled in USD, making them accessible to international participants without currency conversion
  • Selected contracts settle in JPY (Nikkei 225 JPY), SGD (MSCI Singapore), HKD (Hang Seng products), or AUD (S&P/ASX 200)
