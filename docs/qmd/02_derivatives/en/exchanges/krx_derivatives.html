1. Overview & Profile

Item Details
Official Name Korea Exchange (한국거래소)
Abbreviation KRX
Website https://www.krx.co.kr (domestic), https://global.krx.co.kr (English)
Year Established 2005 (KRX formed by merger of KSE, KOSDAQ, KOFEX); predecessor Korea Futures Exchange (KOFEX) established 1999
Country / City South Korea / Busan (headquarters), Seoul (trading floor)
Ownership Structure Public institution under Financial Services Commission (FSC); not publicly traded
Regulatory Authority Financial Services Commission (FSC, 금융위원회) / Financial Supervisory Service (FSS, 금융감독원)
Annual Volume (2024) ~1.5 billion contracts (~2 billion in 2023, ranked 12th globally per FIA)
Historical Peak 3.93 billion contracts in 2011 — world’s largest options exchange by volume
Clearing House KRX operates as integrated exchange-CCP (vertically integrated); KRX Clearing is the central counterparty for all exchange-traded derivatives

Key Historical Milestones

  • 1996: KOSPI 200 futures launched (May 3) — South Korea’s first exchange-traded derivatives product
  • 1997: KOSPI 200 options launched (July 7) — would become the world’s most traded single contract by volume
  • 1999: Korea Futures Exchange (KOFEX) established as a separate entity in Busan
  • 2001: USD futures launched; equity options launched
  • 2002: CD interest rate futures, KTB 3-year futures launched
  • 2004: KTB 10-year futures launched
  • 2005: KRX formed by merging Korea Stock Exchange (KSE), KOSDAQ Committee, and Korea Futures Exchange (KOFEX) (January 27)
  • 2008: Lean Hog futures, Gold futures launched
  • 2009: KRX ranked world’s busiest exchange (3.1 billion contracts); sector index futures/options launched
  • 2011: Peak volume of 3.93 billion contracts; KOSPI 200 options alone accounted for ~3.67 billion contracts
  • 2012: KOSPI 200 options multiplier quintupled from KRW 100,000 to KRW 500,000 (June); annual volume dropped 53.3% as a result
  • 2014: EXTURE+ trading system launched (replacing the original EXTURE system)
  • 2015: Mini KOSPI 200 futures/options launched; V-KOSPI 200 futures launched
  • 2017: Weekly KOSPI 200 options launched
  • 2019: KOSDAQ 150 futures/options launched
  • 2022: Sector index options expanded
  • 2025: Independent night session launched (June 9), replacing the Eurex-linked after-hours session; KRX technology exported to Thai Stock Exchange (contract signed April 2025)
  • 2026: KOSPI 200 options strike price expansion (48 additional strikes from April 27); new stock futures/options listings; TIGER Semiconductor ETF Futures launched

2.5 Market Maker / Liquidity Provider Programs

KRX maintains designated market maker (유동성공급자) programs across derivatives products:

Program Structure: - Market makers are appointed by KRX for specific products and are required to provide continuous two-sided quotes - Market makers receive trading fee rebates and volume-based incentives - Subject to periodic performance evaluation by KRX; underperforming market makers may lose designation

Active Programs: - KOSPI 200 options (including weekly and mini) - Sector index options - USD options - Single stock options and futures - Mini KOSPI 200 products - KOSDAQ 150 options

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Auction Types

Opening Single-Price Auction (개시단일가): - First portion of regular and night sessions - Accumulated orders are matched at a single price that maximizes matched volume - Random end period applied to prevent manipulation

Closing Single-Price Auction (종가단일가): - Last 10 minutes of regular and night sessions (15:35–15:45 KST for regular session) - Settlement price determined through single-price auction algorithm - Random end period applied

Intraday Volatility Interruption: - Triggers a temporary single-price auction when dynamic or static VI thresholds are breached - 2-minute cooling-off period followed by single-price auction

3.2 Continuous Trading

  • Price-Time Priority (가격우선·시간우선): Standard FIFO matching — best-priced orders fill first; among orders at the same price, earliest order fills first
  • Order-driven market: No specialist or dealer system; all liquidity comes from public and market maker orders
  • Pure electronic matching via EXTURE+ platform

3.3 Price Discovery

  • Opening price: Determined by single-price auction algorithm at session open
  • Settlement price: Last traded price during the settlement window (or VWAP method for final settlement in some products)
  • Final settlement price for KOSPI 200: Weighted average of component stock opening prices during the last 30 minutes of trading on the last trading day, calculated to prevent manipulation
  • Implied pricing: Not supported — KRX uses direct order book matching only (unlike CME Globex’s implied spread functionality)

[Source: KRX, “파생상품시장 업무규정_전문_20250609”]

8. Risk Controls & Circuit Breakers

From 업무규정 and 시행세칙:

8.1 Static Price Limits (Daily)

Product Price Limit
KOSPI 200 Futures +/-10% of previous settlement price
Stock Futures +/-30% (aligned with underlying stock daily price limits)
KTB Futures +/-1.5% (3Y), +/-2.1% (10Y) [unverified — based on regulatory tables]
USD Futures Based on specified percentage of previous settlement

8.2 Dynamic VI (Volatility Interruption, 동적VI)

  • Triggered when execution price deviates +/-3% or more from the reference price (for KOSPI 200 futures)
  • Results in a 2-minute cooling-off period followed by a single-price auction
  • Applies to both futures and options contracts
  • Reference price updated after each VI event

8.3 Static VI (정적VI)

  • Triggered when price deviates beyond the previous day’s settlement price by a specified percentage
  • Results in a trading halt followed by a single-price auction
  • Thresholds vary by product

8.4 Market-wide Circuit Breaker (Sidecar)

  • KOSPI 200 Futures Sidecar: When lead-month futures price reaches +/-5% of previous settlement AND remains for 1 minute with theoretical price variance +/-3% or more
  • Results in a 5-minute halt of ALL derivatives contracts
  • Three levels: 5% -> 10% -> 20%
  • Each level can only be triggered once per day

8.5 Pre-trade Risk Controls

  • Maximum order size limits per member
  • Intraday margin monitoring with real-time position tracking
  • Real-time position limit checks
  • Kill switch capabilities for member firms
  • Automated risk parameter checks before order acceptance

[Source: KRX, “파생상품시장 업무규정_전문_20250609”; KRX, “파생상품시장 업무규정 시행세칙_전문_20251027”]

9. Regulatory Framework

Item Details
Supervising Authority Financial Services Commission (FSC, 금융위원회) — policy-making and rulemaking
Examination / Enforcement Financial Supervisory Service (FSS, 금융감독원) — examination and enforcement
Systemic Oversight Bank of Korea (BOK, 한국은행) — systemic risk and payment system oversight
Key Laws Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act (FSCMA, 자본시장법); Derivatives Market Business Regulations (파생상품시장 업무규정)
Market Surveillance KRX Market Surveillance Division conducts real-time monitoring of trading activity, including detection of spoofing, layering, and market manipulation
Position Reporting Large position reporting required above thresholds defined in 업무규정
Cross-border Framework Qualified Foreign Investor (QFI) framework for foreign participants; foreign investors must register with FSS
OTC Clearing Mandate KRW IRS (Interest Rate Swaps) and KRW CRS (Cross Currency Swaps) clearing mandated through KRX CCP
Self-Regulation KRX operates as a Self-Regulatory Organization (SRO) under FSC supervision

[Source: KRX Global Website; Financial Services Commission (FSC)]

10. Fees & Revenue Model

Item Details
Trading Fee Range Approximately 0.0018% to 0.04995% of notional value (varies by product)
Options Fee Approximately 0.09% to 1.5% of premium
Example (Semiconductor Index) Trading fee 0.0005947% + clearing fee 0.0001161% = total 0.0007108%
Market Maker Rebates Fee rebates available for designated market makers
Fee Restructuring (Dec 2025) First fee restructuring in 20 years — maker-taker model introduced for equities; derivatives fees also adjusted
Competition KRX faces competition from Nextrade (NXT) alternative exchange (launched March 2025 for equities)

Revenue Model: - KRX generates revenue from trading fees, clearing fees, market data fees, listing fees, and technology services - As a public institution (not publicly traded), KRX reinvests surplus into market infrastructure and development - Technology export (e.g., EXTURE+ to Thai Stock Exchange) is an emerging revenue stream

[Source: KRX Global Website; KRX fee schedules]
