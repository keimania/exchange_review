1. Overview & Profile

Item Details
Official Name Shanghai Futures Exchange (上海期货交易所)
Abbreviation SHFE
Website https://www.shfe.com.cn/eng/

Ownership Structure Non-profit, self-regulatory organization
Parent Company / Group Independent; operates under supervision of the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC)
Subsidiary Shanghai International Energy Exchange (INE, established 2013) — handles internationalized products
Regulatory Authority China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC)
Global Ranking 10th largest derivatives exchange globally (FIA 2023 ranking)
Listed Instruments 24+ futures contracts (SHFE + INE combined), plus options on multiple products
Key Products Copper, Aluminum, Zinc, Gold, Silver, Nickel, Natural Rubber, Steel Rebar, Crude Oil (INE), Fuel Oil

Key Historical Milestones

  • 1992 (May 28): Shanghai Metal Exchange (SHME) established — one of the predecessor exchanges
  • 1993: Shanghai Cereals and Oil Exchange and Shanghai Commodity Exchange also operating
  • 1999 (December): Shanghai Futures Exchange formed from the merger of Shanghai Metal Exchange, Shanghai Cereals and Oil Exchange, and Shanghai Commodity Exchange — part of China’s nationwide restructuring of futures markets
  • 2004: Fuel oil futures listed
  • 2007: Zinc futures listed
  • 2008: Gold futures listed (January 9) — first gold futures in mainland China
  • 2012: Silver futures listed
  • 2013: Shanghai International Energy Exchange (INE) established as a subsidiary for internationalized products
  • 2014: Hot-rolled coil futures listed; nickel and tin futures listed
  • 2018 (March 26): INE crude oil futures (SC) launched — first commodity futures in mainland China open to overseas investors, priced in RMB
  • 2018: Copper options listed (September 21) — SHFE’s first options product
  • 2019: Natural rubber options, stainless steel futures (September 25), TSR 20 rubber futures (INE) listed
  • 2020: Low sulfur fuel oil futures listed on INE; INE bonded copper futures launched
  • 2022: Cast aluminum alloy futures listed; alumina (aluminum oxide) futures listed
  • 2024: SHFE wins three international awards at FOW Trading Amsterdam; containerized freight index futures (SCFIS) launched on INE
  • 2025: Offset paper futures listed [unverified listing date]

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Order Matching

  • Matching Method: Computerized centralized matching using price-time priority
  • Priority Rules: Price priority first (higher bids and lower offers matched first), then time priority (earlier orders at the same price have priority)
  • Matching Engine: Orders enter the mainframe for centralized matching; concluded deals feedback delivered to trading terminals

3.2 Auction Mechanisms

  • Call Auction (Opening): Used at the start of each session to determine the opening price. Orders accumulated during the pre-opening period are matched at a single price that maximizes volume.
  • Continuous Auction: After the opening, incoming orders are continuously matched against resting orders on the book.

3.3 Order Types

Order Type Description
Limit Order Order to buy or sell at a specified price or better
Market Order Order executed at the best available price; unfilled portion cancelled

3.4 Settlement Price

  • The daily settlement price is typically the volume-weighted average price of all trades during the trading day (or a specified calculation window)
  • Used for daily mark-to-market margin calculations

3.5 Spread Trading

  • Calendar spread (inter-month spread) trading is supported for selected products
  • Cross-commodity spreads may be available for related products [unverified]

5. Trading System / Technology

5.1 CTP (Comprehensive Transaction Platform)

SHFE operates the CTP (Comprehensive Transaction Platform) as its core trading system, which is widely used across China’s futures industry.

Item Details
System Name CTP (Comprehensive Transaction Platform)

Access Methods CTP API (primary), Hang Seng, POBO (Bloomberg), Multicharts terminals

5.2 System Capabilities

  • Order Processing: Centralized matching with price-time priority for all listed futures and options
  • Risk Management: Real-time position monitoring, margin calculation, and automated forced liquidation
  • Market Data Distribution: Real-time price and depth-of-market data distributed to members and information vendors
  • Delivery System: Electronic warehouse receipt management and physical delivery processing

5.3 Infrastructure

  • Primary data center located in the VIP enclosure of the Zhangjiang Data Center, Shanghai
  • Co-location / hosting services available at the exchange’s data center for low-latency trading access
  • Rapid trading platforms deployed in the hosting room for high-frequency trading participants
  • Standardized open interface access for individual investors, institutional investors, and professional HFT participants

5.4 Disaster Recovery

  • SHFE maintains business continuity plans with backup systems
  • Specific DR site location and RTO/RPO details are not publicly disclosed [unverified]

8. Risk Controls & Circuit Breakers

8.1 Daily Price Limits

SHFE applies daily price limits to all futures contracts, calculated as a percentage of the previous trading day’s settlement price. Limits are adjusted based on market conditions and during holiday periods.

Standard Price Limits (Normal Conditions):

Copper (CU) ±3% (base) Raised to ±7-9% during volatile periods (e.g., 9% in January 2026)
Aluminum (AL) ±3% (base) Raised to ±9% during volatile periods
Zinc (ZN) ±4% Adjusted during holidays
Lead (PB) ±4% Adjusted during holidays
Nickel (NI) ±5% Higher base limit due to volatility
Tin (SN) ±4% Adjusted during holidays
Gold (AU) ±3% (base) Raised during volatile periods
Silver (AG) ±3% (base) Raised during volatile periods
Natural Rubber (RU) ±3% Standard
Fuel Oil (FU) ±5% Standard
Steel Rebar (RB) ±4% Standard
Stainless Steel (SS) ±5% (base) Raised to ±8% during holidays
INE Crude Oil (SC) ±4% (base) Adjusted based on international crude price volatility

Expansion Mechanism: - If a contract settles at its price limit for consecutive trading days, SHFE may expand the price limit range and increase margin requirements - SHFE can also implement ad hoc adjustments before holidays or during extreme market conditions - In January 2026, SHFE significantly tightened rules amid precious metals surge — expanded daily price limits for gold, silver, copper, and aluminum while raising margin requirements

8.2 Trading Margin Requirements

SHFE uses a fixed-percentage margin system. Margins are calculated as a percentage of contract value.

Standard Minimum Trading Margins:

Product Minimum Margin Rate Notes
Copper (CU) 5% (base) Raised to 8-11% during volatile periods; 11% for speculative positions in Jan 2026
Aluminum (AL) 5% (base) Adjusted during volatile periods
Zinc (ZN) 5% (base) Standard
Lead (PB) 5% (base) Standard
Nickel (NI) 8% (base) Higher due to volatility
Tin (SN) 8% (base) Higher due to volatility

Margin Adjustment Rules: - Margins increase as a contract approaches its delivery month (stepped increases) - Margins increase when open interest exceeds specified thresholds - SHFE raises margins before long holidays (Spring Festival, National Day, Labor Day) - The exchange can raise margins at any time based on market conditions

8.3 Position Limits

  • SHFE imposes speculative position limits specifying the maximum long or short position each member or client may hold
  • Position limits differ by product and are expressed as maximum lots
  • Daily opening position limits apply to certain products (e.g., silver and tin have reduced daily opening position limits)
  • Hedging positions can be approved for higher limits upon application
  • Large trader reporting requirements apply when positions exceed specified thresholds

8.4 Risk Management Framework

SHFE applies a comprehensive risk management regime including:

Measure Description
Margin Requirement Percentage of contract value deposited as collateral
Price Limit Maximum daily price fluctuation range
Speculative Position Limit Maximum position size for non-hedging accounts
Large Trader Reporting Mandatory reporting when positions exceed thresholds
Forced Position Liquidation Compulsory closing of positions when margin requirements are not met
Risk Warning Alerts issued when abnormal trading patterns are detected

8.5 Forced Liquidation

  • If a member’s margin falls below the required level and is not replenished by the next trading day’s opening, SHFE may forcibly close positions
  • Positions may also be forcibly liquidated if position limits are exceeded
  • The exchange can invoke emergency measures including trading suspension during extreme conditions

9. Regulatory Framework

9.1 Supervising Authority

Regulator Role
China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) Primary regulator; oversees all futures and derivatives exchanges in China; approves new product listings

9.2 Key Regulations

  • Regulations on the Administration of Futures Trading (State Council): Primary national regulation governing futures markets in China
  • Futures and Derivatives Law (2022): China’s first comprehensive law on futures and derivatives, effective August 1, 2022; provides legal foundation for market development, investor protection, and cross-border activities
  • SHFE Trading Rules: Exchange-level rules governing trading conduct, order management, price limits, and market operations
  • SHFE Clearing Rules: Rules governing margin, settlement, delivery, and default management
  • SHFE Risk Management Rules: Rules on margins, price limits, position limits, large trader reporting, and forced liquidation
  • INE Business Rules: Separate but aligned rules for Shanghai International Energy Exchange operations and international participant access

9.3 Market Surveillance

  • SHFE conducts real-time market surveillance monitoring for abnormal trading patterns, market manipulation, and insider trading
  • Coordinates with CSRC on enforcement actions
  • Reports large positions and unusual trading activity
  • Daily opening position limits enforced for certain products to control speculative activity

9.4 Internationalization via INE

The Shanghai International Energy Exchange (INE), established in 2013 as a subsidiary of SHFE, serves as the platform for internationalized commodity products:

Feature Description
Purpose Provide a regulated venue for overseas investors to trade Chinese commodity futures
Currency All contracts denominated and settled in RMB
Access Channels Direct overseas participation through overseas intermediaries, or via QFII/RQFII channels
Products Open to Foreign Traders Crude Oil (SC), Low Sulfur Fuel Oil (LU), TSR 20 Rubber (NR), Bonded Copper (BC), Containerized Freight Index (SCFIS)
Bonded Delivery Physical delivery through bonded warehouses in Shanghai Free Trade Zone and other designated zones
Significance Crude oil futures (SC, launched March 2018) was the first commodity futures in mainland China open to overseas investors

9.5 Self-Regulatory Organization

  • SHFE functions as a self-regulatory organization (SRO) under CSRC supervision
  • Operates 224 delivery warehouses across China
  • Member firms include futures commission merchants (FCMs) and non-futures-company members
  • SHFE sets and enforces its own trading rules, clearing rules, and membership requirements

Margin Framework

Margin Methodology

SHFE uses a fixed-percentage margin system (not SPAN or VaR). Trading margins are calculated as a percentage of contract value, with the percentage varying by product, delivery month proximity, position size, and market conditions.

Margin Calculation

  • Initial Margin = Contract Price x Contract Size x Margin Rate
  • Example: Copper at CNY 70,000/ton, 5 tons/lot, 8% margin = CNY 28,000 per lot

Margin Escalation Rules

Condition Effect
Approaching Delivery Month Margin rate increases in stepped increments as the contract nears the delivery month
High Open Interest When total open interest exceeds specified thresholds, margin rate increases
Consecutive Limit Moves If a contract settles at its price limit for consecutive days, margin increases
Holiday Periods SHFE routinely raises margins before long holidays (Spring Festival, National Day, Labor Day)
Exchange Discretion SHFE can adjust margins at any time; in January 2026, margins for copper raised from 7% to 8% (general) and 11% (speculative) amid precious metals surge

Variation Margin

  • Settled daily (mark-to-market) based on the daily settlement price
  • Gains credited and losses debited to the member’s margin account daily
  • If margin falls below the maintenance level, a margin call is issued; failure to replenish by the next trading day’s opening may result in forced liquidation

Delivery Margin

  • During the delivery month, margin requirements increase significantly
  • Both buyers and sellers must deposit delivery margins
  • Physical delivery through SHFE-designated warehouses (224 warehouses across China)

INE Margin Specifics

  • INE products (crude oil, bonded copper, etc.) follow the same fixed-percentage methodology
  • Overseas participants can use foreign currency (USD) as margin collateral, subject to haircuts [unverified — specific haircut rates]
  • INE margin requirements are generally higher than domestic SHFE products due to the international nature of the contracts
