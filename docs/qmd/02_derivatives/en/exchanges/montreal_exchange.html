1. Overview & Profile

Item Details
Official Name Bourse de Montréal Inc. (Montreal Exchange)
Abbreviation MX
Website https://www.m-x.ca
Year Established 1832 (informal); 1874 (formal incorporation); 1999 (derivatives specialization)
Country / City Canada / Montreal, Quebec (Tour de la Bourse, 800 Victoria Square)
Ownership Structure Wholly-owned subsidiary of TMX Group Limited (TSX: X)
Parent Company / Group TMX Group Limited
Regulatory Authority Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF, Quebec) as lead regulator; Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) for market integrity oversight
President & CEO Luc Fortin (since August 2016)
Clearing House Canadian Derivatives Clearing Corporation (CDCC), wholly-owned subsidiary of MX
Average Daily Volume (2025) ~937,000 contracts/day combined (~520K IRD + ~417K EQD); record year with ~20% YoY growth in both segments
Key Product Areas Interest rate derivatives (CORRA futures, Government of Canada bond futures), equity index derivatives, equity options, ETF options

[Source: Wikipedia, “Montreal Exchange”; TMX Group website; MX website (m-x.ca); MX Q4 2025 Highlights report]

Key Historical Milestones

  • 1832: Informal stock exchange established at Exchange Coffee House in Montreal — one of Canada’s earliest financial markets
  • 1874: Formally incorporated as the Montreal Stock Exchange by Lorn MacDougall and partners
  • 1903: Purpose-built exchange building opened, designed by George B. Post (architect of the NYSE building)
  • 1926: Montreal Curb Market created for speculative and junior stocks
  • 1953: Curb Market renamed to Canadian Stock Exchange
  • 1965: Moved into Tour de la Bourse (Stock Exchange Tower), Montreal’s third-tallest building
  • 1974: Canadian Stock Exchange merged with Montreal Stock Exchange
  • 1975: First exchange in Canada to list stock options
  • 1982: Renamed from “Montreal Stock Exchange” to “Montreal Exchange” to reflect expanded financial instruments
  • 1989 (September): Launched Ten-Year Government of Canada Bond Futures (CGB)
  • 1999: Following restructuring agreement among Canadian exchanges, MX became Canada’s sole derivatives exchange; equity trading transferred to Toronto Stock Exchange
  • 2001: Completed migration from open outcry to fully automated electronic trading — first traditional exchange in North America to achieve this
  • 2004 (February): Became sole provider of electronic trading systems for Boston Options Exchange (BOX); held 31.4% stake, increasing to over 51% by 2009
  • 2006 (July): Established Montreal Climate Exchange (joint venture with Chicago Climate Exchange) — first regulated environmental market in Canada
  • 2007 (December): TSX Group announced acquisition of MX for C$1.31 billion
  • 2008 (May 1): Acquisition completed; combined entity renamed TMX Group Inc.
  • 2013: Upgraded SOLA trading platform with ~300% improvement in trading response time and doubled messaging capacity to 100,000 quotes/second
  • 2020: Launched Three-Month CORRA Futures (CRA) market-making program
  • 2023 (January): Launched One-Month CORRA Futures (COA)
  • 2024 (April 26): Open BAX positions converted to CRA contracts on a 1:1 basis following CDOR cessation
  • 2024 (June 28): CDOR permanently ceased publication; BAX contracts formally discontinued
  • 2025: Record year across both interest rate and equity derivatives; ETF options ADV +80% YoY
  • 2025 (June): Announced FTSE Canada Bank Credit Index Futures (CBC) — first-of-its-kind credit derivatives product for Canadian market, planned Q1 2026 launch

[Source: Wikipedia, “Montreal Exchange”; MX website; TMX press releases 2007–2025; Finextra, “Montreal Exchange upgrades Sola derivatives trading platform”, 2013; MX Q4 2025 Highlights; Mondovisione, “Montréal Exchange To Launch FTSE Canada Bank Credit Index Futures”, June 2025]

2. Market Structure

2.1 Market Segments / Asset Classes

MX is Canada’s sole exchange for listed financial derivatives, organized by underlying asset class:

Asset Class Products Key Contracts
Interest Rate — Short-Term CORRA-based futures and options Three-Month CORRA Futures (CRA), One-Month CORRA Futures (COA), Options on Three-Month CORRA Futures (OCR)
Interest Rate — Government Bonds Government of Canada bond futures and options Two-Year (CGZ), Five-Year (CGF), Ten-Year (CGB), Thirty-Year (LGB) bond futures; Options on CGB (OGB)
Equity Index Index futures and options S&P/TSX 60 Index Standard Futures (SXF), S&P/TSX 60 Index Mini Futures (SXM), S&P/TSX Composite Index Mini Futures (SCF), S&P/TSX 60 Index Options (SXO)
Equity Options Options on individual Canadian equities American-style options; ~300+ optionable classes; penny-increment pricing for eligible classes
ETF Options Options on Canadian-listed ETFs Options on major ETFs (e.g., XIU, ZEB, HXT); fastest-growing segment (+80% ADV in 2025)
Share Futures Single-stock futures Futures on select Canadian equities
Currency FX derivatives Canadian dollar-denominated currency products
Credit (planned) Credit index futures FTSE Canada Bank Credit Index Futures (CBC) — expected Q1 2026

[Source: MX website, “Equity Derivatives”, “Interest Rate Derivatives”; MX Q4 2025 Highlights; Yahoo Finance, “Montreal Exchange to Launch FTSE Canada Bank Credit Index Futures”]

2.3 Participant Structure

Participant Type Description
Approved Participants Firms authorized by MX to trade on the exchange; must be CIRO member firms; subject to MX rules and Regulatory Division oversight
Market Makers Designated firms with quoting obligations for assigned products; selected by the Bourse based on ability to meet quoting requirements and past performance
Clients End-users trading through approved participants

Market Making Programs: - MX operates multiple market-making incentive programs across equity options, ETF options, index derivatives, and interest rate derivatives - Market makers have basic obligations including minimum rate of quote presence at the open and during continuous trading - Market makers who are clients of approved participants are subject to MX self-regulatory jurisdiction - Selection criteria include quoting ability, past performance, and contribution to market activity

[Source: MX website, “Market Making Programs”; MX website, “Accessing the Montréal Exchange”; MX Regulatory Division, “Approved Participants”]

3. Trading Mechanism

3.1 Order Types

MX supports a variety of order types on the SOLA trading platform:

Order Type Description
Limit Order Order with a specified maximum (buy) or minimum (sell) price
Market Order Executes at the best available price for the total quantity available
Stop Order Triggered when the market reaches a specified price level
Stop Limit Order Stop order that converts to a limit order once triggered
Iceberg (Hidden Quantity) Displays pre-determined portions of a larger order sequentially; minimum display: 25 contracts (50 for CGB)

Time-in-Force Instructions:

Qualifier Description
Day Valid for the trading day
GTC (Good Till Cancelled) Remains active until executed or cancelled
IOC (Immediate or Cancel) Executes immediately (full or partial); unfilled portion cancelled
FOK (Fill or Kill) Must be filled entirely and immediately, or cancelled entirely

[Source: MX SOLA Overview; Trading Technologies, “MX Overview”; MX Equity Options Reference Manual]

3.2 Order Matching / Priority Rules

  • Price-Time Priority: Orders are matched first by best price, then by time of entry (FIFO) at the same price level
  • Pro-Rata Allocation: Certain products may use pro-rata matching for market maker quotes [unverified]
  • Continuous Trading: Fully electronic order-driven Central Limit Order Book (CLOB)

3.3 Special Transaction Types

MX supports off-exchange trading facilities:

Transaction Type Description
Cross Trades Pre-arranged trades between two orders from the same firm or between related firms; must be entered at or within the prevailing bid-ask spread
Pre-Arranged Trades Transactions negotiated between two parties before entry into the trading system
Block Trades Large-size negotiated transactions executed at an agreed price outside the CLOB; subject to minimum volume thresholds

[Source: MX website, “Cross, Prearranged and Block Trades Transactions”]

5. Trading System & Technology

5.1 SOLA Trading Platform

SOLA is MX’s proprietary, low-latency electronic trading engine developed in-house. It is also licensed to external venues, including the Boston Options Exchange (BOX).

Item Details
Platform Name SOLA
Developer Bourse de Montréal Inc. (in-house)
Architecture Centralized matching engine
Median Response Time ~460 microseconds (at network edge, post-2013 upgrade)
Messaging Capacity 100,000+ quotes per second
Co-location TMX Data Centre, Markham, Ontario
Physical Connectivity 1 Gbps / 10 Gbps Ethernet

5.2 Connectivity Protocols

Protocol Use Case Description
SAIL (SOLA Access Information Language) Order entry (native) Native protocol for SOLA; supports all functionalities for order entry brokers, HFT firms, and designated market makers
FIX Protocol Order entry (standard) Industry-standard connectivity for order entry brokers
HSVF (High Speed Vendor Feed) Market data Legacy ASCII-format market data feed
OBF (Order Book Feed) Market data Binary-format order book data feed

5.3 Post-Trade Systems

System Description

Trade Manager FIX API FIX-based API for post-trade management
PAR Reports (Participant Activity Reports) Detailed activity reports for participants
TMX Connect Web-based post-trade portal
Drop Copy Services ATR (Automated Trade Report), SAIL Drop Copy, FIX Drop Copy

5.4 Risk Management Tools

MX provides real-time risk management tools including pre-trade risk controls and kill-switch functionality accessible through the SOLA platform.

[Source: MX website, “SOLA Overview”, “Market Access”, “Connection & Testing”, “Risk Management Tools”; Finextra, “Montreal Exchange upgrades Sola derivatives trading platform”, September 2013; PR Newswire, September 2013]

6. Listing & Membership

(Lighter treatment — Tier 3)

6.1 Approved Participant Requirements

To become an approved participant on MX: - Must be a CIRO member firm - Must demonstrate sufficient expertise, technology infrastructure, and firm capital - Subject to ongoing compliance with MX rules and Regulatory Division oversight - Must comply with the Derivatives Act (Quebec, CQLR, Chapter I-14.01)

6.2 Access Models

  • Direct Market Access (DMA): Approved participants may offer clients DMA through the SOLA platform
  • Co-location: Available at TMX Data Centre in Markham, Ontario for latency-sensitive strategies

[Source: MX website, “Accessing the Montréal Exchange”; MX Regulatory Division]

7. Fees & Data

(Lighter treatment — Tier 3)

MX publishes a comprehensive fee schedule covering: - Transaction fees per contract (varies by product and participant category) - Market-making incentive fee rebates - Connectivity and co-location fees - Market data subscription fees (real-time and delayed)

The most recent fee circular (effective January 2023) is published at: https://www.m-x.ca/f_circulaires_en/165-22_annx_en.pdf

[Source: MX Fee Circular 165-22]

8. Risk Controls & Market Integrity

8.1 Exchange-Level Controls

Control Description
Order Price Filters Static validation of incoming futures and options-on-futures orders based on price; range set as a differential (±) from the previous day’s settlement price
Maximum Volume Thresholds Incoming orders validated against maximum volume limits per order
Circuit Breakers (Equity Derivatives) Automatic trading halt triggered when circuit breakers are activated on the underlying equities/indices on TSX
Early Session Price Limits Index futures (e.g., SXF) restricted to ±7% from previous day’s settlement during the early session (6:00–9:15 AM ET)
Kill Switch Pre-trade risk tool allowing firms to cancel all orders and prevent new order entry
Iceberg Minimum Display 25 contracts minimum display for most futures; 50 contracts for CGB

8.2 Circuit Breaker Coordination

MX derivatives circuit breakers are coordinated with the underlying equity market: - Trading halts in equity options are invoked when the underlying stock or ETF is halted on TSX (Single Stock Circuit Breaker / SSCB) - Index derivatives follow equity market-wide circuit breaker levels set by CIRO/exchanges - After a halt, re-opening follows an auction-like process

8.3 Clearing-Level Risk Controls (CDCC)

See Section 11 (Margin Framework) for detailed CDCC risk management methodology.

[Source: MX website, “Exchange Market Thresholds and Controls”; MX Share Futures specifications; MX Equity Options Reference Manual]

9. Regulatory Framework

9.1 Regulatory Structure

MX operates within a multi-layered Canadian regulatory framework:

Regulator / Body Role
Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) Lead regulator; recognized exchange and self-regulatory organization (SRO) status granted by AMF under Quebec’s Derivatives Act (CQLR, Chapter I-14.01)
MX Regulatory Division (MX-R) Independent unit within MX; performs surveillance, compliance, and enforcement for MX derivative markets
Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) National SRO formed January 2023 (from merger of IIROC and MFDA); oversees market integrity; performs cross-asset surveillance between MX-listed derivatives and underlying securities
Bank of Canada Oversees systemic risk; supervises CDCC as a designated systemically important financial market infrastructure
Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA) Umbrella organization of provincial/territorial regulators; harmonizes regulation across Canada

9.2 Self-Regulatory Functions

As a recognized SRO, MX is required by the AMF to maintain the Regulatory Division (MX-R), which: - Monitors trading activity in real time - Investigates potential market abuses (manipulation, insider trading, front-running) - Enforces MX rules against approved participants and market makers - Conducts compliance examinations - Publishes regulatory notices and circulars

9.3 Rule-Making Process

  • MX rule amendments are self-certified under the self-certification process established in the Derivatives Act
  • Significant rule changes are subject to AMF review and public comment
  • CIRO’s Universal Market Integrity Rules (UMIR) apply to cross-market trading activity

9.4 Key Regulatory Developments

  • 2023: CIRO formed from IIROC-MFDA merger (effective January 1, 2023; renamed CIRO on June 1, 2023)
  • 2024: T+1 settlement implemented across Canadian markets (May 27, 2024)
  • 2024: CDOR cessation and BAX-to-CRA transition completed under ISDA framework

[Source: AMF website, “Self-Regulatory Organizations”; MX Regulatory Division website (reg.m-x.ca); CIRO website, “About CIRO”; MX “Accessing the Montréal Exchange” document]

10. Recent Developments & Outlook

(Lighter treatment — Tier 3)

  • 2025 Record Volumes: Both interest rate derivatives (~520K ADV, +20% YoY) and equity derivatives (~417K ADV, +20% YoY) reached record levels
  • ETF Options Boom: Fastest-growing segment at 186K ADV (+80% YoY) in 2025
  • CORRA Transition Complete: Smooth conversion from BAX (CDOR-based) to CRA (CORRA-based) products; CRA volumes at 170K ADV (+33% YoY)
  • Credit Derivatives Launch: FTSE Canada Bank Credit Index Futures (CBC) announced June 2025 for Q1 2026 launch — first credit index derivative in Canada
  • CDCC Margin Enhancements: Ongoing refinement of SPAN-based margin calculations; margin file corrections for CORRA products as of March 2026

[Source: MX Q4 2025 Highlights; Mondovisione, June 2025; CDCC notices, March 2026]

12. Margin Framework (CDCC)

12.1 Overview

The Canadian Derivatives Clearing Corporation (CDCC) is the central counterparty (CCP) for all exchange-traded derivatives on MX and for a growing range of OTC customized financial instruments.

Item Details
Full Name Canadian Derivatives Clearing Corporation
Ownership Wholly-owned subsidiary of Bourse de Montréal Inc.
Headquarters 100 Adelaide Street West, 3rd Floor, Toronto, Ontario
Designation Recognized as a clearing house by AMF; designated as a systemically important financial market infrastructure by the Bank of Canada
Margin Methodology SPAN-based (Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk), adopted 1997

12.2 Margin Methodology

Initial Margin (SPAN-Based): - CDCC adopted SPAN in 1997 as the core framework for calculating initial margin requirements - Risk parameters are calibrated on a daily basis, including Margin Interval and Intra-Commodity Spreads - Incorporates historical volatility of daily price returns - Volatility estimator uses a confidence level exceeding 99% - Variable Margin Period of Risk (MPOR) applied depending on product liquidity

Anti-Procyclicality Measures: - Stress Risk component calculated via Stress Value at Risk (SVaR) with a 25% weighting factor - Volatility floor calculated as the average of the daily volatility estimator observed over the last 10 years - These measures prevent margin requirements from dropping too low during periods of calm and spiking excessively during stress

Margin Relief (Inter-Commodity Spreads): - Currently available for a limited list of futures products exhibiting high correlation: - Index Futures combinations - Short-Term Interest Rate (STIR) Futures combinations - Canadian clients may use the CDCC SPAN file as an alternative to other margin files

Additional Capital Margin: - Measures credit exposure of clearing members - Requires collection of a minimum of 50% of the excess when a clearing member’s Base Initial Margin exceeds their capital amount

12.3 Variation Margin

  • Mark-to-market settlement performed daily
  • Futures: daily settlement price used for P&L calculation; variation margin collected/paid daily
  • Options: premium-style margining for equity and ETF options

12.4 Default Management

CDCC maintains a default waterfall including: 1. Defaulting member’s margin and collateral 2. Defaulting member’s contribution to the clearing fund 3. CDCC’s own dedicated resources (skin-in-the-game) 4. Non-defaulting members’ clearing fund contributions 5. Additional assessments

[Source: CDCC website, “Margining”, “Risk Management”; CDCC Operations Manual (August 2025); OSC, “Canadian Derivatives Clearing Corporation” RFC document, February 2023; CDCC Notice 154-22; Bank of Canada, “Stress testing central counterparties for resolution planning”, March 2025]
