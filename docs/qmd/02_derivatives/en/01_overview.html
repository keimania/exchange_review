1. Introduction

This document provides a high-level overview of global derivatives markets (futures, options, and other listed derivatives), their structure, key players, and trends. It serves as the capstone summary for the derivatives domain of the Global Exchange Research project, synthesizing findings from 17 individual exchange research files.

Related Resources: - Individual exchange files: exchanges/ - System deep-dives: systems/ (Globex, Genium INET, T7) - Regulatory analysis: regulations/ (U.S., EU, Asia)

3. Market Structure

3.1 Exchange-Traded vs OTC

The global derivatives market spans both exchange-traded derivatives (ETD) and over-the-counter (OTC) markets:

  • ETD markets reached ~205 billion contracts in 2024, dominated by equity options (~86% of volume)
  • OTC notional outstanding exceeded $700 trillion (end-2024), dominated by interest rate derivatives (~$548T)
  • Post-2008 regulatory mandates (Dodd-Frank, EMIR) have pushed standardized OTC derivatives toward central clearing, blurring the line between exchange-traded and OTC markets
  • Major CCPs now clear both ETD and OTC products (e.g., CME Clearing, Eurex Clearing, ASX Clear Futures, LCH)

3.2 Trading Mechanisms

Mechanism Description Examples
Electronic order book (CLOB) Continuous matching via price-time priority; dominant mechanism globally CME Globex, Eurex T7, Optiq, SOLA, ASX 24, HKATS, J-GATE, Titan, Cboe Titanium
Request for Quote (RFQ) Electronic negotiation for less liquid instruments; market makers provide competitive quotes Eurex EnLight, ICE, HKEX, Cboe AIM
Block trading / negotiated trades Off-book large-lot transactions at pre-agreed prices, reported to exchange for clearing All major exchanges (e.g., CME EFP/EFS/EOO, Eurex TES, OSE J-NET, SGX NLT, ASX Custom Market)
Trade at Marker (TAM) Execution at a published daily marker price DME/GME (Oman Marker Price)

3.3 Market Participants

  • Hedgers: Commercial entities managing commodity, interest rate, FX, and equity risk
  • Speculators: Including retail investors (particularly dominant in India’s options market), hedge funds, and proprietary firms
  • Arbitrageurs: Exploiting price discrepancies across related contracts and venues
  • Proprietary trading firms / HFT: High-speed algorithmic market makers providing liquidity
  • Market makers: Exchange-designated liquidity providers with quoting obligations and incentives

4. Margin & Risk Framework

Global derivatives exchanges employ several margin methodologies, with a clear industry-wide trend from scenario-based (SPAN) to VaR-based approaches:

Methodology Type Key Exchanges/CCPs Status
SPAN (original) Scenario-based (16 scenarios) TAIFEX, CDCC (Montreal), HKCC (PRiME), many smaller exchanges Legacy; being phased out at major exchanges
SPAN 2 HVaR-based (thousands of scenarios) CME Clearing Energy products migrated 2023; broader rollout ongoing
Prisma Filtered Historical Simulation VaR Eurex Clearing In production since 2013; first CCP to apply VaR to financial futures
ICE Risk Model 2 (IRM 2) Filtered Historical Simulation VaR ICE Clear Launched 2024-2025, replacing legacy IRM
Euronext Clearing VaR-based Euronext Clearing (formerly CC&G) Expanded to all Euronext financial derivatives Sept 2024
Fixed-percentage Percentage of contract value CFFEX, DCE, SHFE (China) Chinese exchanges use fixed-percentage margins with regulatory discretion to adjust
SEBI Peak Margin Upfront 100% margin NSE (India) Implemented 2021; additional ELM on expiry day from Nov 2024
KRX-SPAN Scenario-based (modified SPAN) KRX Active; evaluation of VaR-based migration underway

Key Observations: - The industry is converging toward VaR-based methodologies (SPAN 2, Prisma, IRM 2) that use historical simulation with thousands of scenarios, replacing SPAN’s fixed 16-scenario framework - Chinese exchanges (CFFEX, DCE, SHFE) remain on fixed-percentage systems with extensive regulatory discretion to adjust margins during volatile periods or holidays - Cross-margining across products is increasingly available at CME, Eurex, and other multi-asset CCPs - Anti-procyclicality measures (stress VaR floors, volatility buffers) are now standard at major CCPs

Cross-cutting regulatory trends: - Increasing central clearing mandates for OTC derivatives globally - Enhanced position limit regimes and large trader reporting - Retail investor protection measures (particularly India’s SEBI reforms) - Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) in the EU (effective January 2025) - PFMI (Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures) compliance as global standard

6. Key Trends

6.1 Explosion of Options Trading & Retail Participation

  • Global options volume reached 177 billion contracts in 2024 (+63.8% YoY), now ~86% of all ETD volume
  • NSE India alone traded ~92-108 billion contracts in 2024 (mostly equity options), driven by massive retail participation
  • SEBI’s September 2024 study revealed 93% of individual F&O traders incurred losses between FY22-FY24, leading to comprehensive regulatory curbs
  • Post-SEBI curbs (November 2024), NSE volumes declined ~30-38%, and B3 overtook NSE as #1 globally in H1 2025

6.2 0DTE (Zero Days to Expiration) Options Revolution

  • 0DTE options averaged 14 million contracts/day in 2025, up 41% YoY
  • 0DTE accounted for 24.1% of all U.S. listed options volume in 2025 (up from 21.5% in 2024, nearly double the 2022 share)
  • SPX 0DTE contracts became the most traded S&P 500 options, surpassing all other expiration dates combined in Q4 2024
  • Cboe’s daily SPX expirations (launched 2022) were the catalyst; 0DTE reached ~59% of SPX options volume by 2025
  • Both retail (50% of options volume via retail broker flows) and institutional participants are adopting 0DTE strategies

6.3 Micro/Nano Contracts for Retail Access

  • CME Micro E-mini suite (S&P 500, Nasdaq, Gold, Crude) continues to grow, lowering capital requirements for retail
  • TAIFEX launched Micro TAIEX Futures; ASX offers Mini SPI 200
  • DME/GME launched Micro Oman Crude Oil Futures (100 barrels vs. standard 1,000)
  • Euronext introduced mini single stock options (May 2025) and mini ETF options (March 2026)

6.4 ESG & Environmental Derivatives

  • ICE environmental markets saw a record 20.9 million contracts in 2025 (+4% YoY)
  • EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) allowances remain the most liquid environmental derivative (ICE Futures Europe primary venue)
  • ASX lists ACCU (Australian Carbon Credit Unit) Futures, NZU Futures, and LGC Futures
  • Euronext launched CAC 40 ESG index futures
  • Global carbon market exceeded $1 trillion in 2025
  • Renewable energy carbon credit market exceeded USD 43.3 billion in 2024

6.5 Cryptocurrency Derivatives

  • Crypto derivatives market expanded to $8.94 trillion monthly in 2025
  • CME Bitcoin and Ether futures/options continue to grow as regulated venues
  • Average daily volume in Ether options grew 65% from 2024 to 2025
  • Bitcoin and Ethereum together account for ~68% of crypto derivatives volume

6.6 Extended Trading Hours

  • TAIFEX offers 19-hour coverage (day + after-hours T+1 session since 2017)
  • ASX 24 provides near-24-hour trading for interest rate and equity index futures
  • CME Globex offers ~23 hours/day across asset classes
  • SHFE/DCE/CFFEX night sessions extend coverage to overlap with London and New York
  • Euronext extended equity index futures hours to 22:00 CET (covering U.S. session)
  • KRX launched independent night session (18:00-06:00 KST) on June 9, 2025, expanding derivatives trading to ~19 hours/day

6.7 Technology Modernization

  • Margin methodology transition: SPAN to VaR-based (SPAN 2, Prisma, IRM 2) across major CCPs
  • Euronext completed Optiq platform migration for all derivatives (including IDEM, April 2024)
  • Euronext Clearing migration from LCH SA completed for all financial derivatives (September 2024)
  • ASX 24 platform modernization with LSEG announced (March 2026, target go-live H1 2028)
  • NSE planning nanosecond-latency upgrade (100M transactions/second, from April 2026)
  • OSE J-GATE 4.0 planned on Nasdaq Eqlipse platform (~2028)

6.8 China’s Internationalization

  • INE crude oil futures (launched 2018) established RMB-denominated energy benchmark
  • DCE iron ore and palm olein opened to overseas traders
  • SHFE bonded copper and low sulfur fuel oil accessible to foreign participants
  • QFI scheme expanded access across CFFEX, DCE, SHFE products
  • Futures and Derivatives Law (2022) provided comprehensive legal foundation

6.9 Fixed Income Derivatives Growth

  • BOJ rate normalization driving TONA futures growth on OSE
  • Euronext launched mini cash-settled government bond futures (BTP, OAT, Bund, Bono) in September 2025
  • Montreal Exchange CORRA futures (CRA) reached ~170K ADV in 2025 following successful BAX-to-CRA transition
  • ASX interest rate futures hit record volumes in Q4 2025 (+23% YoY)
  • CFFEX 30-year CGB futures launched April 2023, completing China’s yield curve coverage
