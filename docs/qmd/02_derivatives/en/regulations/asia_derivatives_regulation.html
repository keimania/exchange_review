1. Japan

1.1 Regulatory Authorities

Authority Role Scope
JFSA (Financial Services Agency) Primary regulator Securities, banking, insurance, derivatives; reports to Cabinet Office
SESC (Securities and Exchange Surveillance Commission) Enforcement & surveillance Market manipulation, insider trading, inspection of FIBOs
JPX-R (JPX Regulation) Self-regulatory organization Market surveillance, member compliance for JPX-listed markets
Bank of Japan Central bank / systemic oversight Systemic risk oversight of JSCC; monetary policy implications
METI (Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry) Commodity oversight (historical) Commodity derivatives (pre-2012 unification); now largely consolidated under JFSA
  • Japan operates a ministry-level regulatory model: the JFSA is an external bureau of the Cabinet Office, giving it broad administrative authority.
  • The SESC, while organizationally under the JFSA, maintains operational independence for enforcement actions.
  • JPX-R provides front-line surveillance for the Osaka Exchange (OSE) and Tokyo Commodity Exchange (TOCOM) derivatives markets.

Source: JFSA official website; Chambers Derivatives 2025 — Japan

1.2 Key Legislation

Legislation Year Scope
Financial Instruments and Exchange Act (FIEA) 2006 (orig.), multiple amendments Primary law governing securities and derivatives; defines Financial Instruments Business Operators (FIBOs)
Commodity Derivatives Act 2009 (orig.) Governs commodity derivatives; largely merged into FIEA framework post-2012
2012 Comprehensive Reform 2012 Unified regulation of securities derivatives and commodity derivatives under JFSA; previously commodity derivatives were under METI/MAFF
2024 FIEA Amendments 2024 (enacted); effective May 2026 Shareholder disclosure reforms; extends reporting obligations to cash-settled equity derivatives; clarifies “joint holder” definitions

Key FIEA provisions for derivatives: - Registration requirements for Financial Instruments Business Operators (FIBOs) dealing in derivatives - Mandatory clearing of designated OTC derivatives through a licensed clearinghouse - Trade reporting obligations to trade repositories (amended April 2024 to require LEI, UTI, and CDE reporting) - Conduct rules: suitability obligations, risk disclosure, segregation of customer assets - Position limit framework delegated to exchange rules

Source: ICLG Derivatives 2025-2026 — Japan; Funds-Axis — FIEA 2024 Amendments

1.3 Clearing Framework

Central Counterparty: Japan Securities Clearing Corporation (JSCC) — sole CCP for listed and OTC derivatives

Service Products Mandatory?
Listed derivatives clearing All OSE/TOCOM listed futures & options Yes (all exchange-traded)
OTC IRS clearing JPY-denominated interest rate swaps (TONA compounded in arrears) Yes (for designated FIBOs)
OTC CDS clearing iTraxx Japan index CDS Yes (for designated FIBOs)
  • JSCC clears only yen-denominated OTC products (IRS and CDS).
  • Mandatory clearing applies to FIBOs and Registered Financial Institutions meeting threshold criteria, as designated by the JFSA Commissioner under Cabinet Office Orders.
  • 2025 development: The U.S. CFTC issued Staff Letter No. 25-32 (September 12, 2025) and an Amended Order of Exemption, permitting U.S. eligible contract participants to clear JPY-IRS through JSCC clearing members. This represents a significant cross-border milestone.

Source: JSCC — OTC Derivatives Clearing; CFTC Letter No. 25-32; Gibson Dunn — CFTC/JSCC

1.4 Position Limits & Reporting

  • Position limits are set by the Osaka Exchange (OSE) at the exchange rule level, not prescribed by statute.
  • Limits vary by contract and are expressed in terms of number of contracts (net position).
  • Large position reporting is required when positions exceed exchange-prescribed thresholds.
  • Hedge exemptions are available upon application to the exchange.
  • The JFSA retains authority to direct exchanges to modify position limit rules if warranted by systemic concerns.

Source: JPX — Index Futures Specifications (May 2025)

1.5 Trade Reporting

  • April 2024 reform: Amendments to the trade data reporting requirement came into force, requiring:
    • Reporting through licensed trade repositories (rather than directly to the JFSA)
    • Mandatory use of Legal Entity Identifier (LEI)
    • Mandatory use of Unique Transaction Identifier (UTI)
    • Improved Critical Data Elements (CDE) aligned with CPMI-IOSCO standards

Source: ICLG Derivatives 2025-2026 — Japan

2. Hong Kong

2.1 Regulatory Authorities

Authority Role Scope
SFC (Securities and Futures Commission) Primary market regulator Securities, futures, OTC derivatives regulation; independent statutory body
HKMA (Hong Kong Monetary Authority) Banking / monetary regulator OTC derivatives regulation for authorized institutions (banks); co-regulator with SFC
HKEX Exchange and CCP operator Operates the exchange and three clearing houses; also conducts front-line regulation
FSTB (Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau) Policy Sets legislative policy direction
  • Hong Kong uses an independent statutory authority model. The SFC is not a government ministry but an independent body with quasi-judicial powers.
  • For OTC derivatives regulation, the SFC and HKMA share jurisdiction: the SFC oversees licensed corporations, while the HKMA oversees authorized institutions (banks).
  • HKEX performs dual roles as commercial exchange operator and market regulator.

Source: SFC — About the SFC; ICLG Derivatives 2025-2026 — Hong Kong

2.2 Key Legislation

Legislation Year Scope
Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) 2003 Primary law governing securities and futures markets; licensing, market conduct, derivatives regulation
Securities and Futures (OTC Derivative Transactions — Reporting and Record Keeping Obligations) Rules 2015+ (phased) Mandatory reporting of OTC derivatives
Securities and Futures (OTC Derivative Transactions — Clearing and Record Keeping Obligations and Designation of Central Counterparties) Rules 2016+ (phased) Mandatory clearing of designated OTC derivatives
Securities and Futures (Contracts Limits and Reportable Positions) Rules Various Position limits and large open position reporting for exchange-traded derivatives

Source: SFC — OTC Derivatives Regime FAQ

2.3 Clearing Framework

Clearing Houses under HKEX:

Clearing House Scope Products
HKCC (HKFE Clearing Corporation) Listed derivatives All exchange-traded futures and options on HKEX
SEOCH (SEHK Options Clearing House) Listed stock options Stock options on HKEX
OTC Clear (OTC Clearing Hong Kong Limited) OTC derivatives IRS (HKD, USD, EUR, GBP, CNY), cross-currency swaps, NDFs

Mandatory OTC clearing: - Mandatory clearing applies to IRS transactions between “prescribed persons” and “Financial Services Providers” (FSPs — major dealers outside Hong Kong). - Covered currencies: HKD, USD, EUR, GBP fixed-to-floating IRS. - The FSP List is updated annually; the 2025 update was gazetted Q4 2025 for implementation January 1, 2026. - 2026 consultation: In January 2026, HKMA and SFC jointly consulted on standard calculation periods under OTC derivative Clearing Rules, proposing two annual calculation periods (March-May and September-November) effective March 1, 2027.

Margin for non-centrally cleared derivatives: - Margin requirements for non-centrally cleared OTC single-stock options, equity basket options, and equity index options were deferred to January 4, 2026, aligning with UK and EU timelines.

Sources: HKEX — OTC Clear Clearing Services; HKMA — FSP List Update (Apr 2025); HKMA — Clearing Rules Consultation (Jan 2026)

2.4 Position Limits

The SFC prescribes statutory position limits for exchange-traded derivatives:

Key position limits (effective July 2, 2025):

Contract Position Limit (Delta) Change
Hang Seng Index (HSI) futures & options 15,000 +50% increase
Hang Seng China Enterprises Index (HSCEI) futures & options 25,000 +108% increase
Hang Seng TECH Index futures & options 30,000 +43% increase
  • Position limits are expressed in position delta (net delta across futures and options).
  • Reportable position thresholds are set at lower levels; exceeding these triggers large open position (LOP) reporting to the SFC.
  • Hedge exemptions and market-maker exemptions are available upon application.
  • The SFC consulted on these increases in early 2025 and received 25 submissions from local and overseas market participants showing strong support.

Sources: SFC — Position Limit Increases (Jun 2025); SFC — Consultation Paper 25CP2

2.5 Trade Reporting

  • September 29, 2025: Enhanced HKTR (Hong Kong Trade Repository) reporting requirements took effect:
    • Mandatory Unique Transaction Identifier (UTI)
    • Mandatory Unique Product Identifier (UPI)
    • Mandatory Critical Data Elements (CDE) aligned with CPMI-IOSCO standards

Source: EY — Evolving HKTR Requirements (Mar 2025)

3. Singapore

3.1 Regulatory Authorities

Authority Role Scope
MAS (Monetary Authority of Singapore) Integrated regulator + central bank Securities, banking, insurance, derivatives, monetary policy — single unified regulator
SGX RegCo Self-regulatory organization Front-line regulation of SGX-listed markets; subsidiary of SGX Group
  • Singapore uses a fully integrated regulatory model: MAS is both the central bank and the financial regulator.
  • SGX RegCo was established as a separate subsidiary in 2017 to strengthen the independence of regulatory functions from SGX’s commercial operations.

Source: MAS — About MAS; SGX RegCo

3.2 Key Legislation

Legislation Year Scope
Securities and Futures Act (SFA) 2001 (current version) Primary law governing securities, futures, and derivatives markets; licensing, market conduct, clearing
Securities and Futures (Reporting of Derivatives Contracts) Regulations 2013 2013 (amended 2024) Mandatory reporting of OTC derivatives contracts
Securities and Futures (Licensing and Conduct of Business) Regulations Various Licensing requirements for capital markets services

Source: MAS — Securities and Futures Act 2001; Singapore Statutes Online — SFA

3.3 Clearing Framework

Central Counterparty: SGX Derivatives Clearing Limited (SGX-DC)

Service Products Notes
Listed derivatives clearing All SGX-listed futures & options Equity index, FX, commodity, interest rate derivatives
OTC IRS clearing SGD and USD IRS Mandatory for entities exceeding S$20 billion threshold
OTC FX clearing NDFs, FX options Voluntary clearing available

Mandatory clearing scope: - MAS mandated clearing for certain IRS contracts (originally SGD fixed-to-floating SOR IRS and USD fixed-to-floating LIBOR IRS; transitioned to SORA-based benchmarks). - Applies to banks and other specified persons exceeding the S$20 billion gross notional outstanding threshold (measured over four consecutive calendar quarters for derivatives booked in Singapore). - Banks below the threshold and non-bank entities below the threshold are exempt.

SGX-DC is CFTC-registered as a Derivatives Clearing Organization (DCO), enabling U.S. customers to access SGX-cleared products.

Sources: MAS — Consultation Paper on Mandatory Clearing; CFTC — SGX-DC Registration

3.4 Position Limits

  • Position limits are set by SGX at the exchange rule level.
  • SGX uses a dual framework:
    • Position limits: Hard caps on net positions for speculative accounts.
    • Accountability levels: Soft thresholds that trigger exchange inquiry but do not automatically prohibit additional positions.
  • Large position reporting is required when positions exceed prescribed thresholds.
  • Hedge exemptions are available upon application to SGX.

Source: SGX-DC Clearing Rules

3.5 Trade Reporting

October 21, 2024 reform (MAS Rewrite): - Major overhaul of OTC derivatives reporting requirements, harmonizing with CPMI-IOSCO standards: - Adoption of ISO 20022 messaging standard - Mandatory reporting to licensed trade repositories within two business days - Covers: interest rate, credit, FX, commodity, and equity derivatives contracts booked or traded in Singapore - Re-reporting requirement: All outstanding specified derivatives contracts with remaining maturity of 6+ months as of October 21, 2024, were required to be re-reported by April 21, 2025.

Sources: MAS — Reporting Guidelines (May 2024); Rajah & Tann — OTC Reporting Changes (Oct 2024)

4. Comparative Analysis

4.1 Regulatory Structure Comparison

Dimension Japan Hong Kong Singapore Korea
Primary regulator JFSA (Financial Services Agency) SFC (Securities and Futures Commission) MAS (Monetary Authority of Singapore) FSC (Financial Services Commission)
Regulator type External bureau of Cabinet Office (ministry-level) Independent statutory body Integrated regulator + central bank Government commission under Prime Minister’s Office
Supervisory arm SESC (enforcement) SFC Enforcement Division MAS (integrated) FSS (Financial Supervisory Service)
SRO JPX-R HKEX (dual role) SGX RegCo KRX (dual role: exchange + SRO)
Central bank role BOJ — systemic oversight of JSCC HKMA — co-regulator for OTC derivatives (banks) MAS is the central bank BOK — limited direct role in derivatives regulation
Dual regulator? No (JFSA unified post-2012) Yes (SFC + HKMA for OTC derivatives) No (MAS is fully integrated) Yes (FSC policy + FSS supervision)

Key distinctions: - Singapore stands out as the only fully integrated model where the central bank is also the financial regulator. - Korea has a unique dual structure: the FSC sets policy while the FSS (a separate organization) executes supervision and examination. This differs from Hong Kong’s SFC/HKMA split, which is based on entity type (licensed corporations vs. banks), not function. - Japan completed its unification in 2012, bringing commodity derivatives under JFSA from METI/MAFF.

4.3 Clearing Mandate Comparison

Dimension Japan Hong Kong Singapore Korea
CCP for listed derivatives JSCC HKCC, SEOCH SGX-DC KRX (vertically integrated)
CCP for OTC derivatives JSCC OTC Clear SGX-DC KRX (limited OTC clearing)

Mandatory OTC clearing — CDS Yes (iTraxx Japan) No mandatory CDS clearing No mandatory CDS clearing No mandatory CDS clearing
Clearing threshold Applies to designated FIBOs Transactions between prescribed persons and FSPs S$20 billion gross notional threshold [varies by regulation]

Notable patterns: - All four jurisdictions have implemented mandatory OTC IRS clearing, with Hong Kong covering the broadest range of currencies. - Japan is unique in mandating CDS clearing (iTraxx Japan). - Singapore applies a quantitative threshold (S$20 billion) to determine clearing obligations. - All four jurisdictions have recently upgraded trade reporting to align with CPMI-IOSCO CDE standards (2024-2025 implementation wave).

  • All four jurisdictions are IOSCO MMOU and APRC Supervisory MMoU signatories, facilitating cross-border enforcement and supervisory cooperation.
  • Cross-border CCP recognition is well-established: JSCC, OTC Clear, and SGX-DC all have CFTC exemption/registration status, and ESMA equivalence assessments.
  • Korea’s KRX had a cooperation arrangement with Eurex for KOSPI 200 products, though Eurex cancelled admission to trading for its KOSPI derivatives [unverified — specific timeline].

4.5 Key Differences from Korean Framework

Korea’s derivatives regulatory framework has several distinctive features compared to the other three jurisdictions:

4.5.1 FSC/FSS Dual Structure

  • Korea’s FSC (policy-making) and FSS (supervision/examination) split is functionally different from Hong Kong’s SFC/HKMA split. Hong Kong divides by entity type (licensed corporations vs. banks); Korea divides by function (policy vs. execution).
  • The FSS is not a government body but a special statutory corporation, which creates a unique institutional dynamic.
  • In Japan and Singapore, these functions are consolidated in a single body (JFSA and MAS, respectively).

4.5.2 KRX as Vertically Integrated Exchange + CCP

  • KRX operates as both exchange and CCP (vertical silo model), similar to HKEX’s structure.
  • This contrasts with Japan, where JPX (exchange holding) and JSCC (CCP) are separate entities (though JSCC is a JPX subsidiary).
  • SGX also uses a vertical silo model (SGX-DC is a subsidiary), but MAS has maintained regulatory separation through SGX RegCo.
  • The vertical integration debate is particularly relevant for Korea given KRX’s monopoly position as the sole exchange and CCP for Korean derivatives.

4.5.3 KOSPI 200 Derivatives Position Limits

  • Korea has historically applied position limits differentiated by investor type (individual, institutional, foreign), with particularly strict limits for individual investors on KOSPI 200 options following the 2011 options multiplier increase (from 100,000 to 500,000 KRW).
  • This investor-type-based approach is more granular than the approach in Japan, Hong Kong, or Singapore, where limits are generally the same regardless of investor type (though market-maker exemptions apply everywhere).
  • March 2026: KRX expanded KOSPI 200 options strike price ranges from +/-80 to +/-120 points (first through third nearby months), and similarly for Mini KOSPI 200 options.

4.5.4 Foreign Investor Access

  • Korea requires foreign investors to obtain a unique Investor Registration Certificate (IRC) from the FSS before trading derivatives.
  • This contrasts with:
    • Japan: Foreign investors access through licensed FIBOs; no separate investor registration
    • Hong Kong: Access through licensed intermediaries; no separate investor registration
    • Singapore: Access through licensed capital markets services holders; no separate investor registration
  • Korea’s IRC system enables account-level tracking of foreign positions but adds an administrative layer not present in the other three markets.

5. Summary & Outlook

5.1 Convergence Trends

All four jurisdictions are converging on several fronts: 1. Trade reporting harmonization: All adopted CPMI-IOSCO CDE, UTI, and UPI standards during 2024-2025. 2. OTC clearing mandates: All have mandatory IRS clearing, though scope and thresholds vary. 3. IOSCO participation: All are full MMOU and APRC Supervisory MMoU signatories. 4. Cross-border CCP recognition: Mutual recognition frameworks are well-established with major Western counterparts (CFTC, ESMA).

5.2 Divergence Points

  1. Regulatory architecture: Ranges from fully integrated (Singapore/MAS) to split-function (Korea/FSC+FSS) to split-by-entity (Hong Kong/SFC+HKMA).
  2. Position limit authority: Statutory (Hong Kong) vs. exchange-level (Japan, Singapore, Korea).
  3. Foreign investor access: Korea’s IRC system remains more restrictive than the other three.
  4. Exchange structure: KRX’s monopoly quasi-public model differs from the publicly listed exchange groups in the other three jurisdictions.

5.3 Key Watchpoints (2026-2027)

  • Japan: FIEA 2024 amendments taking effect May 2026; continued TONA futures market development
  • Hong Kong: Southbound Swap Connect development; Clearing Rules calculation period amendments (proposed effective March 2027); ODP platform rollout
  • Singapore: Continued expansion of FX futures product suite; potential updates to mandatory clearing thresholds
  • Korea: Potential KRX IPO implications for regulatory structure; ongoing derivatives market reforms; foreign investor access modernization
